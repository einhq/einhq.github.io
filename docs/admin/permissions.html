Administration

Permissions in depth

Every node in EiN has an owner, a group, mode bits and, when it needs one, an access list. This page is for administrators, and for anyone who wants to see what the Share dialog writes: how EiN decides who may read, change and pass through a node, what a new node starts with, and how to read and change it all from the terminal.

The Share dialog sets all of this without numbers, and Sharing explains it for everyone in the family. Accounts and groups themselves are made on Accounts and groups.

The four parts of a node's access

Like a file on Linux, every node carries:

Part What it is Shown as
Owner One account, by its uid. Usually whoever created the node. helen
Group One group, by its gid. Usually the group of the folder it was created in. users
Mode Read, write and execute, once for the owner, once for the group and once for everyone else. rwxr-x---, or 0750
Access list Further users and groups, each with its own read, write and execute. Optional. group:family:rwx

What the three letters allow on a node:

Bit On a node On a folder, album or anything with children
r read See its bytes and attributes. See what it holds; listing takes r and x.
w write Change it, its attributes, its mode and its access list. Add to it and remove from it (with x).
x execute — Pass through it to what lies below.

Any node can hold children here, so the folder column applies to albums, people, locations and files alike: a photo holds its thumbnails and metadata as children.

How EiN decides

EiN asks, in this order, and stops at the first yes:

  1. Are you an administrator? Then yes, always.
  2. Is this a write, and are you not signed in? Then no, whatever the bits say.
  3. Are you the owner, and do the owner's bits allow it?
  4. Is the node's group one of your groups, and do the group's bits allow it?
  5. Do everyone else's bits allow it?
  6. Does an entry in the access list name you or one of your groups, and allow it?

Every step can only grant. Unlike Linux, being the owner does not shut you out of what the other bits give: a node at 0074 still lets its owner read it. The access list adds people and never takes anyone away; there is no entry that denies.

Note

Write on a node is enough to change its mode and its access list. That is why Can edit in the Share dialog also lets someone change who the album is shared with. Changing the owner is the exception; see Change the owner and the group.

Who you are to EiN

Who uid Groups What is special
The administrator, admin 0 admin (0), users Passes every check.
An account, such as helen its own its own group, users, and every group it is a member of —
A member of sudo who chose Administer… its own as above Passes every check until the sign-in ends or they leave admin mode.
The guest, guest 1001 guest (1001) Never writes. Whoever opens EiN without signing in.
A share link one of its own, from 1073741824 up none Never writes; its entries stop granting when the link expires.

A few rules follow from how groups are read:

  • Every signed-in person is in users (gid 1002). Sharing with users, which the dialog calls Everyone with an account, shares with all of them.
  • A member is someone with a membership link. group-add-member family jon puts a link named jon to /etc/users/jon in /etc/groups/family/users. Anything else placed there makes nobody a member.
  • Groups are read when someone signs in. A person added to or taken out of a group keeps the groups they signed in with until they sign in again. Taking someone out of sudo is the exception: it ends their admin mode at once.
  • Being in the admin group makes nobody an administrator. It is the administrator's own group, as root's is on Linux, and gives no write to the system's folders. To let someone act as the administrator when they need to, add them to sudo; see Accounts and groups.

To see who you are:

# in EiN's terminal
user-whoami
user-groups helen

See a node's permissions

In the web interface

  1. Open Nodes under System in the sidebar.
  2. Go to the node. The Mode, Owner and Group columns show its access; a lock in place of the arrow means you may not list what it holds.
  3. Choose Properties in the node's context menu, or press Alt+Enter.

The properties dialog has three tabs:

  • General shows the node's Mode as a number and as a grid of Owner, Group and Other against r, w and x, with Owner and Group pickers. Apply saves them, and stays off when you may not change the node.
  • Attributes lists the node's attributes.
  • Sharing is the Share dialog's own body: owner, grants, visitors and links in words.

In the terminal

# in EiN's terminal
ls -l '/archive/albums/Christmas at the cottage'     # mode, owner and group of what it holds
stat '/archive/albums/Christmas at the cottage'      # the node itself: type, mode, owner, times
acl '/archive/albums/Christmas at the cottage'       # owner, group, bits and access list
share-info '/archive/albums/Christmas at the cottage' # the Share dialog's view, as YAML

In ls -l a + after the mode, as in rwxr-x---+, says the node has an access list. acl prints the bits and the list in the shape getfacl uses:

# node: /archive/albums/Christmas at the cottage
# owner: helen (1003)
# group: users (1002)
user::rwx
group::r-x
other::---
group:family:rwx
user:jon:r-x
link:1073741825:r-x

A link: line is a share link's entry, by its uid; share-link list <path> says which link it is. share-info also lists the photos below a folder that keep their own sharing, its links, and, for an administrator, everyone who has access and through what.

Change the mode

chmod takes a mode as four octal digits or as symbolic clauses:

# in EiN's terminal
chmod 0750 '/archive/albums/Christmas at the cottage'
chmod g+w '/archive/albums/Christmas at the cottage'
chmod u=rwx,g=rx,o= '/archive/albums/Christmas at the cottage'
chmod -r o-rw /archive                 # the whole subtree; each node keeps its other bits
Form Meaning
0750, 750 Set all nine bits. The same mode on every node of a recursive change.
u, g, o, a Owner, group, everyone else, all three. No letter means all three.
+, -, = Add these bits, take them away, or set exactly these.
r, w, x The bits. Clauses are joined with commas.

A symbolic mode is worked out from each node's own mode, as coreutils' chmod -R does, so chmod -r o-rw clears only those bits and leaves every node's other bits as they were. EiN has no setuid, setgid or sticky bit and no X.

-r applies the change to the whole subtree (see What a recursive change does); -L changes a softlink itself rather than what it points to.

Change the owner and the group

# in EiN's terminal
chown jon '/archive/albums/Summer 1987'           # owner only (administrator)
chown jon:family '/archive/albums/Summer 1987'    # owner and group (administrator)
chown :family '/archive/albums/Summer 1987'       # group only
chown -r :family '/archive/albums/Summer 1987'    # group of the whole subtree

The owner and group are written user:group (or user.group), by name or by number, and either half may be left out. The rules are Linux's:

  • Only an administrator gives a node to another user. Anyone else is refused with Access denied for operation 'change uid'.
  • Anyone else may move a node only into a group they are in, and needs write on it. Another group is refused with Access denied for operation 'change gid'.

Without these rules someone could make what they wrote look like someone else's, or file it in a group they do not belong to. The owners of accounts, groups and sign-in sessions are the administrator's alone to change.

Grant more people with an access list

The mode names one group. An access list adds as many users and groups as you need, each with its own bits:

# in EiN's terminal
acl -m g:family:rwx '/archive/albums/Christmas at the cottage'   # the family group may edit
acl -m u:jon:rx '/archive/albums/Christmas at the cottage'       # Jon may view
acl -x u:jon '/archive/albums/Christmas at the cottage'          # take Jon's entry away
acl -b '/archive/albums/Christmas at the cottage'                # remove every user and group entry
acl -r -m g:family:rx '/archive/albums/Christmas at the cottage' # the whole subtree
  • An entry is u:<user>:<bits> or g:<group>:<bits> (user: and group: work too), by name or number. -m replaces an entry for the same user or group rather than adding a second.
  • Can view in the Share dialog is rx and Can edit is rwx.
  • -b leaves share links' entries in place: share-link gives and takes those. share-set --drop-links removes them too.
  • Each node in a recursive change edits its own list, as setfacl -R does, so an entry a folder inside already has beyond the edit stays.

Set it all at once with share-set

share-set changes the mode, the owner, the group and the access list together, in one walk of the subtree, and is what the Share dialog runs when you click Save. Unlike chmod -r, it is recursive unless you say otherwise:

# in EiN's terminal
share-set '/archive/albums/Christmas at the cottage' --mode 0750 -b -m g:family:rwx -m u:jon:rx
share-set '/archive/albums/Summer 1987' --group family --mode g+rx
share-set '/archive/albums/Summer 1987' --default-owner          # the configured default sharing
share-set '/archive/albums/Summer 1987/files/IMG_0042.jpg' --reference '/archive/albums/Summer 1987'
share-set '/archive/albums/Summer 1987/files/IMG_0042.jpg' --mode 0700 -b --drop-links --pin
updated 214, kept own access 3, skipped 0, failed 0
Option What it does
--mode The mode, octal or symbolic.
--owner The owner, by name or uid. Administrators only.
--group The group, by name or gid: one you are in, unless you are an administrator.
--default-owner The configured default owner and group (see What new albums start with), applied by the server to every node you may write.
--reference <path> Share the node as another one is: its group, mode and access list. The owner stays.
-m, -x, -b Edit the access list, as acl does.
--drop-links Remove share links' entries too, so no link opens the nodes.
--pin, --unpin Hold the node against changes from further up, or let them reach it again.
--no-recursive Change only the node named.
--strict Exit non-zero when any node below could not be changed.

share-set exits 0 when only some nodes below refused, and reports them in its counts and on standard error; it fails only when every path it was given refused. Every option is in the reference.

Keep one photo's own sharing

A pinned node keeps its access when a change comes from further up. A recursive change always writes the node it was aimed at, but passes over a pinned node and everything below it. This is what Share in an album's selection bar does when you give chosen photos sharing of their own, and how one photo stays private in a shared album; see Keep some photos private in a shared album.

# in EiN's terminal
share-set '/archive/albums/Summer 1987/files/IMG_0042.jpg' --pin
share-set '/archive/albums/Summer 1987/files/IMG_0042.jpg' --reference '/archive/albums/Summer 1987' --unpin

The pin is written on that node only. share-info on the album lists the pinned nodes below it, and a share link made on the album does not open them.

What a recursive change does

chmod -r, chown -r, acl -r and share-set walk the subtree the same way:

  • It reads the whole subtree first and writes the folder you named last. A change can take away the access the walk needs to get through, so nothing is written until everything has been found, and the folder's own access, which share-info reports, is never ahead of what is below it.
  • Pinned nodes and everything below them are passed over, and counted as "kept own access".
  • Softlinks are changed as links, never followed to their targets, so a link in an album to a photo elsewhere does not change that photo.
  • It stops at mount points. What another store mounts inside the subtree is left alone.
  • A node is changed once, however many places it hangs in.
  • A node that refuses is counted, not fatal, as chmod -R behaves over files someone else owns. What you may not read is not listed to you, so it counts against its folder without names.
  • Ctrl+C stops between batches, with the folder you named as it was.

A plain chmod of a large folder rewrites the stored reach of everything below it (see A folder governs what is under it), so expect it to take a while on an album of thousands of photos.

What a new node starts with

When a node is created and nothing names its access:

Part What it gets
Owner Whoever created it.
Group Its parent's group, as a setgid directory does on Linux.
Mode Everything for the owner; read and pass-through for the group, plus write where the group could write the parent; for everyone else, only the read and pass-through the parent gives them. Others' write is never inherited.
Access list Its parent's, as a Linux default ACL carries down.

So a family album's new uploads, and the thumbnails, faces and metadata processing adds under them, take the album's group and grants whoever created them: a person or group granted an album sees what is added to it later. What is made under /home/<user> stays in that user's group, and the system tree under / stays 0755.

Moving a node with mv keeps its own access, as on Linux. Copying with cp is a create in the destination: the copy takes the destination's group and keeps its source's mode, less group write where the new parent gives none and less everyone else's write.

What new albums start with

A new album, made in the web interface or with album create, and what staging publishes start with the default sharing: an owner, a group and a mode, which an administrator sets under Settings → Permissions, on the Sharing card.

Setting On the card Default
CFG_DEFAULT_SHARE_OWNER Owner empty: whoever creates it
CFG_DEFAULT_SHARE_GROUP Group users
CFG_DEFAULT_SHARE_MODE Permissions: None, View or Edit for owner, group and everyone else 0750

On a standard installation a new album is therefore owned by its creator, seen by everyone with an account and changed only by its owner. Each person narrows or widens that for what they create with How widely what you create is shared on their profile (CFG_USER_DEFAULT_SHARE); see Your profile. Default in the Share dialog, and share-set --default-owner, put a node back on the default sharing.

The same settings in the terminal, in the octal form:

# in EiN's terminal, as the administrator
config set CFG_DEFAULT_SHARE_GROUP family
config set CFG_DEFAULT_SHARE_MODE 0750

Configuration explains where settings are kept.

A folder governs what is under it

Reaching a node by its path takes execute on every folder along the way, from / down. EiN applies the same rule wherever it finds a node without walking a path: search, the Media feed, the map, a person's faces, duplicates, links. A node shows up only when you may read it and at least one of its paths is open to you all the way down.

This is what keeps staging private. /home/<user>/staging is 0700, so the uploads inside it stay out of everyone else's search and map, whatever their own modes say.

  • A node linked in two places is reachable through either. A hard link (ln without -s) into an open folder makes it visible there even when its other path is closed.
  • A softlink is not a path. It points somewhere; whoever follows it still needs the access along the target's own path.
  • What is in someone's trash is never offered by a search, to anyone, the administrator included, though counts may still include it until the trash is emptied.

EiN keeps on each node who can get there, and works it out again below every node whose parents or access change, so group membership and link expiry are judged as you read and changing them rewrites nothing. If you suspect the stored answer is wrong, an administrator can check a sample of nodes against a walk of their paths:

# in EiN's terminal, as the administrator
reach-check
reach-check --sample=10000
2000 nodes judged for 9 callers: 0 disagree

Any line under it names a node, a path and a uid for which the stored answer and the walk differ, and the uids or gids the stored answers name that no account, group or link holds.

The other bits are the system's

The bits for everyone else give every visitor and every account the parts of EiN they all need to run: /, /etc, the commands, the web interface, the modules' files. Content does not use them to share:

Path Owner and group Mode Why
/archive admin, users 0771 Everyone may pass through; nobody but the group reads it.
/archive/tags admin, users 0771 The same.
/home/<user> the account, its own group 0751 Others may pass through to what the owner grants, and read nothing else.
/home/<user>/staging the account 0700 Nobody else, not even through search.
/etc/users/<name> admin 0755 The account itself; only the administrator changes it.
/etc/users/<name>/.password admin 0600 Its user can neither read nor replace it.
/etc/groups/<group>/users its creator, the group 0750 Members may see who else is in the group, not add anyone.
/var/links/<id> admin 0600 A share link's record, holding its token.

Content reaches every account through the users group, and visitors who are not signed in through an access-list entry for the guest group, g:guest:rx, which the Share dialog shows as Visitors who are not signed in. When you open the Share dialog on something whose everyone-else bits still read it, the dialog shows them as Everyone with an account, and Save moves them there and clears the bits.

To clear what older content still gives everyone else:

# in EiN's terminal, as the administrator
chmod -r o-rw /archive

Visitors and share links

Someone who opens EiN without signing in acts as the guest. Whether they see anything depends on Visitors may browse under Settings → Permissions (CFG_GUEST_BROWSING, off unless set):

  • Off: the guest gets the sign-in page and nothing else. In the terminal they may run only login, logout, user-whoami, share-link, config and config-list; anything else answers sign in first.
  • On: the guest sees what is granted to the guest group, and what the other bits give.

The guest never writes, whatever a mode or an access list says, its own /home/guest included.

A share link is a principal of its own: a uid nobody else holds, in no group. Making a link adds an entry rx for it on the node and on everything below it that its creator may change, passing over pinned nodes. Whoever opens the link reads what it opens and nothing an earlier link opened, never writes, and loses the access when the link expires. Turning a link off takes its entry off every node, wherever the node has moved since. Someone already signed in who opens a link stays themselves. How to make and send one is on Sharing.

Administrators

The administrator, uid 0, passes every check, as root does on Linux: private albums and other people's homes and staging included. A member of the sudo group passes them too while their sign-in is elevated, which they start with Administer… in the account menu (or admin in a terminal) and end with Leave admin mode (or admin --off). A terminal acting as the administrator ends its prompt in #. Accounts and groups covers who may do this.

Commands that only an administrator may run refuse everyone else before they start:

user-create: permission denied: only an administrator may run it

Only an administrator creates or hard-links an account, group or session node, and nobody, the administrator included, changes an account's uid or a group's gid once it exists, since ownership and access lists name the number. When an account or group is deleted, its number is taken off every node first: what it owned goes to the account named with --to (the administrator by default), and every access-list entry naming it is dropped, so whoever is given that number later inherits nothing.

Over SSH, SFTP and the web

The same rules apply wherever you come in: the web interface, EiN's terminal, SSH and SFTP all sign in as the same account and see only what it may. Downloading a file over the web takes read on it. See SSH and SFTP.

A folder mounted from another machine with mount … sftp is different: every node in it is owned by the mount's owner and group (the administrator who mounted it, by default), and its bits are the ones the SFTP server reports.

When something goes differently

A command says Access denied for operation 'w' on node: …. The letter is what was missing: r, w or x. The node named is the one that refused, which may be the folder above what you asked for: creating, removing or moving a node takes w and x on its folder, and moving it also takes w on the node itself. Check with acl on the node and on its folder.

Someone added to a group still cannot see the album. Groups are read at sign-in. Ask them to sign out and in again.

A folder's mode changed but a photo inside did not. The photo is pinned, or you may not write it. The counts after share-set (kept own access, failed) and the lines on standard error say which. share-info on the folder lists the pinned nodes; share-set <path> --unpin lets changes reach a node again.

A photo is open to someone but they cannot find it in search or on the map. A folder on every path to it is closed to them. Give them pass-through on that folder, or link the photo into a folder they can reach.

chown says Access denied for operation 'change gid'. You are not in the group you named. An administrator can make the change, or add you to the group.

share-info says sign in to see who has access. You are acting as the guest. Who has access names people and groups, so it is shown only to someone signed in.

A visitor sees the sign-in page instead of a shared album. Visitors may browse is off. Turn it on under Settings → Permissions, or send them a share link, which works either way.
