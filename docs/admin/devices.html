Administration

Devices and their keys

Devices are everyone's to look at and to switch, a few people's to add and remove, and a device's own to write to when it has a key. This page is for administrators: who may do what with the devices under /dev, how to narrow a switch to some people, what a device key opens, and what reaches EiN without signing in. Devices describes the pages, and Devices, endpoints and drivers how devices are kept.

Who may do what

Who What they may do
Everyone signed in See every device, its readings and its history; switch and set every control; bind queues and logs of their own to any device's routing keys
Members of the devices group Also make devices (device-create), rename them (device-label) and forget them (device-forget); make and renew the key of any device
A device's owner Whoever made it by hand: also write values to its endpoints, edit its track (its trips, with the Archive), make its key, and narrow it with chmod
The administrator Everything: also add people to devices, own the devices drivers find, and narrow who may switch a control

EiN makes the group devices (Devices, "May make, name and forget the devices under /dev") at every start when it is missing. Add someone to it in Settings → Groups, or in the terminal; the change applies from their next sign-in:

# in EiN's terminal, as the administrator
group-add-member devices helen

/dev is the administrator's, with the group devices and the mode 1775, so members of the group add devices to it; EiN sets this owner and mode again at every start. The device commands check the group themselves: device-label and device-forget act on any device for a member, whoever made it. Remove a device with device-forget rather than rm: it also tells the device's driver to let it go, so a broker's last announcement does not make it again.

A device a driver finds belongs to the administrator, since drivers run as EiN's services; a device made with device-create belongs to whoever made it. Either way its group is devices, with the mode 0775. Its endpoints take the device's owner and group: readings and tracks 0644, so only the owner adds to them, and controls 0664 with the group users, so everyone with an account may switch them. A rule of the Automation module switches as the person whose rule it is, with that person's rights.

Important

Readings are not narrowed device by device. Everyone signed in may read any device's history and bind a queue or a log of their own to its routing keys, door and motion sensors included: whoever has an account can tell when the front door opened or someone moved in a room, and a phone's track says where its owner has been. Narrowing a device's nodes, as below, keeps its history from others, but not what it publishes from then on: anyone signed in may still bind a queue to dev.<device>.#.

Keep a switch to some people

Each control is a node of its own, and whoever may write it may switch it. To keep the heater to a group, such as parents:

# in EiN's terminal, as the administrator
chown :parents /dev/heater-plug/switch-0

Anyone else is then refused with Access denied for operation 'w' on node: …, on the page, in the terminal and in their rules. chmod 644 /dev/heater-plug/switch-0 keeps it to its owner instead, the administrator for a device a driver found. A driver leaves the owner, group and mode of an endpoint it already has as they are, so the change holds.

Keep a device to some people

To keep a whole device's history, such as a phone's track, to its owner, the devices group and one group more:

# in EiN's terminal, as its owner or the administrator
chmod o-rx /dev/ruby-phone
acl -m g:parents:rx /dev/ruby-phone

Reaching an endpoint takes passing through its device, so this closes every endpoint of it to everyone else, visitors included. Permissions in depth explains modes and access lists.

Device keys

A device cannot sign in. A phone, a tracker or a script that posts to EiN carries the device's key instead, made with the device's Info tab or device-key:

# in EiN's terminal
device-key ruby-phone --new --origin https://ein.wrenfield.example
https://ein.wrenfield.example/file/dev/ruby-phone?key=Zt2v…
  • What it opens. Writes to /file/dev/<device> and /file/dev/<device>/<endpoint> of that one device, and nothing else: no other device, no reading, no other address. A GET with the key on the device is the device talking, for a tracker that sends only GETs, such as OsmAnd. A write with the key is made as the device's owner, limited to the device's folder, and the device's driver is told it came from the device (device:ruby-phone).
  • How it is sent. As Authorization: Bearer <key>, or as key= in the address for a client that takes only an address, such as Traccar Client.
  • Who sees it. Whoever may change the device: its owner, the members of devices and the administrator. It is kept in the device's .key, mode 0600, so the address can be shown again.
  • Renewing it. --new, or New key on the Info tab, makes another; the old address stops working at once. --off, or Take the key away, removes it, and nothing writes to the device without signing in.

A request with a wrong key, a key for another device, or a device whose owner's account is disabled is answered 403 Forbidden, the same for each, and logged in the system log as device write refused with the reason and the client's address:

# in EiN's terminal, as the administrator
dmesg | grep 'device write refused'

A write without a key is a signed-in person's, as every /file write is, and needs their session.

What reaches EiN without signing in

  • A device with its key, writing to /file/dev/<device> as above. Any other write there needs a signed-in session.
  • Visitors, while Visitors may browse is on. A visitor reads what everyone else may (Visitors and share links), and a device and its readings are readable by everyone else unless narrowed: the device's page opens for a visitor who has its address. Keep a device from visitors with the two lines in Keep a device to some people, granting users instead of a group. Visitors never switch a control.
  • The MQTT broker, with the Automation module's, is not EiN: the devices sign in to it with their own password, and whoever reaches it with that password switches them. Keep its port in your home network (Before you open EiN to the internet).

A phone away from home reaches EiN only when EiN is reachable from the internet over HTTPS; see Phones and trackers posting.

When something goes differently

What you see What it means What to do
only the devices group's members make, name and forget devices The person is not in devices, or was added since they signed in Add them, and have them sign in again
device-key: only whoever may change <device> sees or changes its key The person may not change the device Its owner, a member of devices or the administrator makes the key
A phone's posts are refused with 403 Forbidden Its key was renewed or taken away, or its owner's account was disabled Give the phone the address device-key shows now, or a new one
device write refused in the system log, from an address you do not know Something tries keys, or holds an old address Renew the key with device-key <device> --new if it may have got out
Access denied for operation 'w' on node: /dev/… when switching The control is narrowed to others Widen it, or leave it
