Administration

Health and status

This page is for the administrator who keeps EiN running: how to tell at a glance whether it is well and keeping up, what each part of the System status shows, how to ask the same questions in the terminal or from another computer, and what to do when the answer is not Healthy.

Open the Status page

Choose Status under System in the sidebar. The sidebar shows it only to administrators; the page is at /system, and someone who is not an administrator and opens that address reads the same figures without the controls to start and stop services.

The page, System status, has up to three badges above its title, with how long EiN has been running beside them (up 3d 4h), and five tabs below it, six with the ems module installed:

Badge What it says
Healthy, Catching up, Stuck or Needs a person How things stand. Hover it for the sentence behind it, such as 1,240 jobs waiting, the slowest kind done in 25m00s.
offline The browser has lost its connection to EiN and is reconnecting; the figures are from the last update.
warming up The sampler that records CPU, memory and disk has not written anything yet. The charts fill in within a minute of a start.
Tab What it holds
Overview The checks that are not fine, the machines' CPU and memory, the work done, storage and the database
Work Every pipeline and every kind of job: what is waiting, how fast it goes, when it will be done
Media service With the ems module: the media service's own state; see The media service
Services Which services run, with start, stop and restart for the administrator
Log The last problems logged, and the system log as it is written
Activity Who signed in, opened a share link or downloaded what, and who changed what

The tab you choose goes into the address (/system?tab=work), so a link opens the same view. The pipeline figures update whenever the pipelines change; every other card asks EiN for its own figures every 10 seconds, and only while its tab is open. The badges and the alarm above the tabs are kept current on every tab. Nothing is fetched while the browser tab is hidden, and the numbers refresh as soon as you come back.

What the verdict means

The badge is the first line of the health command. It is decided in this order: a failed check first, then whether anything is waiting, then whether the waiting work moves.

Verdict When What to do
Healthy No check failed and nothing is waiting. Nothing.
Catching up Work is waiting, and some kind of it was done, or some pipeline instance finished, in the last hour. Wait. After an import this can last hours; it turns to Healthy when the work is done.
Stuck Work is waiting, and none of it was done and no instance finished in the last hour. Look at the checks on Overview and at Where the work is on Work; see below.
Needs a person At least one check failed. Read the failed check on Overview: each one says what is wrong and, often, the command that fixes it.

Warnings never change the verdict on their own. An install that has the ems module but no media service, or the Assistant but no language model, reads Healthy with a warning about each, and the same warnings show for a while after every start while the services load their models. An EiN with no modules has neither check.

Read the checks

On Overview, a card above the tiles lists every check that is not fine, failed ones first, each with its reason; a red edge marks a failed check, a yellow one a warning. When every check is fine the card is not there. health in the terminal lists them all, the fine ones too.

These are the checks core makes about EiN's own work, with or without modules:

Check State Reason, as it reads What to do
intake ok 12 matched triggers waiting to start pipeline work Nothing: new work about to start.
kind <name> ok 340 left, 120/h eta 2.8h, 340 waiting, the oldest for 4m10s, or 340 waiting behind other work, the oldest for 2h05m Nothing: a line of work that is being done, has just arrived, or waits while its worker does other lines. The Work tab shows which.
kind <name> ok 340 waiting: EMS could not run it just now, and it is tried again on its own in 1m30s Nothing: with the Archive, the media service was busy, restarting or loading a model. The check goes back to the line above as soon as one of the jobs is done.
kind <name> warn 340 waiting: EMS has not been able to run it for 42m00s. See EMS on the Services tab; the Work tab shows what it answered With the Archive: the media service has turned this work away for half an hour. Look at it on Services; what it answered is under Where the work is on Work. See The media service.
kind <name> warn 340 waiting, the oldest for 2h05m, and the workers that take it are idle: EiN is not handing it out. The Log tab may say why Work waits while the workers that take it do nothing. Read the Log tab for why; restarting EiN starts its workers anew.
kind <name> fail 340 waiting, and no worker offers this kind, the oldest for 12m30s See Work that nothing will run.
failed work warn 3 failed instances, run again on their own after 1 h, 6 h and 24 h Nothing yet: failed work is run again on its own.
failed work fail 2 instances failed again after three more runs and need a person (pipeline-instances --status failed) List them with pipeline-instances -s failed and see Processing and pipelines.
input ok 5 steps met input nothing can process, and will not be run again Nothing: a damaged or unreadable file. Running it again would give the same result.
quiet steps warn 1 running step silent for 30m00s or more: media-transcode/transcode A step has written nothing and moved no progress for a long while. It keeps running; nothing stops a step for taking long.
queue <name> fail 4 entries dropped after their delivery attempts (queue-dead /var/queues/archive-bridge: --retry or --drop) Look at them with queue-dead <path>, then put them back with --retry or delete them with --drop.

How long a step may stay silent before it counts as quiet is the setting CFG_PIPELINE_STEP_QUIET_MS, 30 minutes by default.

Modules add checks of their own, through the services they run. Each is there only with its module installed, and asked only while its service runs:

Check From What it says
ems and ems … the ems module's ems-link service (ems-status --checks) Whether the media service answers, its model worker, each degradation and the card; the table is in The media service. Only ems gpu can fail.
llm and llm model the Assistant's llm-link service (llm-status --checks) Whether the language model answers, whose it is and where it runs, and whether its model has been tried with the assistant. See The language model.
automation bridge the Automation module's automation-bridge service (automation-status --checks) Whether the bridge hears the MQTT broker: fine with the devices its drivers drive and their endpoints, a warning while it cannot reach the broker, failed while its service is stopped; none while no broker is set. See Check that EiN hears them.
the service's name any service whose check does not answer its check did not answer, as a warning.

When a check names a hosted language model, its reason says so plainly, for example …: questions, and what the assistant reads to answer them, leave this host.

The Overview tab

Under the checks, Overview shows the machine and the work in four parts.

  • The machines: a card for each machine you may see, made from its device under Devices. The machine EiN runs on comes first (Core's host driver). With the ems module installed, Media service is the machine the media service runs on, a card of its own even when it is the same machine (The media service's machine as a device). A NAS or a server that EiN reads over SNMP has one too (Automation). A card's name opens the device's page, and its badge says whether it answers. Its tiles, each with a small chart of the last six hours:

    • CPU: the share of all the machine's processor cores in use, with its load. On EiN's own machine it also gives EiN's own share. A machine that does not tell its CPU share has its load instead.
    • memory used: the share of the machine's memory in use, with how much of how much. On EiN's own machine it also gives how much EiN holds.
    • GPU and VRAM used, on a machine with a graphics card: how busy the card is, with its temperature and power draw, and the share of its memory in use. They are missing when the media service sees no card: no tile means no card, not an idle one (The graphics card).
    • The hottest of the machine's temperatures, named as the machine names that sensor (x86_pkg_temp is the processor).

    A share in use turns its tile amber at 75% and red at 90%. A device of class host or storage that has none of these readings, such as a NAS EiN only pings, has no card.

  • Work: Done per hour, the pipeline instances finished in the last hour, with the count for the last 24 hours and a chart of them; the total number of instances; and Instances by state, a bar of In line, Running, Failed, Done and Cancelled.

  • Storage: a card for each storage tier, with its path, a pie of its free space and of the used space split in two, the tier's own files and other, whatever else is on that disk; then the free space, % of … used, the size of the tier's files and of the other, how much it grows per day and, when it grows, full in ~N days, in red when that is under 14 days. Tiers that share one disk say shares and the device: their free space is the same space, counted once, and each counts the other's files as other. The size of a tier's files is the sum of the sizes EiN has recorded for them, counted every 15 minutes, so it is absent until the first count and includes a file that is only a link to bytes on another disk, such as an original imported from an older archive. Beside them, Database: its size and a pie of what takes it, its five largest tables and the rest as other. The free space on the database's own disk is not shown, since the database may run on another disk or machine; see Performance and sizing for how much it needs. Files and storage explains the tiers.

The storage projection is measured over the last seven days. A tier that is not growing has no "full in" figure, and a tier filling up fast is the one thing on this tab that needs action before it becomes a problem: Your data covers moving the archive to a bigger disk.

The Work tab

Work shows what the pipelines are doing; Processing and pipelines explains pipelines, how to rerun, cancel and retry work, and what each one does.

  • Where the work is draws the work left to right as it moves: Not started (changes noticed, work not made yet, by pipeline), In line (made, waiting for a worker; each line of work under the workers that serve it, the one a worker is on now outlined), and Running in EiN (each worker, its slots and the line it is on, with how much of its turn is left). With the ems module installed, the media service's lanes and the models it holds follow. Under it, each line that is not moving says why. Processing and pipelines explains it.
  • The pipeline table has a row for each pipeline doing something, with Workload, Not started, In line, Running, Failed, Done and Cancelled; the idle ones fold into one row that show opens. In is the work arriving for it per hour, Out what it finished in the last hour, and Finishes in the work not started divided by Out; no rate when nothing finished recently, — when nothing waits. When queue entries were dropped after their delivery attempts, a red N dropped stands above the table. Choose a row to see that pipeline's instances.
  • Lines lists each line of work in the work pools that has something in it or was done lately: In line, Running, In, Out, Finishes in and Oldest in line. A red no worker badge marks a line nothing serves; with the Archive, a yellow waiting for EMS badge marks a line resting because the media service refused several of its jobs in a row, with the next try and what the media service answered in its tooltip. EMS could not run one marks a line whose last job the media service refused, with its answer in the tooltip, until one of the line's jobs is done.
  • Ongoing lists the instances running now, with how far the running step has come, for a step that says, and how long each has run. Steps that have written nothing and moved no progress for the quiet time are listed above it in a yellow box.

Work that nothing will run

When a line has jobs ready and no running worker serves it for more than five minutes, a red box, Work that nothing will run, stands above the tabs on every tab, and the kind <name> check fails. The box names the pool, how many jobs are ready and for how long, and the pipelines that queue that line.

  • When it names pipelines, the worker that should serve the line is not running. Open Services and start the stopped one, or read the Log tab for why it stopped.

  • When it says that no defined pipeline queues the line any more, the jobs belong to instances whose pipeline changed or is gone. If the pipeline now queues that step in another line, cancel the jobs with the command the box gives, and the engine queues them again in the new line:

    # in EiN's terminal, as an administrator
pool cancel /var/pools/media --kind gpu-describe

    If the pipeline is no longer defined, cancelling does not last, since the engine queues the jobs again while their instances are open; pipeline prune removes the instances and their jobs once it has been gone for ten minutes. Work that nothing will run has both.

The Media service tab

The ems module adds this tab, and it is there only while that module is installed. It shows the media service's version, its models, what it runs now and what is on the graphics card; The media service describes it.

The Services tab

Services lists the services inside EiN, the ones that are not running first. Each row has the service's name, its own status line or description, how long it has been up, how many times it has restarted (×3), and its state: running, starting, stopping, stopped, failed or disabled. The heading counts them, 21 healthy, or in red 2 down when enabled services are not running.

An administrator has Restart and Stop on a running service and Start on a stopped one. A disabled service has no buttons; enable it from the terminal first. Services and logs explains each service and the commands behind these buttons.

The Log tab

Log has two cards. Recent problems shows the last 10 lines logged at WARN or ERROR, newest first, each with what was logged beside its message (the step, the pool, what a service answered) under it, or Nothing logged at WARN or above. Live log follows the system log, /var/logs/system, as it is written: choose DEBUG, INFO, WARN or ERROR to show that level and above, pause it, or clear it. While you read further up, new lines do not scroll the view away.

With the Archive, a warning such as pooled step could not run yet, job waiting means the media service was not ready for a job; the job is tried again on its own, and the kind's other jobs run meanwhile. pooled step could not run yet, kind resting means it refused several in a row, so the whole kind waits. Services and logs covers the logs and dmesg.

The Activity tab

Activity lists, newest first, who reached EiN and what they did:

Shown as When
signed in, signed out Someone signed in or out in the Web UI, a terminal or over SSH. An SSH sign-in says password or publickey.
sign-in failed A wrong password, an account that may not sign in, or too many tries. The reason is under the row. An account that does not exist shows as unknown account: what was typed is not kept, since it is sometimes a password.
became administrator, elevation failed A member of sudo chose Administer…, or got the password wrong.
opened share link A share link was opened. A visitor shows as the link's number; someone signed in as themselves.
downloaded A file someone put in /archive or a home was sent in full, saved or shown, in the browser or over SFTP. Thumbnails and other renditions are not counted, and the same person reading the same file again within ten minutes counts once.
downloaded zip Several files were downloaded as one zip; the row names the folder that holds them and how many there were.
created, changed, moved, deleted … A change to the archive, from the audit trail: the row names the kind of node and, for a change, what changed.

Each row has the time, the account, where the event happened (a path), how it came (web, terminal, ssh, sftp or http) and from which address, and the session. Choose a name, path, address or session in a row to show only that; the session shows a sign-in together with everything that sign-in changed. Above the list you can show only Sign-ins, Failures, Downloads, Share links or Changes, and narrow by user, path, address, session and days. Show more reads further back. Changes are read from the last 14 days; at the end of the list, Show older changes reads the rest, which takes longer. Choosing days or a session reads those days or that session, however old.

What pipelines derive from each file (thumbnails, faces, text) is left out of the changes unless you turn on Include what pipelines derived: there are about ninety such records for every photo.

The same lists are in the terminal, for administrators only:

# in EiN's terminal
access-log --event sign-in-failed --since 2026-11-01
access-log /archive/albums/wrenfield-summer-1987 --event download --event download-archive
audit --sid session-01JC4ZK3E2 --no-pipeline

EiN keeps sign-ins and downloads for a year: the access-log-purger service removes older ones, after Days the access log keeps sign-ins and downloads (CFG_ACCESS_LOG_RETENTION_DAYS, 0 keeps them for good). Changes are kept for good, as Trace a result back to its cause describes.

Ask in the terminal

Every figure on the Status page comes from an ordinary command, and you can run the same commands in EiN's terminal or over SSH. health gives the verdict and every check:

# in EiN's terminal
health

On an EiN with the Archive and the ems module, it reads like this:

catching up: 1,240 jobs waiting, the slowest kind done in 2h48m
warn  kind transcribe  40 waiting: EMS has not been able to run it for 42m00s. See EMS on the Services tab; the Work tab shows what it answered
ok    ems              EMS 3f2a91c answering, up 2.1h
ok    intake           12 matched triggers waiting to start pipeline work
ok    kind faces       1,188 left, 420/h  eta 2.8h
What you want to know Command
The verdict and the checks health
CPU, memory and event-loop lag, with history host-metrics
Free space per tier, when it fills, the database's size df
Which instance, its versions and uptime system-info
Each pool and kind of job: left, rates, oldest waiting pool-stat
Every pipeline's counts and rates pipeline-overview
The meters services add, such as the card's memory gauges
The services and their status lines service-list --detail
The last problems in the log dmesg --level warn -n 10
What is running now, and who is connected ps, who

host-metrics also shows the event loop's lag, lag p99 1.2 ms max 8.4 ms, which the Status page does not: how long ready work waits for EiN's process. High lag with low CPU is the case where everything feels slow and nothing looks busy; Performance and sizing covers it.

Put JSON_OUTPUT=1 in front of any of them for the answer as JSON. The verdict is then one of healthy, catching-up, stuck and needs-person:

# in EiN's terminal
JSON_OUTPUT=1 health | jq -r .verdict

Watch it live with top

top draws on one screen what the Status page spreads over its tabs: a CPU graph and the load, memory, the event loop and the services' gauges, the disks, the verdict with every check that is not fine and the work done in the last hour, the running commands, the sessions, the services and the latest problems. It redraws every 2 seconds.

# in EiN's terminal
top

In the terminal panel, press Alt+M first to give it the whole window (The terminal).

Key What it does
q or Ctrl+C Quit
↑ ↓, k j Scroll the list of commands
PgUp PgDn, Home End Scroll a page, or to the top or bottom
s Sort the commands: newest, oldest, by user or by command
+ and - Update less or more often, in steps from 0.5 to 60 seconds
p Pause, so a list can be read; again to go on
Space Update now

top -d 5 starts at 5 seconds between updates. top -n 1 writes one frame and stops, which is what you want in a script or over ssh without a terminal.

Check from another computer

EiN sends no e-mail and raises no alert outside itself: the Status page and health are where problems show. To look without opening the Web UI, send health over SSH:

# on your own computer
ssh -p 2222 helen@ein.local health

health always ends with exit status 0, whatever the verdict, so a script that watches EiN has to read the verdict. The first word of the text is enough, or the JSON:

# on your own computer, for example from cron
verdict=$(ssh -p 2222 helen@ein.local 'JSON_OUTPUT=1 health' | jq -r .verdict)
[ "$verdict" = needs-person ] && echo "EiN needs a person" | mail -s "EiN" helen@example.com

SSH and SFTP explains signing in with a key so a script needs no password.

Where the figures come from

The metrics-sampler service records EiN's own and the work's figures, one log per series under /var/logs/metrics, and the machine's as the readings of its device, /dev/<machine>; the Status page, top and the commands above read them back. The charts therefore survive a reload of the page and a restart of EiN.

Series What it holds How often Kept
cpu Processor time used by EiN's container 15 seconds 7 days
memory EiN's container's memory in use against its limit 15 seconds 7 days
runtime Event-loop lag 15 seconds 7 days
pools Jobs per pool, by state 15 seconds 7 days
pipelines Instances completed per pipeline 1 minute 30 days
disk Free and total space per storage tier 15 minutes 90 days
nodes How many nodes of each type 15 minutes 365 days

With the ems module installed, its ems-sampler service writes the media service machine's card, memory and CPU every 15 seconds as the readings of its device, /dev/ems, which the Overview draws as the Media service card (The media service's machine as a device), and adds an ems-models series, every minute, of what the media service holds. Memory and CPU are read both ways: the whole machine's, since the media service and the language model, when installed, load it from containers of their own, and EiN's container's own, from its cgroup. The machine's are its device's readings, cpu, load, memory-used and memory-total, with its disks and temperatures beside them, kept for 30 days and a year; Core's host driver lists them, and the device's page under Devices charts them.

How the tree has grown over the years is not on the Status page: Statistics, under System, shows it, and anyone can open it (Home and statistics).

When something goes differently

What you see Why What to do
warming up stays, the tiles are empty, host-metrics says no metrics sampled yet — is metrics-sampler running? The metrics-sampler service is not running. Start it on the Services tab, or service start metrics-sampler, and read the Log tab for why it stopped.
Storage says No tier data sampled yet, df says no disk sample yet — is metrics-sampler running? The disk is sampled every 15 minutes; right after a start there is no sample yet. Wait 15 minutes. If it stays, check metrics-sampler as above.
offline next to the verdict The browser lost its connection to EiN, for example while EiN restarts. Wait; it reconnects by itself. If it stays, reload the page and see Troubleshooting.
Stuck, with EMS has not been able to run it on a kind With the Archive: the media service is down, restarting over and over, or cannot load a model. See The media service. The work goes on by itself once it answers.
Stuck, with the workers that take it are idle on a kind The work is not handed out. Read the Log tab, and restart EiN. Services and logs.
Needs a person, kind <name> failed Nothing runs that kind of job. Work that nothing will run.
Needs a person, ems gpu failed With the ems module: the media service is meant to have a graphics card and sees none. The graphics card.
Needs a person, failed work or queue <name> failed Work failed for good, or queue entries were dropped. Follow the command in the check's reason, and see Processing and pipelines.
full in ~N days in red A storage tier will be full within two weeks at its present rate. Free space, or move the archive to a bigger disk: Your data.
A machine's CPU or memory tile is red for long The machine's processors or memory are 90% or more in use. Performance and sizing.
Status is not in the sidebar You are not an administrator. Ask an administrator, or open /system to read the figures; health works for everyone.
