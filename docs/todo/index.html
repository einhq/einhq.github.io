Todo

Todo lists

The Todo module keeps lists of things to do: lists of your own that nobody else sees, and lists you share with a group, such as the household's shopping list. Each list is a board whose columns an item moves through, from Todo to Done. An item can have a day it is due and someone to do it, and what falls due shows on Home among the events.

Install it

Install it from Settings → Modules, or in EiN's terminal:

# in EiN's terminal, as the administrator
mod install todo

Todo is then in the sidebar. Modules covers installing and removing modules.

Lists are folders, items are files

A list is a folder, named by its title, and each item on it is a Markdown file you can read and change in any editor, over SFTP or in EiN's terminal as well as on the Todo page:

---
due: 2026-12-01
assignee: helen
---

# Renew the passport

The old one is in the desk drawer.
  • The first heading is the item's title, and what follows it is its description.
  • The front matter between the --- lines says the day it is due, who is to do it (assignee) and its place on the list (order). All are optional, and an item with none needs no front matter at all.
  • A new item's file is named for when it was added, such as 2026-11-05-140322.md, so the files sort in the order they came. Any other name works as well: rename a file, or write a text file of your own into the list, and it is an item.
  • Items are in the order their order numbers say, in each column of the list. Dragging an item on the page, or todo-move, numbers the items of the column it lands in from 1 in their new order; an item with no number comes after those that have one, in the order it was added.
  • An item can be about something else in EiN, such as a picture to fix or an album to sort: a softlink under the item's own file points at it, and goes with the item when it is done. When the picture is moved or renamed, the link follows it.
  • A done item is moved to the list's done/ folder. An item in done/ is done whether or not its file says so. Moving it there on the page, or with todo-done or todo-move, also writes done: and done-by: into its front matter, the time and who did it.

Columns are folders too

A list is a board of columns, and an item is in one of them:

  • Todo is the list's own folder: what is not started yet. ls of a list is its Todo column.
  • Every folder in the list is a column of its own, such as In progress/ or Waiting/. It is called by its folder's name, unless the folder has a name attribute, which is then its title: attrs ~/todos/Fix/doing -s 'name=In progress'. The columns stand in the order of their order attributes, which moving a column on the page writes, and those without one after them in the order the folders were made.
  • Done is the list's done/ folder, always the last column. A new list has one from the start.
  • archived/ is where done items are put away. It is no column, so they are off the board and out of the counts, but they are still files in the list.

Your own lists and a group's

A list is either yours or a group's, and that is decided when you make it:

  • Your own lists are in ~/todos in your home. Nobody else sees them, the administrator aside.
  • A group's lists are in todos/ of the group's shared home, such as /groups/family/todos. Every member of the group can read, add, tick off and change them, and nobody else sees them. Everyone with an account is in users, so a list shared with users is the whole household's.

You can share a list only with a group that has a shared home and that you are in. users has one from the start; the administrator gives other groups theirs.

Use the Todo page

Open Todo in the sidebar. The bar at the top has a tab for Overview and then one for every list you can see, your own marked with a list and a group's with two people; hovering a tab says whose it is. On a phone the tabs scroll sideways.

A list's columns fill the page under the bar side by side, a line between each, sharing its width and running to its foot; each scrolls its own cards. When there are more than fit, the board scrolls sideways.

On a phone a list reads as a plain list: one row per item, the columns one after the other with no headings, Todo first and Done last.

  • The box at a row's left ticks the item off, moving it to Done; unticking it in Done moves it back to Todo.

  • A slowly turning spinner after the title says the item is under way: it is in a column of your own, neither Todo nor Done.

  • A chevron after that, when the item has a description, unfolds it.

  • At the right are its due day (or a calendar to give it one) and whoever is to do it.

  • What it links to shows under the title as pictures alone.

  • Hold a row still for half a second to open the item in its dialog; hold it and move to move it.

  • Add an item floats at the foot of the screen, and adds to Todo.

  • Columns are made, renamed and deleted, and done items archived, on a computer, or with the commands below.

  • Make a list: choose ⋮ in the page's bar and New list, type a Title such as Shopping, and under Shared with keep Nobody, it is mine or pick a group. Choose Create list.

  • Add an item: open the list's tab, type in Add an item at the foot of Todo (on a phone, of the screen) and press Enter. The line stays for the next one. Shift+Enter, or pasting several lines, gives it more: the first line is the item's title and the lines after it its description, blank ones at its top and foot left out.

  • Move an item: drag its card to another place in the column, or to another column; a dashed frame shows where it will land. Dropped on Done it is done, and dragged out of Done it is not. On a phone, lifting a row brings up the names of the columns at the foot of the screen: let go over one and the card goes to the end of that column.

  • Assign it: the round button at a card's top right (on a phone, at the row's right) is the face of whoever is to do it, their person's picture or their initials, or an empty circle when it is nobody's. Choose it and, under Assign to, a member of the list's group, or Nobody in particular.

  • Give it a due day: the calendar at a card's top right picks one; once set, the day stands there instead, before the face, and opens the picker again, where No due day takes it away.

  • Read the description: a card shows the first line of its description, with … when there is more. Choose it to read the whole of it, as formatted Markdown, and again to fold it.

  • Change an item: the pencil at a card's top right, or on a phone a long press on its row, opens the item in a dialog: its Title, its Description, written in Markdown (lists, links, bold; Preview shows how it will look), the day it is Due, and Assign to, where typing a name narrows the members to choose from. Save keeps the change.

  • Link an item: in the same dialog, type in Link to to find a person, a place, an album or the other things the top bar's search finds by name, and choose one; or type a whole path, such as /albums/Summer/beach.jpg, to link a picture or a folder. A link is made as soon as you choose it, and the cross on one takes it away.

  • Delete an item: Delete in the dialog removes it for good, with its links; what they point at stays where it is.

  • Add a column: open the list's tab, choose ⋮ in the page's bar and New column, and type its name; it comes before Done. The pencil in a column's heading renames it, Left and Right move it among the list's own columns, and once no card is left in it, Delete takes it away. Todo stays first and Done last: they cannot be moved, Todo cannot be renamed, Done cannot be deleted, and done and archived are names the list keeps for itself.

  • Delete a list: the cross on its tab, then Delete the list. The list goes for good, with every item on it, archived ones too, and for everyone a group's list is shared with; what the items link to stays where it is.

  • Archive done items: tick the cards in Done you want put away, or the box in its heading for all of them, and choose Archive. They move to the list's archived/ folder.

On a computer the due day and the face stand at the right of a card's title, which runs on under them when it is long; while the pointer is over the card its pencil, and its calendar when it has no due day, show beside them. A due day that has passed is red.

On a computer a stripe down a card's left edge is coloured by how long it has been on the list: green on the day it is added, then yellow, and red once it is four weeks old; a done card's is grey. Hovering the card says when it was added. An item can be given only to a member of the list's group; on a list of your own every item is yours.

Overview is everything of yours, soonest due first: the items assigned to you on any list, and the items of your own lists assigned to nobody, done ones too (archived ones are not). Its columns are those of the lists your items are on, put together by title: Todo, then every other column in the order it is first met, two lists' Ongoing being one column, and Done last. A card names its list with a label at its foot, with the icon of the list's tab, and on a phone a row's title starts with it, Shopping › Milk; choose the list's name to open its tab. Drag a card to another column to move it there on its own list; a column its list does not have takes no drop.

A card's links are listed under its description, each with its picture: a picture's own, an album's cover, a person's, place's or camera's picture. A picture or a video shows as its picture alone, its name said on hovering it; any other link is named too, called what it points at is called, such as an album's title. Choosing one opens it: a picture in the lightbox, an album or a person on their page. A link to something that is gone, or that you may not see, is greyed out.

The todo widget

The todo widget keeps the todos about what you are looking at in reach on every page. Turn it on with the pin in the Todo page's bar, Show the todo widget, and off with the same button, Hide the todo widget; it is also Todo widget under Profile → Preferences, and it is yours alone, on every device you sign in on.

While it is on, a round button floats over every page and over the lightbox; dialogs open over it. Drag it wherever it is out of your way; it stays there on that device.

  • Its badge counts the open todos, on any list you can see, that link to what is on screen: the picture open in the lightbox, or the album, person, place or camera whose page is open. Stepping from picture to picture in the lightbox, the count follows.
  • With none, choosing the button opens New todo item at once, with About what is on screen ticked, so the new item links to it. Adding it leaves you where you were.
  • With some, choosing it slides out a list of them, above the button or below it, whichever way has more room. Tick one to mark it done, choose its title to open it in the item's dialog, or choose New todo about this to add another. Choosing anywhere else puts the list away.

On a page about nothing in particular, such as Home or Search, the button has no badge and adds an item that links to nothing.

On Home

Home's Events lists the items falling due that are yours to do: given to you, or to nobody on a list you can see. They show from a week before they are due until a week after, beside the other events, and choosing one opens its list. Home and statistics covers the rest of Home.

Ask the assistant

With the Assistant installed, the chat reads and changes your lists too: ask "what's on the shopping list?", "what do I have left to do?" or "add milk and bread to the shopping list". It finds a list you name among those you can see, and with a list open on the Todo page, "this list" and "add it" mean that one. Reading happens at once; adding, ticking off, moving or changing an item is a proposal card you run or decline.

From the terminal

Every part of the page is a command, and a list or an item is named by its path:

# in EiN's terminal
todo-lists                                          # every list you can see
todo-list-create Shopping --group users             # a list shared with users
todo-list-create 'Before the trip'                  # a list of your own
todo-add /groups/users/todos/Shopping Milk
todo-add ~/todos/'Before the trip' 'Renew the passport' --due 2026-12-01
todo-add ~/todos/'Before the trip' Pack --description 'Warm clothes, the charger.'
todo-items /groups/users/todos/Shopping             # the list, its columns and their items
todo-item /groups/users/todos/Shopping/2026-11-05-140322.md   # one item, with its description and links
ls /groups/users/todos/Shopping                     # what is not started, as files
todo-done /groups/users/todos/Shopping/2026-11-05-140322.md
todo-done --undo /groups/users/todos/Shopping/done/2026-11-05-140322.md
todo-set /groups/users/todos/Shopping/2026-11-05-140322.md --assignee helen --due 2026-11-20
todo-move /groups/users/todos/Shopping/2026-11-05-140322.md --before /groups/users/todos/Shopping/2026-11-04-091500.md
mkdir '/groups/users/todos/Shopping/In the basket'  # a column
todo-move /groups/users/todos/Shopping/2026-11-05-140322.md --column '/groups/users/todos/Shopping/In the basket'
todo-archive /groups/users/todos/Shopping/done/2026-11-05-140322.md
todo-add ~/todos/Fix 'Straighten the horizon' --link /albums/Summer/beach.jpg
todo-link ~/todos/Fix/2026-11-05-150210.md /albums/Summer   # another link
todo-mine                                           # what is left for you
todo-mine --done                                    # and what you have done, as Overview shows it
todo-about /albums/Summer/beach.jpg                 # what is left to do about a picture
todo-about                                          # every open item that links to anything

An item's path is its list's, then its column's folder unless it is in Todo, and then its file's name, which todo-items shows; a done item's is in the list's done/, an archived one's in archived/. todo-move --column or todo-done --undo takes an archived item out again. An item's links are under its file, …/2026-11-05-150210.md/beach.jpg, and rm of one takes the link away and leaves the picture. Once ~/todos is there, mkdir in it makes a list as well as todo-list-create does, and cat and any editor read and change an item. Remove an item, or a whole list, with trash put. Todo commands lists every option.

When something goes differently

What you see What it means What to do
Shared with offers no group None of your groups has a shared home Ask the administrator to give your group one, or share it with users
todo-add: 'peggy' is not in the group 'family' An item goes only to a member of the list's group Pick a member, or ask the administrator to add them to the group
todo-add: an item on your own list can only be yours A list of your own is nobody else's Make the list a group's to share it
A list someone made has no tab It is someone else's own, or of a group you are not in Group changes apply the next time you sign in
todo-list-create: there is already a list called … A list's name is its title, and that one is taken Pick another title, or add to the list that is there
An item's due day or person is not shown Its front matter is not YAML the module can read Open the file and correct the lines between the --- lines
An item due long ago is not on Home Home shows a week back Overview lists every item of yours, overdue or not
A folder in the list is not a column Its name starts with a dot, or it is archived Rename the folder
