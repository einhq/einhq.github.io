Automation

Automation

The Automation module brings the devices in the house that talk MQTT into EiN: sensors, plugs, switches, lights and buttons, such as those running ESPHome and Shelly's. It adds drivers that hear them on an MQTT broker and make each one a device under /dev, with its readings, presses and switches, a driver for cameras whose picture you watch live, and automations: nodes wired together, which switch things and run command lines when a reading, a press, a file coming into a folder or the clock says so. The devices, their pages and their commands are EiN's own, the same for a plug as for a phone: Devices.

This page is for the administrator who installs the module and connects the devices, and for the household that uses them. Automations covers making automations.

What it adds

Part What it is
The ha-discovery driver Anything that announces itself over MQTT the way Home Assistant reads it: ESPHome, and Zigbee2MQTT as far as it keeps to that; lights with their brightness and colour
The shelly driver Shelly's second generation and later: Plus, Pro, Gen3 and Gen4; relays, lights with their brightness and colour, and covers
The shelly-gen1 driver Shelly's first generation: Plug S, 1, 1PM, 2.5, i3, Dimmer, Bulb, Duo, RGBW2
The ipp driver A network printer, asked over IPP: its state, its warnings and how much of each ink or toner is left
The cast driver A Google Cast speaker or screen: what it plays, its volume and mute, play, pause and stop
The snmp driver A NAS, a router or anything else that answers SNMP: its uptime, load, memory, each volume's use and its temperatures
The network driver Anything else on the network: whether it answers, and how fast
The http driver A web API that answers JSON, asked at its address for the readings you name, and set at the addresses you name: the weather, the price of electricity, a device on your network with a web API of its own; see A web API
The virtual driver Devices whose readings are worked out from other devices' (an average, the most, whether any is on) and whose controls hold what is set; see Virtual devices
The rtsp driver A camera that sends its picture over RTSP: its streams, a picture of each now, and the picture live; see A camera
The onvif driver A camera that speaks ONVIF, found on the network: its streams as rtsp's, its motion and the people it sees, and its presets and moves where it turns; see An ONVIF camera
The Live tab A camera's picture as it comes, on its device page
The automation-bridge service Hears the broker and runs the drivers
The automation-engine service Runs the automations under /etc/automations, each as its owner
The group automation Its members make automations
automation-status, automations, automation-new, automation-set, automation-check Whether the broker is heard; the automations, making and changing them, and what one would do now
Found on the network The devices on the network that are not devices yet, with a button on the Devices page; see Add a device found on the network
automation-found, automation-adopt, automation-ignore What is on the network and all it said, adding it as a device, and leaving something out

With the Assistant, it also teaches the chat to read the devices and to propose switching them.

What it needs

  • An MQTT broker, the server the devices and EiN exchange their messages through, for the devices that speak MQTT: those running ESPHome, and Shelly's of every generation. The installer runs one beside EiN with --automation, in the automation sidecar; a broker you already run works as well.
  • A restreamer for cameras, which the sidecar has too: it keeps one connection to each camera, and EiN's pages and commands watch the camera through it. Without it a camera gives only its still picture, when it has one.
  • No broker for the rest: printers, Google Cast speakers, NAS boxes, web APIs and anything else on the network are asked by EiN directly, once added, with or without one. Without the installer's broker nothing is found on the network, though, so add them by hand with device-create.
  • Nothing else: neither the Archive nor the media service.

Install it

With the installer's broker, add the broker and the module in one run:

# on the machine EiN runs on, in ~/ein
./setup.sh --automation --module automation

On a new machine, name the module to the installer, which makes it the same options: curl -fsSL https://einhq.eu/install.sh | bash -s -- automation. With a broker of your own, install the module alone, from Settings → Modules or in EiN's terminal, and then give it the broker (A broker you already run):

# in EiN's terminal, as the administrator
mod install automation

The module runs two services: automation-bridge, which hears the broker and drives the devices, and automation-engine, which runs the automations. When automation-engine first starts it makes the group automation, whose members make automations. Modules covers installing and removing modules.

The installer's sidecar

--automation runs the automation sidecar, one container beside EiN on the machine's own network, automation, with three services in it:

  • The MQTT broker, Mosquitto. It takes no anonymous clients. EiN signs in as ein and the devices as device, with passwords setup.sh made at the install and keeps in ~/ein/.env: MQTT_EIN_PASSWORD and MQTT_DEVICE_PASSWORD. A password changed there takes effect at the next ./setup.sh. The devices reach it on port 1883 of the machine (MQTT_PORT in .env), on the address EIN_BIND names. It keeps what the devices announce in the data folder's mqtt folder, so it is all there again after a restart.
  • A listener on the machine's own network, which finds the devices that are not on the broker yet: Add a device found on the network.
  • A restreamer, go2rtc, which keeps one connection to each camera EiN has and serves it again, to EiN on port 1984 (STREAMS_PORT) and over RTSP on 8554 (RTSP_PORT), behind the account ein and STREAMS_PASSWORD from .env. EiN gives it the cameras itself.

setup.sh gives EiN the broker's and the restreamer's addresses and passwords itself, and the device account a device added from the network is given: those settings say From deployment on the Settings page and need nothing from you. ./setup.sh --no-automation takes the sidecar out again, and ./setup.sh --only automation updates it alone.

Under Docker, and podman run as root, EiN reaches the sidecar at the machine's address on its container network, so the sidecar needs EIN_BIND to be 0.0.0.0: setup.sh says so when it is not.

The devices need the device password. Read it on the machine:

# on the machine EiN runs on, in ~/ein
grep MQTT_DEVICE_PASSWORD .env

Warning

The broker speaks plain MQTT, without TLS. Whoever reaches port 1883 with the device password can switch the house's devices and read what they say, and whoever reaches 1984 or 8554 with the restreamer's password watches the cameras. Keep the ports on your home network and never forward them on your router; Ports, HTTPS and remote access lists it with EiN's other ports.

A broker you already run

Give EiN the broker's address and an account on it, under Settings → System in the Automation group, or with config set:

# in EiN's terminal, as the administrator
config set CFG_AUTOMATION_MQTT_URL mqtt://192.0.2.20:1883
config set CFG_AUTOMATION_MQTT_USER ein
 config set CFG_AUTOMATION_MQTT_KEY 'the password'   # the leading space keeps it out of your history

MQTT broker takes mqtt://host:1883, or mqtts://host:8883 for a broker that takes TLS. MQTT password is a key: nobody can read it back, and EiN sends it only to that broker (Give a key to another service). Saving any of the three restarts automation-bridge. While MQTT broker is empty no device is heard on a broker, and only the devices EiN asks over the network itself are read.

Connect a device

A device announces itself on the broker, and its driver makes it a device as soon as it hears it: within seconds of the device connecting it is on the Devices page with all it reads and does. Nothing is entered in EiN.

A device that is on the network but not yet on the broker is found too, and a Shelly is added from EiN without opening its own web page: Add a device found on the network. The sections after it say how to point each kind at the broker by hand.

Add a device found on the network

With the installer's broker, its listener discovery asks the network every two minutes what is there, over mDNS (Bonjour) and SSDP (UPnP), the way phones and computers find printers and speakers, and over WS-Discovery, the way cameras that speak ONVIF are found, and hears what announces itself over mDNS in between. It does not scan addresses: it hears only what announces itself. Each thing that answered is named by what it said, such as a Shelly Plus 1PM, an ESPHome board, a HomeKit or Matter accessory, a Hue bridge, a camera, a speaker, a TV or a printer, and is matched to the devices EiN has already, by its name and its hardware address.

When something found can become a device and is none yet, the Devices page's header has a button that says how many, such as 2 found. It opens Found on the network, a card for each, and Add on a card makes it a device:

What was found What Add does
A Shelly, of any generation EiN sets its MQTT to the broker, with the device account, and restarts it. It is under Devices when it is back, within a minute, with all it reads and switches.
A camera that speaks ONVIF Add asks for the account EiN signs in to the camera with, and checks it with the camera: a device of the onvif driver at once, with its streams, its motion and the people it sees, and its presets and moves where it turns (An ONVIF camera).
A printer A device of the ipp driver at once: its state (idle, printing, stopped), its warnings (such as marker-supply-low-warning) and a level in % for each ink or toner it reports.
A Google Cast speaker or screen A device of the cast driver: player says whether it plays (idle, playing, paused) and playing what, as title – artist (app); volume and muted can be set, and play, pause and stop pressed.
Anything that answers SNMP: a NAS, a router … A device of the snmp driver: uptime, load, memory-used, for each volume <volume>-used (%) and <volume>-free (GB), and a <sensor>-temperature for each temperature its agent reports. EiN asks with the community SNMP community in the Automation settings, public unless you change it.
Anything else: a NAS without SNMP, a TV … A device of the network driver, online while it answers, with its response-time. EiN does not speak its own protocol yet.
ESPHome or Tasmota No Add: the line under it says how to point it at the broker, and the sections below say more.

A device added from the network follows it to a new address: EiN knows it by its hardware address. A printer is asked every two minutes, a Cast device every 15 seconds and the rest every minute.

SNMP is often off on a NAS or a router until it is turned on in its own settings, usually read-only with the community public; a NETGEAR ReadyNAS has it under System → SNMP. One added before SNMP was on is a network device: forget it and add it again. To see what a device's agent answers beyond what the driver reads, walk it:

# in EiN's terminal, as a member of the devices group
automation-snmp 192.0.2.40                      # its name, description and uptime
automation-snmp 192.0.2.40 1.3.6.1.2.1.25.2.3   # its volumes
automation-snmp 192.0.2.40 1.3.6.1.4.1          # what its maker adds

Choose a card to see all the listener heard of it: what EiN makes of it, each mDNS service with its records, each SSDP answer with what the device's own description says, and the raw message, as it came. For something EiN does not support, it is what there is to go on. Ignore leaves one out of the count and the list; Show Everything finds it again, with Take back. Open opens one that is a device already.

Show chooses Can be added (the default), Not a device yet, or Everything the listener heard, devices included. The page and the button are for members of the devices group, who make devices; the button stays away for everyone else.

Note

A Shelly with a password of its own refuses EiN; turn the password off on its web page, or set its MQTT there as below. A first-generation Shelly does not use Shelly's cloud while MQTT is on.

The same from the terminal:

# in EiN's terminal, as a member of the devices group
automation-found                # what can become a device, and is none yet
automation-found --all          # everything the listener heard
automation-found 7c2ebd614055   # one, with all the listener heard of it
automation-adopt a8032ab12345   # add the Shelly with this id, as automation-found says it
automation-adopt 7c2ebd614055   # add the Cast speaker
 automation-adopt ec71db0a1b2c --user admin --password 'the password'   # add the camera; the leading space keeps it out of your history
echo 30 > /dev/kitchen-speaker/volume
echo press > /dev/kitchen-speaker/pause
automation-ignore d0ad08c5e709  # leave the printer out

The listener needs the machine EiN runs on to be on the same network as the devices: mDNS, SSDP and WS-Discovery stay on the network they are sent on. A device on another network, or another VLAN, is not found, and is connected by hand as below. Broker for devices in the Automation settings is the address a device added from the network is given; empty, it is the address the listener says the machine has on the devices' network. With a broker of your own there is no listener: the page says so, and devices are connected by hand.

A device is named in EiN as it names itself, in lower case with hyphens, such as hall-sensor, and each of its endpoints by its own name within it, such as hall-sensor/temperature. Commands, routing keys and rules use these names. Edit on a device's page gives it and its endpoints friendlier labels on the pages and leaves the names as they are.

ESPHome

Add an mqtt: block to the device's configuration, with the machine EiN runs on as the broker, and install the configuration on the device as usual:

# hall-sensor.yaml, the device's ESPHome configuration
esphome:
  name: hall-sensor

mqtt:
  broker: 192.0.2.10 # the machine EiN runs on
  username: device
  password: !secret mqtt_password # in secrets.yaml: MQTT_DEVICE_PASSWORD from ~/ein/.env

With an mqtt: block ESPHome announces each of its entities the way Home Assistant's MQTT discovery reads them, which is on unless you turn it off. Leave its prefix as it is, homeassistant (Discovery prefix in the settings). The device is named by its name:, and an endpoint by its entity's own name: a sensor called Temperature on the device above is hall-sensor/temperature.

A light is on or off, and has its brightness and its colour beside it when it has them, each to be set: <light>-brightness, 0–100 % whatever scale the light counts in, and <light>-color, as #rrggbb, such as #ff8000; 255,128,0 is taken too. A light that takes its colour as hue and saturation, or as x and y, as many Zigbee lights do, is read and set in them.

# in EiN's terminal, as a member of the devices group
echo on > /dev/living-room/ceiling
echo 40 > /dev/living-room/ceiling-brightness
echo '#ff8000' > /dev/living-room/ceiling-color

Other software that announces devices the same way, such as Zigbee2MQTT, is read as far as it keeps to what ESPHome sends; automation-status names what it could not read.

Shelly, second generation and later

Shelly's Plus, Pro, Gen3 and Gen4 devices are read by the shelly driver.

  1. Open the Shelly's own web page, at its address on your network, and go to Settings → MQTT.
  2. Turn MQTT on. Set the server to the machine EiN runs on with the port, such as 192.0.2.10:1883, the user to device, and the password to the device password.
  3. Turn on Generic status update over MQTT and RPC status notifications over MQTT.
  4. Save. The Shelly restarts and connects.

The topic prefix may stay as it is, the Shelly's id, such as shellyplus1pm-a8032ab12345. It is the device's name in EiN, so a short one of your own, such as hall-plug, makes commands and rules easier to read. Choose it before the Shelly is first heard: a new prefix is a new device.

Its endpoints are named after its parts: switch-0 is its first relay, switch-0-power, switch-0-energy, switch-0-voltage and switch-0-current what the relay measures, and input-0 its first input. An input set to button mode gives presses, single, double, triple or long, rather than on and off.

A light is light-0, or rgb-0, rgbw-0 or cct-0 for a colour or white-tone one such as the Plus RGBW PM, and each can be set:

Endpoint What it is
light-0 On or off
light-0-brightness Its brightness, 0–100 %
rgb-0-color Its colour, as #rrggbb, such as #ff8000; 255,128,0 is taken too
rgbw-0-white Its white channel, 0–255
cct-0-color-temperature Its white's colour temperature, in K

A cover, such as a Plus 2PM in cover mode, has cover-0-position (0–100 %, set to move it there), cover-0-state (open, closed, opening, closing or stopped), and cover-0-open, cover-0-close and cover-0-stop to press:

# in EiN's terminal
echo 40 > /dev/desk-lamp/rgbw-0-brightness
echo '#ff8000' > /dev/desk-lamp/rgbw-0-color
echo 30 > /dev/kitchen-blind/cover-0-position
echo press > /dev/kitchen-blind/cover-0-stop

Shelly, first generation

The first generation, such as the Plug S, the 1, the 1PM, the 2.5, the i3, the Dimmer, the Bulb, the Duo and the RGBW2, is read by the shelly-gen1 driver.

  1. Open the Shelly's own web page, at its address on your network, and go to Internet & Security → Advanced – Developer Settings.
  2. Turn MQTT on. Set the server to the machine EiN runs on with the port, such as 192.0.2.10:1883, the user to device, and the password to the device password. Leave its custom MQTT prefix off: the driver listens under shellies/.
  3. Save, and restart the Shelly: a first-generation Shelly takes its MQTT settings when it starts. With MQTT on it does not use Shelly's cloud.

It is named by its own id, such as shellyplug-s-c8c9a3, which cannot be changed; give it a label with Rename. Its endpoints are relay-0 for its first relay, relay-0-power and relay-0-energy (the energy since it last started, in Wh) for what the relay measures, temperature for its own, and input-0 for a button's presses: single, double, triple, long, single-long or long-single. A Shelly the bridge has not heard announce itself, because it connected before the bridge started, is asked to.

A light is light-0, and an RGBW2 in white mode has light-0 to light-3, one for each channel. A light has as many of these as it reports, each of which can be set: light-0-brightness (0–100 %), light-0-color (#rrggbb), light-0-white (an RGBW2's white channel, 0–255) and light-0-color-temperature (in K). A light that has both modes, such as a Color Bulb, turns to colour mode when set to a colour, and to white when set to a colour temperature; its brightness is that of the mode it is in. For white light from a Color Bulb, set its colour temperature: it has no white channel to mix with a colour. light-0-power and light-0-energy are what a light that measures it draws, as for a relay. A 2.5 in roller mode has roller-0-state (open, close or stop, the way it last moved), roller-0-position (0–100 %, empty until the roller is calibrated), and roller-0-open, roller-0-close and roller-0-stop to press.

A web API

The http driver asks an address that answers JSON, as often as you say, and keeps the readings you name in its answer: today's weather, the price of electricity this hour, or a device on your network that answers on a web API of its own, and can be switched there. Nothing announces such a device; a member of devices makes it in the terminal:

# in EiN's terminal, as a member of devices
device-create Weather --driver http \
  --config 'url=https://api.open-meteo.com/v1/forecast?latitude=57.7&longitude=11.97&current=temperature_2m,wind_speed_10m' \
  --config 'read=temperature=current.temperature_2m °C; wind=current.wind_speed_10m m/s' \
  --config interval=15m
Config What it is
url The address to ask. {date} (2026-11-09), {year}, {month}, {day} and {hour} in it become the day and hour it is asked at, in EiN's time zone. A user and password in it, http://admin:secret@192.0.2.20/status, are sent as the device's sign-in.
read The readings, name=path unit, separated by ;. Each becomes an endpoint of that name; the unit may be left out.
interval How often it is asked: seconds, or 30s, 15m, 1h. Five minutes unless given, and ten seconds at the least.
set The controls, name=address on/off, separated by ;; see Switching it. May be left out.

A path walks the answer from the top:

Path Reads
current.temperature_2m a field of a field
relay:0.power a field whose name has a colon in it
energy['total today'] a field whose name has a space in it
meters[0].power, items[-1].value the first item of a list, the last
[time_start..time_end].SEK_per_kWh the item of a list whose time_start has come and whose time_end has not: this hour's price
[from..].price the last item whose from has come, for a list that gives only when each starts
. the whole answer, for an address that answers a number or a word as plain text

A number written as text, "0.42", is kept as a number; a list or an object is not kept. A device whose answer has none of its readings, or that does not answer, is offline until it does, and the bridge's log says why once. A reading's unit says what it is: °C is a temperature, W power and kWh energy, so the device's card shows it first.

Another:

# the price of electricity this hour, in Swedish price area SE3, from elprisetjustnu.se
device-create Electricity price --driver http \
  --config 'url=https://www.elprisetjustnu.se/api/v1/prices/{year}/{month}-{day}_SE3.json' \
  --config 'read=price=[time_start..time_end].SEK_per_kWh SEK/kWh' --config interval=5m

A config device-create does not understand is refused with what is wrong, such as a reading is name=path [unit], not 'price'; the device is made all the same, so change its config, on its Info tab or with device-config, or forget it and make it again:

device-config electricity-price 'read=price=[time_start..time_end].SEK_per_kWh SEK/kWh'

Switching it

A device whose web API also takes commands is switched at an address of its own, which set names for each control: name=address on/off. {value} in the address becomes the value, spelled as the device spells on and off, which is what on/off says: ON/OFF, on/off, 1/0 or true/false. An address without http:// is taken from url's, sign-in included. A control whose name is also a reading's is a switch that shows that reading as its state, read as on or off however the device spells them; EiN asks the device again a second after it is switched. A control with no {value} and no on/off is a button that asks its address when pressed, such as a restart.

Say a garden pump's controller on your network answers {"pump": {"running": true, "power": 420}} at /api/status, runs or stops the pump at /api/pump?run=1 and /api/pump?run=0, and restarts at /api/restart:

device-create Garden pump --driver http --config 'url=http://admin:secret@192.0.2.40/api/status' \
  --config 'read=pump=pump.running; power=pump.power W' \
  --config 'set=pump=api/pump?run={value} 1/0; restart=api/restart' --config interval=30s

pump is then a switch, on while the answer says running is true, and restart a button; both are asked with the sign-in in url.

The address, with a password in it, is the device's config, which every member of devices sees. EiN asks it from the machine it runs on, so an address on your home network is asked from there.

A camera

The rtsp driver takes a camera on your network that sends its picture over RTSP, as most cameras that work without a cloud do (Reolink, Hikvision, Dahua, Amcrest, Axis, Tapo, …). The camera's manual, or its own web page, names its stream's address; many send a second, smaller stream beside the main one, which costs less to watch and to take a picture of. A member of devices makes the camera in the terminal:

# in EiN's terminal, as a member of devices
 device-create 'Front door' --driver rtsp \
  --config url=rtsp://192.0.2.30:554/h264Preview_01_main \
  --config sub=rtsp://192.0.2.30:554/h264Preview_01_sub \
  --config user=viewer --config password='the password'   # the leading space keeps it out of your history
Config What it is
url The main stream's address, rtsp://…
sub A second, smaller stream's address. May be left out
snapshot The address of the camera's still picture, http://…, which a picture is taken from when there is no restreamer. May be left out
user The camera's account
password That account's password. It is kept in the device's .secret, which the administrator alone reads, and goes only to the sidecar

A password or an address that changed is given again on the camera's Info tab, with Change, or in the terminal, and the camera is reached with it at once:

device-config front-door password='the new password'

The camera becomes a device of class camera, with a stream endpoint for each stream: main, and sub with a second one. EiN gives the streams to the sidecar's restreamer, which keeps one connection to the camera however many watch it, and says within a minute how large each picture is and which codec it is sent in. Its page shows a picture of each stream now, taken again every ten seconds while the page is open, and the Live tab plays the picture as it comes: the main stream, or the smaller one, a button away. The Devices page shows a picture of the smaller stream on the camera's card.

A picture is a file too, a JPEG of the stream now, taken each time it is read:

# in EiN's terminal
cat /dev/front-door/main > ~/front-door.jpg

With the Archive, the media service also watches each camera's smaller stream for faces: the camera gets a Faces log, which says who each face that came into view was, or unknown, within a second or so, as dev.<camera>.faces for automations, and the faces' pictures are filed under the camera in the archive, where they are named as photos' faces are (Faces a camera sees). The media service records the camera's largest stream as well, and a clip of each visit, from a little before the face until a little after, is filed beside them as a video (A security camera).

The camera is online while it gives a picture, asked every other minute. Who may see the pictures is who may read the stream: the household, as for every device (Who may do what). EiN's pages reach the camera only through EiN, signed in, so neither its address nor its password reaches a browser.

An ONVIF camera

Most cameras that work without a cloud also speak ONVIF, a standard for asking a camera what it sends, what it sees and how it turns. Such a camera needs no stream address: EiN asks the camera for it. It is found on the network, where Add on its card asks for the account EiN signs in to it with (Add a device found on the network). Many cameras have ONVIF off, or want an account of its own for it, until it is set on their web page, among the advanced network settings: on a Hikvision under Network → Advanced Settings → Integration Protocol. A camera on another network is made in the terminal:

# in EiN's terminal, as a member of devices
 device-create 'Back yard' --driver onvif \
  --config address=192.0.2.31 \
  --config user=admin --config password='the password'   # the leading space keeps it out of your history
Config What it is
address The camera's address, 192.0.2.31 or 192.0.2.31:8000, or its ONVIF service's whole address, http://192.0.2.31/onvif/device_service
user The camera's account
password That account's password, kept in the device's .secret as rtsp's is. The camera is sent a digest of it, never the password itself

The camera's largest picture becomes its main stream and its smallest its sub, with the codec, size and frame rate the camera says, and everything else about them is as for A camera. EiN asks the camera again every hour, and whenever it is not reached, so a stream the camera moved is followed. One added from the network is asked at the address the listener last heard it at, so it is followed to a new address too.

Beside its streams, a camera has what it tells and what it turns:

Endpoint What it is
motion On while the camera sees motion, off when it stops, as the camera itself detects it
person On while the camera sees a person, for a camera that tells people from the rest
ptz-preset One of the positions saved on the camera: setting it turns the camera there. For a camera that turns and has positions saved
ptz-left, ptz-right, ptz-up, ptz-down, ptz-zoom-in, ptz-zoom-out A button each: a press turns or zooms the camera a short step. For a camera that turns, and zooms

motion and person are readings like any other, so an automation takes them: a person in the back yard at night can turn on its light, or turn the camera to the gate.

# in EiN's terminal
echo Gate > /dev/back-yard/ptz-preset    # turn to the position saved as Gate
echo press > /dev/back-yard/ptz-left     # a step to the left

Check that EiN hears them

# in EiN's terminal
automation-status
What it says What it means
5 devices, 31 endpoints, 18420 messages The bridge is connected: the devices its drivers drive, their endpoints, and the messages it has had since it started.
drivers: ha-discovery, shelly, shelly-gen1 The drivers it runs, as Device sources names them.
not read: <device>/<endpoint>: <why> An endpoint whose values the bridge cannot read, such as one announced with a template it does not understand. Nothing of it is kept.
automations: 4 running, 41 actions The automation engine runs, with how many automations and the actions they took; see Automations.
…; no broker set, so only … MQTT broker is empty: the devices EiN asks over the network are read, and none on a broker. Install the broker or give EiN yours for those.
cannot reach mqtt://…: <why> The broker does not answer, or refuses EiN's account. The bridge tries again by itself, waiting a second, then twice as long each time, up to a minute.
not running The automation-bridge service is stopped: service start automation-bridge, as the administrator.

The Status page says the same: its automation bridge check is fine while the bridge is connected, or runs without a broker, a warning while it cannot reach the broker, and failed while its service is stopped (Health and status). devices --drivers lists the drivers with their devices while the bridge runs.

What a device becomes

Each thing a device announces becomes an endpoint of the device under /dev:

The device announces Endpoint Takes
A reading: a temperature, power, humidity, a word a log
Something on or off it only reads: a door, motion, input a log
A button a log of events, its presses
A switch, a relay, a light a control, on or off on, off or toggle
A number it takes, such as a thermostat's target a control, a number a number within its limits and steps
A choice it takes, such as a mode a control, one of its options one of its options
Something it does when asked, such as a restart a control, pressed press

The device's class comes from the endpoint that says the most of what it is, whichever is heard first: what it switches says the most (plug for an outlet, light), then what it senses or a button (sensor, button), and a bare relay least (switch); its power, energy, voltage or current says nothing. A class the device has is replaced only by one that says more, and one that is none of these, such as heater set by a person, stays. What tells of the device itself, such as its own temperature or its Wi-Fi signal, is marked diagnostic and kept apart on its page. An endpoint the device no longer announces is marked retired, and its history stays. Devices shows how the page draws each.

Switch things

The toggles on a device's Overview switch it, and so does writing to the control in the terminal:

# in EiN's terminal
echo on > /dev/hall-plug/switch-0
echo toggle > /dev/hall-plug/switch-0
echo 21 > /dev/heater/target

The command goes to the device through the broker at once, and the switch shows the new state once the device reports it. When the bridge is not connected, the write is refused with hall-plug/switch-0: not connected to the broker, so nothing was sent, and while its service is stopped with hall-plug's driver shelly is not running: a command is never kept for later, since a lamp switched on an hour after it was asked would surprise. Switch something has the rest.

Readings on the bus

Every reading, press and change of state is kept in its endpoint and published on the routing key dev.<device>.<endpoint>, and a device coming or going on dev.<device>, where the automation engine reads them and a queue or a log the administrator binds can take them:

# in EiN's terminal, as the administrator
mkqueue -k 'dev.porch-button.#' ~/presses
mklog -k 'dev.*.temperature' -r '90 day' ~/temperatures

A number is kept and published at most once every 10 seconds for each endpoint (Seconds between readings); Readings on the bus shows the messages, and How often a reading is kept the rest.

Who may do what

Who What they may do
Members of the devices group See every device, its readings and its history; switch and set devices; share, rename and forget them
Someone a device is shared with See that device, and switch it when it is shared to edit
Members of the automation group Also make automations, which act with their own rights
The administrator Everything: add people to either group, and bind queues and logs to the devices' keys

The devices the drivers find belong to the administrator and the devices group, and nobody else reads them, a door or motion sensor included, until a member shares one. Devices and their keys shows how to share a device and keep a switch to some people.

Ask the assistant

With the Assistant installed, the chat reads the devices: ask "how warm is it in the hall?", "which devices are offline?" or "how much did the heater draw yesterday?", and it answers at once, with a chart of a reading's history when you ask how it went. Asking it to switch something, "turn on the hall lamp", gives a proposal card with the echo line, which you run or decline.

From the terminal

The devices' own commands, devices, device-show, device-history, device-label and device-forget, are EiN's; Devices lists them. The module adds:

# in EiN's terminal
automation-status                     # the broker, the drivers, the devices they drive, and the automations
automations                           # the automations, and why one does not run
automation-check hall-light           # what an automation's nodes would say now

Automation commands lists every option.

Settings

The module's settings are in the Automation group of Settings → System, and each restarts automation-bridge when saved:

Setting On the Settings page Default What it does
CFG_AUTOMATION_MQTT_URL MQTT broker empty; --automation sets it The broker, as mqtt://host:1883 or mqtts://host:8883. Empty, only the devices EiN asks over the network are read
CFG_AUTOMATION_MQTT_USER MQTT user empty; --automation sets ein The account EiN signs in to the broker with. Empty, it signs in without one
CFG_AUTOMATION_MQTT_KEY MQTT password none; --automation sets it That account's password, a key nobody can read back
CFG_AUTOMATION_STREAMS_URL Restreamer empty; --automation sets it The restreamer cameras are given to, go2rtc's API as http://host:1984. Empty, a camera has no live view, and its picture is asked of the camera itself
CFG_AUTOMATION_STREAMS_KEY Restreamer password none; --automation sets it The password of the restreamer's account ein, a key nobody can read back
CFG_AUTOMATION_STREAMS_RTSP Restreamer for the media service empty; --automation sets it The restreamer's RTSP as the media service reaches it, rtsp://host:8554. Empty, no camera is watched for faces
CFG_AUTOMATION_SOURCES Device sources ha-discovery shelly shelly-gen1 Which drivers the bridge runs, separated by spaces: ha-discovery for ESPHome and whatever announces itself as it does, shelly for Shelly's second generation and later, and shelly-gen1 for the first
CFG_AUTOMATION_DISCOVERY_PREFIX Discovery prefix homeassistant The topic ha-discovery devices announce themselves under
CFG_AUTOMATION_HISTORY Reading history 365 day How long an endpoint's values are kept: 30 day, 90 day, 365 day or forever. It applies to endpoints found from then on; one found before keeps its own, which attrs on the endpoint changes (below)
CFG_AUTOMATION_MIN_INTERVAL Seconds between readings 10 The shortest time between two kept readings of one number, given to endpoints found from then on as their minInterval, which may be changed on each

An endpoint found before keeps its retention, and each endpoint can take its own interval, which counts within a minute:

# in EiN's terminal, as the administrator
attrs -s 'retention=90 day' /dev/hall-sensor/temperature
attrs -J minInterval=60 /dev/heater-plug/switch-0-power

When something goes differently

What you see What it means What to do
No devices yet on the Devices page No device has announced itself, or EiN does not hear the broker Run automation-status, and check the device's own broker settings
A device never shows up, while others do It cannot reach the broker, or the broker refuses its account Check its server, user and password. ESPHome's log says whether MQTT connected. Open port 1883 in the machine's firewall (network)
A Shelly shows up without readings Its status updates are off On a second-generation Shelly, turn on Generic status update over MQTT and RPC status notifications over MQTT
A first-generation Shelly never shows up It has not restarted since MQTT was turned on, or uses a custom prefix Restart it, and turn its custom MQTT prefix off
A Shelly is there twice, under two names Its topic prefix changed, and a new prefix is a new device Forget the old one
An endpoint is marked not read The device announced it in a way the bridge does not understand automation-status says why. Its readings are not kept
… takes on, off or toggle, not … when switching The value is not one the control takes See What a device becomes
… not connected to the broker, so nothing was sent EiN does not hear the broker just now Run automation-status
<device>'s driver shelly is not running The automation-bridge service is stopped service start automation-bridge, as the administrator
Access denied for operation 'w' on node: … when switching The control is narrowed to others Ask the administrator
A device stays offline It said it was going, or the broker said so for it when it disappeared Check its power and its Wi-Fi
A camera shows No picture, and stays offline The restreamer cannot reach the camera, or the camera refuses it Check url, user and the password, and change them on its Info tab or with device-config. service logs automation-bridge says what the camera answered
Adding a camera says the camera refused admin's password The account is not the camera's ONVIF account, or ONVIF is off on the camera Turn ONVIF on in the camera's web page and give it an account there; some cameras keep ONVIF's accounts apart from the web page's
A camera is not found on the network ONVIF is off on the camera, or it is on another network Turn ONVIF on in its web page, or make it in the terminal (An ONVIF camera)
The Live tab says the stream could not be reached As above, or the sidecar is not running podman compose ps on the machine; ./setup.sh --only automation starts it again
Live plays nothing in a browser on a phone The browser does not play the camera's codec, often H.265 Watch the smaller stream, which is often H.264, or set the camera to send H.264
