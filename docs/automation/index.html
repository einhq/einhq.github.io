Automation

Automation

The Automation module makes the house do things by itself. An automation is nodes wired together, which switch things and run command lines when a reading, a press, a device coming or going, a file coming into a folder or the clock says so: a lamp on at motion when it is dark, a heater between two temperatures, a scan filed as it lands in a folder. Each is a YAML file, run with the rights of the person whose it is. It acts on whatever is under /dev, so the devices, their pages and their commands are EiN's own: Devices.

This page is for the administrator who installs the module and lets people make automations. Automations covers making them.

What it adds

Part What it is
The Automations page Under System in the sidebar: each automation drawn as its nodes wired together, to make, change, simulate and follow as it runs; see The Automations page
automation/tour The module's own automation, off: nine small flows with every kind of node, to simulate or start one of your own from; see A tour of every kind of node
The virtual driver Devices whose readings are worked out from other devices' (an average, the most, whether any is on) and whose controls hold what is set; see Virtual devices
The automation-engine service Runs the automations under /etc/automations and /etc/automations.d, each as its owner, and the virtual devices
The group automation Its members make automations
automations, automation-new, automation-set, automation-show, automation-check, automation-enable, automation-disable, automation-trigger The automations, making and changing them, what one would do now, turning one on and off, and pressing its button
automation-status Whether the engine runs, how many automations it runs, and the ones it holds back

With the Assistant, it also teaches the chat to make automations.

What it needs

Nothing but EiN: no service beside it and no other module. An automation acts on whatever is under /dev:

  • EiN's own devices: a phone or a GPS tracker, a device that posts to a webhook, and the machine EiN runs on (Core's host driver).
  • The Devices module's, when it is installed: sensors, plugs, lights and buttons over MQTT, printers, speakers and screens on the network, web APIs and cameras.
  • The virtual devices this module adds, worked out from the others.

An automation that only reacts to the clock, to files coming into a folder or to EiN's events needs no device at all.

Install it

Install it from Settings → Modules, or in EiN's terminal:

# in EiN's terminal, as the administrator
mod install automation

At a new install, name it to the installer, curl -fsSL https://einhq.eu/install.sh | bash -s -- automation, or give --module automation to setup.sh. Automations is then under System in the sidebar, for members of the automation group. Modules covers installing and removing modules.

The module runs one service, automation-engine. When it first starts it makes the group automation, whose members make automations. Nobody is in it to begin with: add the people who make automations, in Settings → Groups or in the terminal.

# in EiN's terminal, as the administrator
group-add-member automation helen

A person added signs in again before the group applies to them.

Check that the engine runs

# in EiN's terminal
automation-status
What it says What it means
4 running, 41 actions The engine runs, with how many automations it runs and the actions they have taken since it started.
not run: hall-light: <why> An automation the engine holds back, with its first problem; When an automation does not run says what to do.
not running The automation-engine service is stopped: service start automation-engine, as the administrator. Virtual devices are offline meanwhile.

automation-status --brief is the line the automation-engine service has on the Status page's Services tab, and automation-status hall-light says what each node of a running automation last sent (See what automations did).

Who may do what

Who What they may do
Members of the automation group Make automations, which act with their owner's rights and nothing more: they hear and switch only the devices their owner may read and switch
Members of the devices group Make virtual devices, as they make every device
The administrator Everything: add people to the group, turn a module's automations on and off, and raise EiN's events from an automation

Whose automation it is has the rest, and Devices and their keys how to share a device and keep a switch to some people.

Ask the assistant

With the Assistant installed, asking for an automation, "turn the hall lamp on when there is motion after dark", gives a proposal card. The assistant looks up the devices you name and makes the automation from the starting point it fits, with your devices set. One that fits no starting point is proposed as an automation-set line with its YAML. Either line is tried with --dry-run before you see it, and one with something wrong goes back to the assistant to correct, so the card you get is one that can run. It also answers what an automation does and whether it runs.

From the terminal

# in EiN's terminal
automations                           # the automations, and why one does not run
automation-new hall-light --from motion-light   # one from a starting point
automation-check hall-light           # what an automation's nodes would say now
automation-status                     # whether the engine runs, and what it holds back

Automation commands lists every option.
