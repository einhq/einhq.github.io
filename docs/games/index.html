Games

Games

The Games module gives EiN a Games page: a card for each game you can play in the browser, on the computer you sit at, with the keyboard and the mouse. Your saved games and settings are kept in your home, so you go on where you left off on any computer, and a game can be played together, by the people here and by someone you invite from another EiN. This page is for everyone who plays, and for the administrator who sets the games up.

The games themselves come from game modules. Games names none of them: each game module brings the engine that plays its games and the commands that fetch and list them, and Games gives every one of them the same page, the same saves and the same way of playing together.

Install it

Game modules are made by others than EiN, and are not among EiN's published modules: install one from its repository's address, as Modules from other places describes, or by name once its organisation is one of your Module sources:

# in EiN's terminal, as the administrator
mod install https://forge.example/someone/module-rover

A game module needs Games, and mod install installs Games along with it. You can also install Games by itself, mod install games, but its page then says that no game module is installed. Modules covers installing, updating and removing modules.

Games is then in the sidebar, under Browse, for everyone who is signed in.

The Games page

Games shows a card for each installed game, with its picture and a line on what it is, such as its size. A card opens the game's own page. With more than one game module installed, each card also names the module its game is from. Type in Filter games… to narrow the cards by title.

Under Free downloads are the games a game module offers but that are not here yet, such as a free demo or a game whose makers gave it away, each with what it is. Only an administrator downloads one, since it comes from the internet: Download on its card, where an installed game's picture is, which also says how much it downloads. Everyone else sees "An administrator can download it." Once it is downloaded, it moves up among the installed games.

To the administrator, the page also lists at its foot what the game modules could not answer, such as a game module whose list of games failed.

Play a game

Choose a game's card. Its page has the game's picture and title in the header, with Games › above the title to go back. Under the title are what the game is, for a game its module knows, and how it is played: its keys, what a click does now, and that F11 toggles fullscreen. Below is the game's frame, its cover whole and dimmed behind the play button, at the game's shape, until the game runs and again after it ends. Choose the play button to start it.

  • The keyboard: while the game has the keyboard, it takes every key it uses, before EiN does, so EiN's own shortcuts wait until you leave the game. A key held down with ⌘ (Meta) stays the browser's. Click the game to give it the keyboard back, after clicking elsewhere on the page.
  • The mouse: in a game played with the mouse, a click on the game gives it the mouse: the pointer disappears, and moving the mouse turns or aims. Escape gives the mouse back. In a game that says when its menu is up, the mouse is yours while the game's menu or console is up and the game's again when you close it, and the Escape that gives the mouse back opens the menu too. If the browser does not hand the mouse back as the menu closes (Chrome does not after you took the mouse back with Escape), click the game. The line under the title says which is the case.
  • Fullscreen: F11, or the button in the header, shows the running game on the whole screen, and again leaves it; a game may offer it in its own video menu as well. A game always starts in the page, since a browser goes fullscreen only when you press a key or click. In fullscreen the game keeps the mouse, its menus up or not, and Escape is the game's alone: turn fullscreen off, press F11, or quit the game to leave. That takes Chrome or Edge, with EiN's address starting https:// (or on the computer EiN runs on); holding Escape down still leaves fullscreen there. Elsewhere, and in Firefox and Safari, Escape leaves fullscreen, and the game carries on in the page with its menu open.
  • Sound off and Sound on, the loudspeaker in the header, turn the game's sound off and on. Your browser remembers it, for every game.
  • Quit from the game's own menu ends the game, and the page shows the play button again.

A game stands still while its page is hidden, such as in another tab, and its sound waits with it. Leaving the page stops the game. When a game stops by itself with an error, the frame says so, with the last lines the game wrote, which often say why, and Play again.

Start on a map

Some games can start on any of their maps, rather than at their own beginning. Their page has a Play on a map box in the header: type part of the map's name, such as arena, and choose arena3 to start the game there.

Saved games and settings

When you are signed in, what the game writes for you, its saved games and its settings, is kept in your home, in a folder of each game's: ~/.rover/classic for the game classic of a game module named rover. The next time you play the game, on any computer, it reads them from there, and its own menus list your saved games. Nobody else sees them, and each game has its own, so a save is only ever loaded into the game it was made in. A game module may keep your settings once for all its games, in ~/.rover itself, so your key bindings are the same whichever of its games you play. Without an account nothing is kept beyond the page.

Delete a game

Delete game under ⋮ in the header removes the game's files from EiN, for everyone who plays here, after asking. What players keep of it, their saves, stays in their homes. Deleting it needs the right to remove its files, which an administrator has.

Add games and maps by dropping them

Drop a game's files or folder anywhere on the Games page to add it, and drop a map to add it to a game you have. While you drag files over the page it says "Drop games and maps to add them".

Each game module says which files are its own: by their names, such as a name ending in .rvr; for a folder, by a file at its top; and for a file, by the bytes it starts with. The page tries every thing you drop, a file or a folder, against each module's rules, without uploading anything first:

  • When exactly one place takes it, it is uploaded there: a game's files into the game module's folder, a map into the maps/ folder of the game it is for.
  • When more than one place could take it, such as a map that two of your games can play, or when no game module recognises it, a dialog, Where do they go?, asks for each such thing where it goes. A recognised thing has the first place chosen already; one nobody recognised can go into any game module's folder or into any installed game. Leave it out uploads it nowhere. Upload sends the rest.

What is uploaded goes through EiN's uploads, as any upload does, and once it has landed, a notice says what went where, such as "Uploaded level9.map, a Star Rover map, into Star Rover › maps", and the cards are read again. Nothing starts by itself: a new game is a new card, and a new map is played from its game's page, with Play on a map or as the map to host a game on.

Play together

A game that can be played together, one whose module plays together and that has maps, has more buttons in its header: Start server, Invite and Connect.

Start a server here

Start server has EiN run the game on the server for others to join. Choose the Map, how it is played, such as Deathmatch or Cooperative, when the game has more than one way, and how many Players it has room for. Then Start server. It takes a moment to start; you need to be signed in.

While it runs, the play button says whose server it is and who plays: "On anna's server, arena3, deathmatch, 1/4 playing". The play button then enters the game on that server, for you and for everyone else here who opens the game's page. A player who reloads the page comes back as the same player.

The server runs until it is stopped:

  • Stop server in the header stops it, for the one who started it and for an administrator, after asking: everyone in it is dropped.
  • When nobody has played on it for five minutes, it stops by itself.
  • It stops when the service that runs it stops.

When the server stops while you play, the frame says that the game on the server ended.

Invite someone from another EiN

While a server of the game runs here, Invite gives a link for someone on another EiN, such as a friend's. Copy it and send it to them; it lets them in until the time the dialog gives, twelve hours from now. They need the same game on their EiN, with the map the server plays: they play with their own copy of the game there, and their browser plays in the server here.

Connect to a game on another EiN

When someone on another EiN invites you, open the game's page here and choose Connect, paste the link and choose Connect. The link says which game and map it is for. If the game is another of your games of the same kind that has the map, its page opens instead. The play button then says "On the server at" their EiN's address, and enters their game. Disconnect goes back to playing here.

Connect is there while no server of the game runs here; while one does, the play button enters that one.

The services that run them

A hosted game is run by a service, which runs while its module is installed:

  • A game module whose games play against a server brings that server as a service of its own, with a name of its own.
  • For a game module whose players play directly with one another, Games' own service, games-relay, runs the game: it runs none of the game itself, and passes each player's moves to the others.

When the service is not running, Start server says so, and nobody can host its games until an administrator starts it, on the Status page's Services tab or in the terminal:

# in EiN's terminal, as the administrator
service start games-relay

Services and logs covers services, and the lines each service writes about the games it runs, who sat down and who left, are in its log.

On a dashboard

A game module can offer Dashboards a card of its own that plays one of its games' demos, as the game does when nobody plays: silent, without keys or mouse, one demo after the other. Add it as any card; it is listed under the game module.

From the terminal

The same is there in EiN's terminal:

games-list                                # the game modules and their games
games-host rover classic arena3 --max 4 --mode deathmatch
# k3x9
games-servers                             # the games hosted, and who plays in each
games-invite k3x9 --hours 2               # an invitation's link, valid two hours
games-stop k3x9

games-host answers the hosted game's id once it runs, and games-servers lists each with its module, game, map, mode, players and state. games-invite answers the link's path on this EiN's address: put the address in front of it before you send it, such as https://ein.example/__invite/…#rover::classic:arena3. Downloading a game, and its game module's own commands, are the game module's.
