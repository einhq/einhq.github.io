Using EiN

Family tree, events and homes

This page shows how to record how the people in your archive are related, what happened in their lives and where they lived, and what EiN makes of it: the family tree, the timelines, and the birthdays and anniversaries on the Home page. It is for the family members who keep these records, and it says which parts need an administrator.

Who may change what

Everything on this page hangs off a person's page or a location's page. In the web interface, two of the controls are shown only to an administrator:

What Where Who sees the controls
Parents, partners and children the person's Family tab administrators only
A person's homes the person's Homes tab administrators only
A location's residents the location's Residents tab anyone who may change the location
Events of a person ⋮ → Add event on the person anyone who may change the person
Born and died Edit on the person anyone who may change the person
Events of an album, a location or a device ⋮ → Add event on its page anyone who may change it

An administrator is someone signed in as admin, or a member of the sudo group who has chosen Administer… in the account menu and entered their password again. See Accounts and groups. Who may change a person or a location is a matter of its sharing; see Sharing.

Everyone else reads the tree, the homes and the timelines like any other page, and sees no ⋯ menus on the tree's cards and no Add home button.

Note

The administrator rule belongs to the web interface only. The same changes are ordinary commands in the terminal, and the assistant can propose parents, partners and events for you; there they need permission to change the people they touch, not administrator rights. See In the terminal.

The family tree

Open a person and choose the Family tab. The address ends in ?tab=family, so you can link to someone's tree directly.

The tree is drawn the way family trees usually are:

  • Generations are rows. Parents and grandparents are above, children and grandchildren below.
  • Couples sit side by side, joined by a line. When the partnership has dates, the years are written over the line: 1956–2014, or 1983 when it has no end.
  • Each card shows the person's picture, their name and their years: 1934 – 2021, b. 1958 for someone with only a birth date, d. 2011 for someone with only a death date.
  • The person the page is about has an outlined card and a bold name.
  • Click a name to open that person's page.

The tree shows only people you may see. Someone whose page is hidden from you is left out, with the lines to them.

Show more of the family

A large family would not fit on a screen, so the tree opens with two generations up and two down from the person, and the partners of everyone drawn. A card with more family behind it has a round chevron on its edge: at the top when there are more parents ("Show more family for <name>"), at the bottom when there are more children ("Show more descendants of <name>"). Click either one and the tree loads one more generation in each direction around that card. The chevrons then go away from it.

Two things are not drawn until you ask:

  • Brothers and sisters. The tree walks up from the person and down from the person, so their siblings appear once you open the chevron on one of their parents' cards.
  • A partner's own family. A husband's or wife's parents belong to another tree. Open the chevron on the partner's card to bring them in.

Move around the tree

The tree scrolls inside its own box, so the rest of the page stays where it is. The buttons above it:

Button What it does
Centre on this person Scrolls back to the person the page is about. Hidden while the whole family is fitted.
Fit the whole family Shrinks the drawing until all of it fits the box.
Zoom out, Zoom in Changes the size a step at a time.

Hold Ctrl and scroll over the tree to zoom around the pointer; a pinch on a trackpad does the same.

Brothers, sisters and in-laws

There is no brother or sister relation to record. Two people are siblings when they share a parent: give both the same two parents and they are brother and sister, give them one parent in common and they are half-siblings. In-laws and step-parents also follow from the ties you record: the partner of someone's parent is their stepfather or stepmother, and the parent of someone's partner is their father-in-law or mother-in-law. Set each person's Gender with Edit (see Edit a person) so that EiN can say father or mother rather than parent.

Add a parent, partner or child

You need administrator rights for this in the web interface; see Who may change what.

  1. Open the person's Family tab.
  2. Find the card of the person you are adding to. It can be any card in the tree, not only the person the page is about.
  3. Click ⋯ on that card and choose Add parent, Add partner or Add child.
  4. The dialog asks the question: "Whose child is <name>?", "Who is <name> with?" or "Who is a child of <name>?". Leave Someone already here selected and pick the person in Who. Type part of a name to narrow the list.
  5. For a parent or a child, choose the Kind. For a partner, choose the Relationship and, if you know them, the dates in From and Until.
  6. Press Add.

The tree, the person's header and their timeline are read again at once, and the new relative's card appears in its place.

Each tie joins two people. Add child gives the child one parent: if both partners of a couple are the child's parents, add the child from the other partner's card as well, or add the second parent from the child's own card. Adding a partner links no children.

Warning

Choose Someone already here, not Someone new, in this dialog. Someone new makes the person outside the archive's people folder: they are not listed on the People page, their card in the tree does not open their page, and their birthday never reaches the Home page. To add someone who is not in the archive yet, a great-grandmother for example, first create them with Create → Person (see Create a person by hand), then add them here with Someone already here.

Parent kinds

Kind Use it for
Blood A birth parent. This is the default.
Adoptive A parent by adoption, as Jon and Kiri Wrenfield are to Leo.
Step A step-parent you want drawn as a parent, above the child.
Foster A foster parent.

The tree draws every kind of parent alike. The kind shows in the words EiN uses for the tie, such as "adoptive father" or "stepmother", when the assistant or person-how-related says how two people are related. A step-parent does not have to be recorded as a parent at all: the partner of a parent is already a stepfather or stepmother.

Partnerships and their dates

Relationship is Partner or Married. From and Until take a year, a month or a day: 1972, 1972-05 or 1972-05-20. Leave Until empty while the two are together.

The dates do more than label the line in the tree:

  • From puts "<A> and <B> married" (or "became partners") on both people's timelines on that date.
  • Until puts "<A> and <B> parted" on both timelines. Leave it empty when the partnership ended with a death: the death date already ends the anniversaries, and an Until on the day of death makes both timelines say the two parted that day.
  • From with a full date makes the anniversary come round on the Home page: "Jon Wrenfield and Kiri Wrenfield — 30 years married". A partnership dated only to a year or a month, such as Thomas Wrenfield and Rachel Moore's from 2008-05, has no day to come round on. See Birthdays, memorials and anniversaries.

To add or correct the dates of a partnership that is already recorded, choose Add partner again, pick the same partner, and fill in From or Until. EiN updates the dates of the partnership you have instead of adding a second one, and keeps its Relationship as it was. To change the Relationship or to clear a date, remove the relation and add it again.

When EiN refuses

Problems show in a red box in the dialog, which stays open so you can correct them. People are named there by the last part of their page's address, such as Helen_Okafor.

Message What it means
"pick somebody, or create them" Who is empty.
"person-set-parent: refusing to add parent: <a> is a descendant of <b>" The tie would make someone their own ancestor. Check who is the parent.
"person-set-parent: <a> is already a parent of <b>" The tie is already there.
"person-set-partner: <a> and <b> are already partners" The partnership is already there, and no dates were given to update.
"person-set-partner: invalid from date '…' (YYYY[-MM[-DD]])", or the same with to A date is not written as a year, a month or a day.
"person-set-partner: partnership end is before start" Until is earlier than From.

Remove a relation

  1. On the Family tab, click ⋯ on the card of one of the two people and choose Remove a relation….
  2. The dialog lists their ties to the people on screen, such as "Arthur Wrenfield — partner" or "Helen Okafor — child". Choose one.
  3. Press Remove.

The tie goes both ways, so this removes it from both people. Neither person is deleted, and nothing goes to the trash: to undo it, add the relation again, with its kind and dates.

Only ties to people drawn in the tree are offered. If the dialog says "Nothing to remove — no relations of theirs are on screen.", open the chevrons until the other person's card is shown.

How two people are related

There is no page in the web interface that answers this. Ask the assistant, for example "How is Ruby related to Peggy?". Its answer can carry a small tree of only the people on the line between the two, with a Full screen button. In the terminal, person-how-related answers in English or Swedish:

# in EiN's terminal
person-how-related /archive/people/Peggy_Wrenfield /archive/people/Ruby_Wrenfield
person-how-related /archive/people/Erik_Wrenfield /archive/people/Rachel_Moore
Ruby Wrenfield is Peggy Wrenfield's granddaughter
Rachel Moore is Erik Wrenfield's stepmother

Add --lang sv after the two people for the Swedish word.

Record an event

An event is a dated note on a person: a wedding, a move abroad, a graduation, a birth or a death.

  1. Open the person's page, click ⋮ in the header and choose Add event.
  2. Choose the Type: Generic, Birth, Death, Marriage or Engagement.
  3. Type the Date as 1956, 1956-06 or 1956-06-16.
  4. Write the Description, with Markdown if you like. Its first line becomes the event's title on the timeline.
  5. Written by names the person who wrote the text. It starts as you, when your account is linked to a person; clear it or pick someone else.
  6. Press Add event. It becomes available once there is a date or a description.
Type What else it does
Generic Nothing more: it is an entry on the person's timeline.
Birth With a date, it sets the person's Born, replacing what was there. Its description joins the "<name> was born" entry on the timeline.
Death With a date, it sets the person's Died, replacing what was there. Its description joins the "<name> died" entry.
Marriage Counts in the Marriages by month chart on the Statistics page. It does not record a partnership; see Record a wedding.
Engagement Counts in the Engagements by month chart.

Things to know:

  • An event without a date is kept but placed on no timeline, since there is nothing to place it by. Give it a date to see it.

  • Without a description, the timeline shows a name made from the type and the date, such as marriage-1956-06-16. Write a first line that says what happened.

  • A person's events cannot be changed or removed in the web interface once added. An administrator, or anyone who may change the person, can do it in the terminal: the events are files in the person's events folder.

    # in EiN's terminal
ls /archive/people/Peggy_Wrenfield/events
trash put /archive/people/Peggy_Wrenfield/events/event-1999

    trash put moves the event to your trash, where you can restore it; see Your profile, favourites and trash.

Record a wedding

A wedding is best recorded in two parts, because each part feeds a different place:

  1. The partnership, with Add partner, Relationship Married and the full date in From. It puts the wedding on both people's timelines and brings the anniversary to the Home page every year.
  2. A Marriage event, if you have more to say, with the same date and a description such as "Married at Islington Town Hall". It carries the story, and counts in the Statistics chart.

When the event and the partnership fall on the same day, the timeline of the person who has the event shows the two as one entry; the partner's timeline shows the partnership.

Events of albums, locations and devices

Albums, locations and devices have Add event under ⋮ too, for someone who may change them. Their events are always generic, and need both a Date and a Description.

  • On an album, the date starts as the first day with pictures and no event yet. The event stands as a note among that day's pictures, signed "— <writer>" when it has a writer. If you may change it, a pencil on the note opens Edit event, where you change the date, the text or the writer and press Save, or press Delete. A deleted event goes to your trash, and the message "Event moved to the trash" has Undo.
  • On a location or a device, the event appears on its Timeline tab. It cannot be changed in the web interface afterwards.

What appears on a timeline

A person's Timeline tab is put together from what is recorded about them and their family. You do not add entries to it directly.

Entry Where it comes from
"<name> was born", "<name> died" Born and Died, with any Birth or Death event
The event's title Their dated events
"<name> moved to <location>", "<name> left <location>" Their homes, on the Moved in and Moved out dates
"<A> and <B> married", "became partners", "parted" Their partnerships' From and Until
"<child> was born", with "Child of <name>" Their children's Born
An album The albums with pictures of them

A location's Timeline tab shows its residents moving in and out, its own events and the albums with photos taken there. When it has none, it says "Nothing recorded at <location> yet. Residents with dates, and albums with photos taken here, appear on its timeline."

A person's Born and Died also put them on the Year page for those years, with a "Born" or "Died, aged N" badge; see Media, Year and labels.

Homes and residents

Where someone lived is one record seen from two sides: the person's Homes tab lists their homes, and each location's Residents tab lists the people who lived there. A change on either side shows on both.

Add a home to a person

You need administrator rights for this in the web interface.

  1. Open the person's Homes tab. It says "Where <name> has lived, and when."
  2. Press Add home.
  3. Choose Location already here and pick it in Location, or choose New location and type a Location name.
  4. Fill in Moved in and, if they left, Moved out: 1972, 1972-05 or 1972-05-20. Leave Moved out empty while they live there.
  5. Press Add home.

A new location made here has only its name. It has no place on the map until someone opens it and gives it an address or a position; see Locations and the map.

Each row shows the location, which opens its page, and the years: 1956 – 1999, since 1983, until 1999, or dates unknown. A home with a moved-in date and no moved-out date is marked current. Homes without a moved-out date come first, then the others, the latest move first.

  • To correct the dates, press the pencil on the row, change Moved in or Moved out, and press Save changes. The dates you leave are the new ones: emptying Moved out makes it a current home again.
  • To remove a home, press the bin on the row, then Remove under "Remove <location> from this home history?". The location itself stays.

A person with no homes shows "No homes recorded."

Add a resident to a location

The Residents tab of a location works the same way, from the other side, and anyone who may change the location can use it.

  1. Open the location and choose the Residents tab. It says "Who has lived at <location>, and when."
  2. Press Add resident.
  3. Choose Person already here and pick them in Person, or choose New person and type their Name. A new person made here is an ordinary person on the People page.
  4. Fill in Moved in and Moved out, and press Add resident.

The pencil and the bin on each row correct the dates and remove the resident, as on the Homes tab; the question there is "Remove <name> from the residents of <location>?". A location with nobody recorded shows "No known residents."

Why the dates matter

The dates are what turn a home into moves:

  • A home with a moved-in date puts "<name> moved to <location>" on the person's timeline and on the location's. A moved-out date adds "<name> left <location>".
  • A home without dates says where someone lived and nothing about when. It is listed on the Homes and Residents tabs as dates unknown and on the person's Info tab, but on no timeline.
  • A home without a moved-out date counts as where the person lives now, even with no dates at all: the person's header shows it, and the location's header lists them under its current residents. Give a moved-out date to every home somebody has left.

When saving a home fails

The form stays open with the reason in a red box:

Message What to do
"pick a location, or create one", "pick somebody, or create them" Pick one in the list, or switch to New location or New person.
"a location name is needed", "a name is needed" Type the name.
"that name has no letters or digits to make a path from" Use a name with letters or digits in it.
"person-set-home: --from '…' is not a date (YYYY[-MM[-DD]])" Write the date as 1972, 1972-05 or 1972-05-20.
"person-set-home: moved out … before moving in …" Moved out is earlier than Moved in.
"person-set-home: … already exists (use --update to change the dates)" The home is already recorded. Close the form and use the pencil on its row.

Birthdays, memorials and anniversaries on the Home page

The Events panel on the Home page lists what comes round in the next 30 days and what came round in the last 7, under Today, Coming up and Recently. It is filled from the dates on this page, so what it shows depends on what you record:

On the Home page Comes from Needs
Birthdays: "<name> turns N", or "would have turned N" after their death the person's Born a full date, 1934-08-22
Memorials: "N years since <name> died" the person's Died a full date; only on round years
Anniversaries: "<A> and <B> — N years married" or "… years together" a partnership's From a full date; Married says married, Partner says together

A date known only to the year or the month never comes round, since it has no day. Round years, the 5th, 10th, 15th and 20th and then every 10th, are shown in bold, and they are kept first when there are more than 16 entries. Birthdays stop after the 110th. An anniversary comes round up to the year of the Until date or of the first partner's death, and for 75 years at most. Someone born on 29 February appears only in leap years.

Birthdays and memorials come from the people on the People page. Home, memories and statistics describes the panel itself, and the Statistics page with its charts of births, engagements, marriages and deaths by month.

In the terminal

Everything on this page is also a command, which an administrator or a power user can run in the terminal or over SSH. The commands need permission to change the people and locations they write to; they do not ask for administrator rights.

# in EiN's terminal
person-set-parent /archive/people/Leo_Wrenfield /archive/people/Kiri_Wrenfield --kind adoptive
person-set-partner /archive/people/Jon_Wrenfield /archive/people/Kiri_Wrenfield --kind married --from 1996-02-03
person-set-home --from 1996 /archive/people/Jon_Wrenfield /archive/locations/Kelburn
person-add-event --type marriage --date 1956-06-16 --body 'Married at Islington Town Hall' /archive/people/Peggy_Wrenfield
person-family-tree /archive/people/Peggy_Wrenfield

Give person-set-parent, person-set-partner and person-how-related the two people first and the flags after them: placed before the people, a flag's value is taken for a person.

Command What it does
person-set-parent Adds a parent; the child comes first. --kind blood|adoptive|step|foster.
person-set-partner Adds a partnership, or updates the dates of one. --kind partner|married, --from, --to.
person-remove Removes the tie between two people, from both sides.
person-set-home Records a home with --from and --to; --update rewrites the dates of one already there.
person-remove-home Removes a home, from both the person and the location.
person-add-event Adds an event to a person, album, location or device.
person-info Shows a person's dates, relatives, homes and events, the undated ones too.
person-family-tree Prints ancestors and descendants as text.
person-how-related Names how two people are related.
person-timeline Prints a person's timeline.
person-check Looks for broken ties, impossible dates and loops in the tree: person-check /archive/people.
events Prints what the Home page's Events panel shows.

You can also tell the assistant a parent, a partnership or an event the archive should record, for example "Leo was adopted by Jon and Kiri". It proposes the command, and nothing changes until you press Run; it runs as you, with your permissions. It does not record homes.

When something goes differently

There is no ⋯ on the cards, or no Add home button. These are shown only to administrators. Ask one to make the change, or, if you are in the sudo group, choose Administer… in the account menu first.

A relative is missing from the tree. Open the chevron on the nearest card that is shown; siblings and a partner's family appear only then. If there is no chevron, the tie is not recorded, or the relative's page is hidden from you.

A card in the tree opens "Person not found." The person was made with Someone new in the relation dialog, outside the archive's people folder. Remove the relation, create the person with Create → Person, and add the relation again with Someone already here. The stray person stays under /people, where an administrator can delete it in the Nodes explorer or the terminal.

A birthday or anniversary does not show on the Home page. Check that the date has a day, that it falls within the next 30 days or the last 7, and for an anniversary that the partnership has a From date. When more than 16 entries fall in the window, the rest are left out; round years are kept first.

A move is not on the timeline. The home has no dates. Press the pencil on its row and fill in Moved in.

Someone shows a home they left long ago as where they live. That home has no moved-out date. Add one.
