Using EiN

Devices

The Devices page holds the things outside EiN that report to it or can be switched from it: the sensors, plugs, switches, lights and buttons in the house, a phone that says where it is, a GPS logger, a boat. Each has a page with what it reports now, how that went over time, and the switches to turn. The members of the devices group see them all and add phones and loggers; anyone else sees the devices a member has shared with them. This page is for everyone who uses them. Devices, endpoints and drivers explains how they are kept, and Devices and their keys who may do what.

Where devices come from

  • A driver finds them. With the Automation module, a sensor or a plug that announces itself on the house's MQTT broker, such as one running ESPHome or a Shelly, is on the Devices page within seconds of connecting, with everything it reads and does. Nothing is entered in EiN. One that is on the network but not yet on the broker is counted in the header, such as 2 found, and a Shelly is added from there (Add a device found on the network).
  • Someone makes them. A phone or a GPS logger announces nothing, so a member of the devices group makes it by hand (see A phone or a GPS tracker). EiN's own tracker driver then reads what it sends. So is a button, a sensor or a script that posts to an address, read by EiN's webhook driver (see A device that posts to a webhook).

A device has a name, such as hall-plug, that commands and rules use, and a label, such as Hall lamp, that the pages show. Each thing it reports or takes is an endpoint with a name of its own within it: hall-plug/switch-0 is the plug's relay, hall-plug/switch-0-power the power it draws, ruby-phone/position where the phone has been.

The Devices page

Open Devices in the sidebar, under Browse. It has a card for every device you may see: its label, its main value, and badges for online or offline, what it is (such as plug or phone) and how many endpoints it has. The main value is a temperature when the device has one, then power, humidity, light or energy, else any other reading, else a switch; a device with none shows its manufacturer and model instead. A device that only posts what it has, such as a phone or tracker sending its position or one posting to a webhook, is online while it has been heard from in the last 15 minutes; a device that says nothing of whether it is on has neither online nor offline. Choose a card to open the device.

  • Type in the filter box to narrow the cards by label, name, what it is, manufacturer or model.
  • Show All devices or only those Online.
  • Sort by Name (A-Z) or by Latest value, the device that reported most lately first.

The header counts the devices and how many are online. With none yet, the page says No devices yet: "Devices appear here when a driver finds them, or when someone makes one with device-create."

A device's page

The header names the device, its manufacturer and model, whether it is online, what it is, and how long ago its last value came. Under it are three tabs, and any that modules add:

Tab What it shows
Overview The page opens here: a tile for each endpoint as it is now, with the switches
History How each endpoint went over a day, a week, a month or a year
Info What the device is, its driver, each endpoint and its key
Trips With the Archive, on a device that has a track: its trips

Members of the devices group also have Edit in the header's corner, and Forget under ⋮ (see Rename or forget a device); on a phone both are under ⋮.

The address is /devices/ and the device's name, such as /devices/hall-plug, and the tab is in ?tab= (Addresses in the Web UI). A device opened from the Nodes page opens here, and so does any of its endpoints.

Overview

A tile for each endpoint:

  • A reading, such as a temperature: its value with its unit, how long ago it came, and a line of the last six hours.
  • A button: its last press, such as single, and how long ago it was ("pressed 4 min ago"), or "not pressed yet".
  • A position: a small map of the day up to its last position, and that position: its coordinates, its altitude, speed (in knots on a boat, km/h otherwise) and course when the device sent them, and when it came. The button on the map, Enlarge map, opens it large, with the choice of map; Over time opens it on the History tab.
  • A switch or a light: a toggle that switches it.
  • A setting that takes a number, such as a thermostat's target: a slider, set when you let go, or a box with Set when it has no range.
  • A setting that takes one of some choices: a list to pick from.
  • An action, such as a restart or a chime: Press, with when it was last pressed and by whom.

A switch or a setting you change shows what you asked for at once, with "asked on, waiting for the device" under its label, until the device says it has done it; then it shows what the device reports. A switch you may not change is greyed out. What tells of the device itself rather than of the house, such as its own temperature or its Wi-Fi signal, is under About the device itself. An endpoint the device no longer announces is left out; its history stays.

History

Every endpoint kept over time is drawn at once, over the span picked at the right of the header's tabs: Day, Week, Month or Year (on a phone, the span's icon button). A track's map comes first, then a chart for each reading, the device's main value, the one its card shows, first among them. Over time on a position's tile puts its track at the top. An endpoint the device no longer announces is marked (retired).

  • A reading is charted as the average of every 15 minutes over a day, of every 2 hours over a week, of every 6 hours over a month and of every day over a year, to five significant digits.
  • Anything on or off, a switch, a door or a motion sensor, is charted as the share of the time it was on.
  • A button is charted as how many presses there were.
  • A track is drawn on a map, each position of the span, up to the latest 20,000, with how many there are and how long ago the last was.

A span with nothing in it says No values in this span. or No fixes in this span.

Info

  • What the device is: its Name in commands and rules, Class, Driver, Manufacturer, Model, Firmware, Serial and MAC. A driver whose module's service is stopped reads (not running), in red: the device's switches do nothing until it runs again.
  • Web page, the device's own page, opened in a new tab, when its driver knows it: a Shelly's and an ESPHome node's from what they say of themselves, a printer's admin page and the HTTP page of anything added from the network at its address now. It is on your home network, so it opens only where that network is reached.
  • Each endpoint, with its label and name, its type (log, track or control), its unit, its latest value, and whether you may write it. events marks a button's presses, diagnostic or config what tells of the device itself, not read an endpoint whose values its driver cannot read, and retired one the device no longer announces.
  • Device key, for a device whose driver reads what is posted to it, such as a phone: the address it posts to (see A phone or a GPS tracker). Only those who may change the device see it.

Switch something

The toggles, sliders and Press buttons on a device's Overview switch it. In the terminal, write the value to the control:

# in EiN's terminal
echo on > /dev/hall-plug/switch-0
echo toggle > /dev/hall-plug/switch-0
echo 21.5 > /dev/heater/target-temperature
echo eco > /dev/heat-pump/mode
echo press > /dev/hall-sensor/restart

A switch takes on, off or toggle; a number setting a number within its limits; a choice one of its options; an action press. The device's Info tab and device-show say which endpoints are controls and whether you may write them. The value goes to the device at once, and the command ends when the device's driver has taken it, or fails, saying why:

  • hall-plug/switch-0 takes on, off or toggle, not '7': the value is not one the control takes.
  • hall-plug's driver shelly is not running: the module that drives it has its service stopped.
  • hall-plug/switch-0: not connected to the broker, so nothing was sent: with Automation, EiN does not hear the MQTT broker just now.
  • Access denied for operation 'w' on node: …: the control is narrowed to others (Devices and their keys).

Nothing is kept to be sent later: a lamp switched on an hour after it was asked would surprise. Use > to switch, never >> (see Writing to a device).

With the Assistant and the Automation module, asking the chat to "turn on the hall lamp" gives a proposal card with the echo line, which you run or decline. With Automation, rules switch things by themselves when a reading, a press or the clock says so.

Read what a device reported

# in EiN's terminal
devices                                                       # every device, with its main value
devices --online
device-show hall-sensor                                       # its endpoints and their values now
device-history hall-sensor/temperature -n 10                  # the latest 10 values
device-history hall-sensor/temperature --since 7d --bucket 1h            # an average an hour
device-history heater-plug/switch-0-power --since 30d --bucket 1d --agg max   # the highest each day
device-history ruby-phone/position --since 1d                 # the day's positions
cat /dev/hall-plug/switch-0                                   # every state the plug reported
tail -f /dev/hall-sensor/temperature                          # each new reading, until Ctrl+C

device-history prints a line per value, its time, a tab and the value: --since and --until take a time or a duration such as 24h or 7d, and --bucket sums the values up per interval, with --agg as avg (the default), min, max, sum, count, first or last; on and off count as 1 and 0. Without --since it gives the latest 100, -n for another number. A track gives its positions.

A phone or a GPS tracker

A phone that reports where it is as it moves, a GPS tracker on a boat or in a car, and a logger whose files you copy in are all devices driven by EiN's tracker driver, each keeping where it was in its position track. Members of the devices group make one:

# in EiN's terminal, as a member of devices
device-create 'Ruby phone' --driver tracker --class phone
device-create 'Sailboat' --driver tracker --class boat
device-create "Jon's eTrex" --driver tracker --class foot --manufacturer Garmin --model 'eTrex 30x'

device-create prints the device's path, such as /dev/ruby-phone. --class says what it is: phone, tracker, or, for something that always travels the same way, boat, car, bike or foot, which the Archive takes as how its trips travel. --manufacturer and --model say more about it, and let a GPX file that names them find it.

Report from a phone

A phone with the free Traccar Client app adds its position to the device's track as it moves:

  1. Open the device's page at the address the phone will reach EiN at, such as https://ein.wrenfield.example, and its Info tab.
  2. Under Device key, choose Make a key. A QR code and the address appear.
  3. On the phone, install Traccar Client and scan the QR code in its settings, or with the phone's camera, which opens the app. It fills in the server URL with the key in it and, as the device identifier, the device's name. Or type the server URL in by hand shows the address to type instead.
  4. Set how often it reports, for example every 30 seconds, and switch tracking on.

OsmAnd reports the same way: give its online tracking the device's address with &lat={0}&lon={1}&timestamp={2} added. So does a hardware tracker that posts the OsmAnd protocol, or GPRMC sentences, over HTTP.

The phone posts to EiN from wherever it is, so EiN must be reachable from the internet over HTTPS (Phones and trackers posting). The address holds the device's key, and anyone who has it can add positions to the device: New key makes another (the phone stops reaching EiN until it is given the new address), and Take the key away stops it and keeps the track. In the terminal:

# in EiN's terminal
device-key ruby-phone --new --origin https://ein.wrenfield.example   # a new key, and the address
device-key ruby-phone --origin https://ein.wrenfield.example         # the address, while it has one
device-key ruby-phone --off

GPX files

A GPS logger, a watch or a tracking app saves its routes as GPX files. Write one to the device and its positions join the track; a position with a time the track already has is not added twice, and one without a time is left out:

# in EiN's terminal
cat ~/tararua-walk.gpx > /dev/jon-s-etrex

Over SFTP, copy the file onto the device's path, /dev/jon-s-etrex. With the Archive, a GPX file uploaded into staging finds its device by itself, and the device's Trips tab has Upload GPX; see GPX files.

A device that posts to a webhook

Something without a driver of its own, such as a button that calls an address, a sensor that posts JSON, or a script reading a meter, posts what it reads to a device driven by EiN's webhook driver. A member of devices makes the device and gives it the device's key:

# in EiN's terminal, as a member of devices
device-create 'Garden sensor' --driver webhook --class sensor
device-key garden-sensor --new --origin https://ein.wrenfield.example
# on the machine that posts
curl -H 'Authorization: Bearer <the key>' -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  --data '{"soil-moisture": 41.5, "battery": 87}' https://ein.wrenfield.example/file/dev/garden-sensor

The key is the part of the address after key=. Each field becomes a reading on the endpoint of that name, made the first time it comes, here soil-moisture and battery: it shows on the device's page and is published on the bus as dev.garden-sensor.soil-moisture, where the Automation module's rules hear it, like any driver's reading. A device that only takes an address sends its values in it instead, https://ein.wrenfield.example/file/dev/garden-sensor?key=<the key>&soil-moisture=41.5. What the driver reads, and how a button's presses are kept, is in Core's webhook driver.

Values written to an endpoint

A script can also write a value straight to a log it made under a device, with no driver in between:

# in EiN's terminal, as a member of devices
device-create 'Garden sensor' --class sensor
mklog /dev/garden-sensor/soil-moisture
attrs -s valueField=value /dev/garden-sensor/soil-moisture
device-key garden-sensor --new --origin https://ein.wrenfield.example
# on the machine that runs the script
curl -H 'Authorization: Bearer <the key>' --data 41.5 https://ein.wrenfield.example/file/dev/garden-sensor/soil-moisture

Each value is kept with the time it arrived, as {"value": 41.5}, and shows on the device's page like any reading; valueField makes the log keep a value written in the terminal the same way, echo 41.5 > /dev/garden-sensor/soil-moisture. Values written this way are not published on the bus; post them to a webhook device for that.

Rename or forget a device

Members of the devices group rename a device on its page with Edit: Name, what its pages and cards call it, and under Endpoints a name for each endpoint it announces, each field headed by the endpoint's name in /dev. Save writes what changed. Forget, under ⋮, removes the device and everything it kept after asking "Forget <device> and all it has kept? A driver that finds it again makes it again." A sensor that is still on comes back when it next announces itself, without its old history.

In the terminal:

# in EiN's terminal, as a member of devices
device-label hall-plug 'Hall lamp'                     # the label its page shows
device-label hall-sensor/temperature 'Hall temperature'
device-forget old-sensor                               # the device and everything it kept

The name in commands and rules stays as it is. Forgetting removes the device's history with it; a device a driver still hears comes back new.

When something goes differently

What you see What it means What to do
Devices is not in the sidebar You are not signed in Sign in
No devices yet No driver has found one, and nobody has made one With Automation, see Check that EiN hears them
A switch stays "waiting for the device" The device took the command but has not said it switched: it is off, or out of reach Check its power and its network; the switch shows what it reports once it does
The driver reads (not running) The service of the module that brings the driver is stopped An administrator starts it on the dashboard's Services tab, or with service start
toggle needs the state the device last reported, and it has reported none The control has never reported a state Write on or off instead
device-create says only the devices group's members make, name and forget devices You are not in the devices group, or were added since you signed in Ask the administrator to add you, then sign in again
Edit and Forget are missing The same The same
device-create says 'null' is core's own in /dev, not a device's name The name is one of EiN's own in /dev Choose another name
cat ride.gpx > /dev/thermometer says the device has no driver Only a device with a driver that reads raw input takes a file Write it to the tracker it was recorded on
The phone reports nothing It cannot reach EiN over HTTPS from where it is, its key was renewed or taken away, or tracking is off in Traccar Client See Report from a phone and Ports, HTTPS and remote access
