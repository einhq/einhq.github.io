Using EiN

Search and the finder

EiN gives you two ways to find something: the finder in the top bar, which takes you to a page, an action or a command in a few keystrokes, and the Search page. What there is to search comes from the modules installed; this page covers what EiN itself gives, for everyone who uses EiN in a browser. With the Archive installed, Searching your pictures covers finding photos, videos, recordings and documents, and the albums, people and places they belong to.

The finder in the top bar

The box in the middle of the top bar, Search for anything, is the finder. Click it, or press Ctrl+K (⌘+K on a Mac) wherever you are, and a list opens under it. The list closes when you press Esc, click somewhere else or choose a row, and choosing a row empties the box.

When something covers the box (a dialog, the viewer, or a page shown full screen), the same keys open the finder as a dialog of its own, with the prompt Search, or go to a page…. Esc, or Ctrl+K again, closes it.

On a phone the box reads Search, the list takes the width of the screen, and the keyboard's search key does what Enter does. EiN on a phone covers the rest.

With no module installed, the finder finds the pages in your sidebar, the actions in the top bar and EiN's commands. Each module adds its pages and actions, and the Archive adds its albums, people, locations and devices by name.

Go somewhere with nothing typed

With the box empty, the list shows two groups:

  • Go to: every page in your sidebar, with the sidebar section it is in beside it.
  • Do: every button in the top bar, and every row of its menus named after its menu. With the Archive and the assistant installed, these include Chat, Random album, Create: Album, Create: Import media and Account: Favourites.

Use the arrow keys to light a row and Enter to open it, or click it. While nothing is typed, Enter does nothing until an arrow key has lit a row.

Type a name

As you type, the list changes to these groups, in this order:

Group Rows A row takes you to
Search Search for "<your words>", and with the Archive installed Pictures of "<your words>" The Search page, or the Archive's Media page, searching for the words
Go to and Do The pages and actions whose names hold your words That page or action
The kinds the Search page finds Their things whose names match, with the kind of each beside it. With the Archive installed the group is Albums, people, locations, devices Its page

The first row is lit, so Enter opens the Search page for your words, and so does the arrow button at the end of the box. To go straight to a page or a thing, light its row with the arrow keys or click it. With the Archive installed, in the Wrenfield demo, typing peggy offers Peggy Wrenfield, and typing christmas offers albums such as Christmas 1975, Christmas 1998 and Christmas in Wellington.

While names are being looked up the list says Looking…; when nothing fits it says Nothing matches.

Note

Words you take to the Search or Media page from the finder are searched exactly as you typed them. To have a sentence read into people, places and years (see Write what you are looking for), press Enter once more in that page's own box.

Find a command with >

  1. Click the box or press Ctrl+K.
  2. Type > followed by what the command should do, or part of its name, such as >person.
  3. Up to six commands appear under Commands, each with its one-line description.
  4. Choose one to open its entry in EiN's documentation (Documentation in the sidebar).

Choosing a command does not run it: you run commands in the terminal. With fewer than two letters after the >, the list says Type what the command should do., and when nothing fits, No command matches. The line at the foot of the list, Type > to find a command, is there as a reminder.

When the assistant module and the media service are installed, the finder looks for commands by what they are for, so a command can turn up even when its name and description use other words than yours. Without them, and while the media service is not answering, it matches your words against the commands' names and descriptions. The command reference lists every command.

The Search page

Search, under Explore in the sidebar, searches the kinds of things the installed modules make searchable. With no module installed it has nothing to search: before you type it says Search for anything, and a search answers No results found. With the Archive installed it finds albums, people, locations and devices; see Find albums, people, locations and devices.

  1. Open Search in the sidebar, or choose Search for "…" in the finder. The header reads Search and Search by name, date or @mention.
  2. Type in the box, Search, or type @ to pick something by name, and press Enter.
  3. The results appear as cards, 60 at a time, with more as you scroll. A card shows the name, a cover picture and the kind. Click a card to open its page.
  4. When the modules make more than one kind searchable, narrow the results with Show: All, or one of the kinds.
  5. Order them with Sort: Relevance (the default), Name (A-Z), Newest first or Oldest first.

When nothing is found, the page says No results found.

The box takes @ to pick something exactly, turns what it understood into chips you can remove one by one, and offers the Interpreted filter to show what it searched for. Which words it reads as people, places, years and kinds comes from a module: the Archive reads them, and Searching your pictures explains how. Without it, the box searches your words as they are.

Every search finds only what you are allowed to see; a visitor browsing without signing in finds only what is shared with everyone (Sharing).

Before you search

Before you type, the Search page shows Recent searches: your last eight searches on this page. Click one to run it again, its × (Forget …) to drop it, or Clear to drop them all. They are kept in this browser, not in your account, so another computer or phone has a list of its own.

Under them come the sections the modules give the page. With the Archive installed, these are the people, locations, years and labels to start from; see Start from people, places, years and labels. With no recent search and no module to offer a section, the page says Search for anything and "Type a name, a year or a few words; @ picks something by name."

Share or keep a search

The address in your browser holds the whole search: the words, the chips, Show and Sort. So a search can be linked, and the browser's Back and Forward step through your searches. Copy the address to send a search to someone in the family, or to come back to it later. Whoever opens the link sees only what they are allowed to see. To keep a search in your sidebar, see Your sidebar and top bar.

Where search comes from

EiN's search is one command, search, and the modules supply what it searches. A module plugs in by the names of its commands and by its manifest:

What a module gives What it adds to search
search-<x> commands Hits for the words of a search; search asks every one on the path and puts the hits together
subject-<kind> commands What a person, location or device picked with @ has
Node types marked searchable in its manifest The kinds the Search page finds and offers under Show, and the things the finder finds by name
Panels for the Search page The sections shown before you search, and what a search for one label adds
A reader for the box, named by CFG_SEARCH_PARSER How a sentence is read into chips
A command finder, named by CFG_COMMAND_FINDER Commands found by what they are for, in the finder

With no module, search has no provider to ask and the Search page no kind to find. The Archive brings the providers for albums, people, locations, devices, tracks, media and documents, and the reader for the box; the assistant brings the command finder. Writing a module shows how a module plugs in, and the settings are in the settings reference.

In the terminal, search answers the same questions as the pages. Its options are in the command reference; Search from the terminal gives examples over the Archive.
