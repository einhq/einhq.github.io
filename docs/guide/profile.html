Using EiN

Your profile, favourites and trash

This page covers the pages that belong to your account alone: your profile, where you change your name, your preferences and your password; your favourites; and your trash, where what you delete waits before it is removed for good. It is for everyone who has an account on EiN.

All three open from the account menu. Choose your picture at the right end of the top bar:

They need you to be signed in. A visitor who opens one of them is asked to sign in first, and lands on the page afterwards. Finding your way around describes the rest of the account menu.

Open your profile

Choose your picture, then Profile. The page's header shows your display name, your username after an @, and the description you gave yourself. Its picture is the cover photo of the person your account is linked to; without one it shows the first letter of your name.

The profile has three tabs: Account, Preferences and Security. The tab you are on is in the page's address, so /profile?tab=preferences opens your preferences and /profile?tab=security the password form directly.

Change your name and description

  1. Choose your picture, then Profile. It opens on the Account tab.
  2. In Display name, "Your name as it appears to others", type your name the way you want others to see it, for example Helen Okafor.
  3. In Description, "A line about you, shown under your name", type a line about yourself, such as Peggy's daughter, in Wellington, or leave it empty.
  4. Choose Save changes. Saved appears beside the button.

Save changes stays greyed out while Display name is empty: every account has a name. Your picture's tooltip and the profile's header show the new name at once.

Three things about your account are not on this tab:

  • Your username, the name after the @. Nobody can change it once the account exists, an administrator included.
  • Your e-mail address. Only an administrator sets it, in your account under Settings → Users; see Accounts and groups. When your account has one, you can type it instead of your username when you sign in. The profile does not show it; user-info in the terminal does, under From the terminal.
  • Your picture, which comes from the person your account is linked to, below.

Note

Your display name, description and e-mail address are not private. Anyone who is signed in can read them, for example with user-info in the terminal.

Your picture and the person you are

EiN has no way to upload a picture for an account. Your picture is the person in the archive that your account is linked to. Helen Okafor's account, for example, can be linked to her page among the People, and then:

  • your picture in the top bar and on your profile is her cover photo;
  • the account menu has Me above Favourites, which opens her page;
  • News on Home shows her picture beside the albums you add;
  • an event or note you add to an album starts with her under Written by;
  • staging uses her homes to tell a trip from days at home when it suggests albums; see Sorting in staging.

Without a link, your picture is an image of your username's first letters, which the browser fetches from the outside service ui-avatars.com. Where the browser cannot reach the internet, the first letter of your name shows instead.

There is no button to link an account to a person. An administrator makes the link in the terminal: it is a softlink named person in the account's folder under /etc/users. For the account helen and the person whose page is at /people/Helen_Okafor:

# in EiN's terminal, as the administrator
ln -s /archive/people/Helen_Okafor /etc/users/helen/person

Helen sees her picture and Me the next time her page loads. rm /etc/users/helen/person takes the link away again; removing a link never removes the person it points at.

Set your preferences

Choose your picture, then Profile, then the Preferences tab. It says: "These apply to you alone. They are stored in your home directory and follow you to the terminal as well." Your preferences belong to your account, so they follow you to every browser and device you sign in on.

The tab is laid out like the administrator's settings page:

  • Find a setting searches names, descriptions, modules and keys, across every category.
  • Categories, at the left, shows one category at a time. On a phone a Category list above the settings does the same.
  • Overrides only shows only the settings you have changed; the button gives their number in brackets once there are some.
  • A badge under each setting says where its value comes from: Default when nobody has changed it, Your setting when you have, and From deployment when the administrator gave everyone a starting value when setting up EiN. Your own value wins over the other two. A setting a module adds also carries the module's name, such as archive.

To change a setting:

  1. Change the value in its control: pick from the list, type a number, or flip the switch between On and Off.
  2. Cancel and Save change appear under the control. Choose Save change; Cancel puts the value back as it was.
  3. The badge turns to Your setting.

To undo your choice later, choose Restore inherited value under a setting marked Your setting. It removes your own value, and the setting falls back to the deployment's value or to the default. If a save fails, the reason shows in a red box under the setting.

The settings

Category Setting Default What it changes
Appearance Header entrance drawn How the tiles of the coloured band at the top of a page arrive when the page opens; the choices are below. A device set to reduce motion never animates it.
Appearance Header entrance time 450 How many milliseconds that entrance takes, from the first tile starting to the last one in place. 0 shows the header as it is, without an entrance.
Appearance Header over a cover split How the tiles meet a page's cover photo, such as an album's or a person's: split gives the left of the header to the tiles, which dissolve into the photo along a slanted edge; lines lays only their outlines over the photo.
Playback Seconds per slide 3 How long each photo is shown in a slideshow.
Playback Ken Burns effect On Slowly pan and zoom each photo during a slideshow.
Sharing How widely what you create is shared default Where an album you create starts out; see below.
Shell Shell history length 1000 How many commands the terminal remembers between sessions, in ~/.bash_history.

EiN itself gives the settings under Appearance, Sharing and Shell. The two under Playback come with the archive module, and only an EiN with the archive has them. Viewing photos, videos and recordings shows a slideshow, and the settings reference lists each with its key, such as CFG_USER_SLIDESHOW_SECONDS.

How widely what you create is shared

This preference decides how an album you create starts out: one made with Create → Album, with New album “…” in Add to an album, or with New album in staging. It does not change albums that exist already; to change who sees one of those, use its Share button.

Choice A new album starts out
default As the administrator's default sharing says, under Settings → Permissions. Unless the administrator has changed it, everyone with an account can see the album.
private Seen only by you.
shared Seen by the members of the default group the administrator chose. Unless the administrator has changed it, that group is everyone with an account.
everyone Seen by everyone with an account, even where the administrator's default group is a smaller one.

None of them shows an album to visitors who are not signed in; you grant that yourself in the Share dialog. An administrator can see every album, whatever you choose. New private album in staging is always private, whatever you choose here. Sharing explains the default sharing and the Share dialog.

When a change takes effect

Setting Takes effect
Appearance and Playback At once, in the browser tab where you saved it. Other open tabs, and your other devices, when their page loads again.
How widely what you create is shared At once, for the next album you create.
Shell history length In the next terminal you open.

Your preferences in the terminal

EiN keeps your preferences in the file .config in your home folder, /home/<your username>/.config, which only you can read. Every shell you open reads it, in the web terminal and over SSH, so a preference is also a variable there:

# in EiN's terminal
echo $CFG_USER_SLIDESHOW_SECONDS
5

It prints an empty line for a setting you have never changed: defaults are not written into the file. A terminal that was already open when you saved a change keeps the old value; open a new terminal tab, or run source ~/.config in it, to pick up the change.

The config command lists and changes your preferences:

# in EiN's terminal
config list --scope user                  # your preferences, and where each value comes from
config set CFG_USER_SLIDESHOW_SECONDS 5   # the same as Save change
config unset CFG_USER_SLIDESHOW_SECONDS   # the same as Restore inherited value

config list --scope user prints each setting and its value, followed by (user) for your own value or (environment) for the deployment's; a default has no mark:

CFG_USER_HEADER_ENTRANCE	drawn
CFG_USER_HEADER_ENTRANCE_TIME	450
CFG_USER_HEADER_COVER	split
CFG_USER_KEN_BURNS	true
CFG_USER_SLIDESHOW_SECONDS	5 (user)
CFG_USER_DEFAULT_SHARE	default
HISTSIZE	1000

config set answers CFG_USER_SLIDESHOW_SECONDS = 5, and config unset answers CFG_USER_SLIDESHOW_SECONDS reset to 3 (default). A value of the wrong kind is refused, for example config set: CFG_USER_SLIDESHOW_SECONDS: expected a number, got 'five'.

The file holds one line per setting, such as export CFG_USER_SLIDESHOW_SECONDS='5', and you can edit it with edit ~/.config; nothing then checks the values you type. Configuration explains how settings work for the whole installation, and Configuration files lists the files.

Change your password

  1. Choose your picture, then Profile, then the Security tab.
  2. In Current password, type the password you sign in with.
  3. In New password, type the new one.
  4. In Confirm new password, type the new one again. If the two differ, the field says "Does not match the new password".
  5. Choose Update password. It can be chosen once all three fields are filled in and the two new ones match. Password updated appears beside it, and the fields empty.

The eye at the right of each field shows what you typed. EiN has no rules for passwords: it takes any password that is not empty, so how strong yours is is up to you.

The new password is the one you sign in with from then on, in the browser and over SSH and SFTP. A key you added for SSH keeps working; see SSH and SFTP.

Changing your password does not sign out the browsers and terminals where you are signed in already. If you change it because someone else may know it, ask an administrator to choose Disable sign-in for your account and then Enable sign-in: that signs your account out everywhere.

If the password is not changed, the reason shows in a red box above the fields:

Message What it means What to do
user passwd: authentication failed Current password is not your password. Type it again. If you have forgotten it, see the next section.
user passwd: no existing password found, contact admin Your account has no password to check against. Ask an administrator to set one.

If you have forgotten your password

EiN sends no e-mail and has no link for a forgotten password. Ask an administrator to set a new one for you: they do it in your account under Settings → Users, in its Password section, with Set password. Your old password stops working at once. Sign in with the one they give you, then change it on the Security tab to one that only you know.

An administrator cannot make you choose a new password at your next sign-in; EiN has no such switch. An account that an administrator made without a password, and an account brought over from an older archive by the importer, cannot sign in at all until an administrator sets a password for it. Accounts and groups covers this from the administrator's side.

Favourites

Your favourites are the pictures you have marked with a heart, from any album, gathered on one page. Only someone signed in has favourites; visitors have none.

A favourite is a link to the picture where it lives, kept in the folder stars in your home folder. Hearting a picture never copies or moves it, and taking it out of your favourites never deletes it. Links explains how one picture can be in many places.

Heart a picture

  • In the viewer, press the heart in the bar under the picture, whose tooltip says Favourite (s), or press s. The heart fills in. On a phone it is Favourite in ⋮ at the top right.
  • In a grid, such as an album or the Media page, select pictures and choose Favourite in the selection bar. EiN says, for example, 12 files added to favourites, with Undo.

A small heart on a tile marks a picture in your favourites. A picture marked as a favourite in a Google Photos export becomes one of your favourites when you import it; see Getting your photos in.

Open your favourites

Choose your picture, then Favourites. The page, "The pictures you have hearted", shows them in day sections, the way an album shows its pictures. Click one to open it in the viewer, which then steps through your favourites; Start slideshow there shows them all.

With nothing hearted yet, the page says No favourites yet, "Select pictures and choose Favourite, or press the heart in the lightbox.", with a button Open Media.

Take a picture out of your favourites

  • In the viewer, press the filled heart, Unfavourite (s), or press s again.
  • In a grid, select pictures that are all favourites already. The selection bar's button reads Unfavourite; choose it, and EiN says, for example, 3 files taken out of favourites, with Undo.

The trash

When you delete pictures, albums, people and the like in the web interface, they go to your trash first. You can restore them from there for 30 days, unless your administrator chose another number; the few ways of deleting that skip the trash are listed below. Only you see your trash, apart from an administrator, who can see everyone's.

What goes to the trash

What you do What goes
Delete in the selection bar The selected pictures that live where you are; see Albums.
Move to trash under ⋮ on an album, person, location or device That album, person, location or device, with everything that lives in it.
Delete in Edit event That event or note.
Discard in staging The files, kept together as one entry; see Sorting in staging.
Removing copies in staging's Duplicates The copies, kept together as one entry; see Duplicates.
trash put in the terminal What you name, if you may change it.

Each of these says what it did in a message at the foot of the screen, with Undo. Undo is the quickest way back; the Trash page is for later.

What you delete goes to your trash, also when it belongs to someone else who lets you change it. If a picture disappears from an album of yours, the person who deleted it has it in their trash.

While something is in the trash, it is gone for everyone: from albums, search, the map and every other list. It keeps what EiN worked out about it, such as faces and a transcript, and comes back with all of it when you restore it.

What is deleted for good at once

Warning

These do not use the trash. What they delete is gone at once, with no Undo:

  • Delete in the Nodes explorer, which runs rm;
  • rm in the terminal;
  • deleting a file over SFTP, which takes its thumbnails and faces with it; see SSH and SFTP;
  • Delete for good and Empty trash on the Trash page.

To delete something in the terminal that you may want back, use trash put instead of rm.

Restore something from the trash

  1. Choose your picture, then Trash.
  2. The list shows what you deleted, newest first. Each row is named by the place it came from, such as /archive/albums/Sailing in the archipelago/IMG_0412.jpg. Things deleted together, such as a group discarded in staging, are one row with a name and a count, such as Discarded from Inbox: “Boat trip” · 86 items.
  3. Select a row. The pane beside the list, or under it on a phone, shows its name, From where it came, when it was Deleted, and a preview. Hold Ctrl (⌘ on a Mac) while you click to add a row to the selection, or Shift to select a range.
  4. Choose Restore, or Restore 3 with three rows selected. EiN says Restored to and the path, or how many it restored.

A restored thing goes back where it came from, and the links to it work again: a restored picture is back in the albums it was added to, and in your favourites if it was one. If something else has taken its name there in the meantime, it comes back beside it with a number, such as IMG_0412 (2).jpg.

A row that holds several things opens with Enter, or the arrow at its left. In a row of things deleted together, you can select one of them and restore it alone. Something inside a deleted album or folder cannot come back without the rest: the pane says "Inside what was deleted; restoring puts back all of it."

Restore all, in the bar at the top of the page, puts back everything in the trash at once, without asking.

Delete something for good

  1. On the Trash page, select one or more rows.
  2. Choose Delete for good, or press Delete.
  3. The dialog Delete for good? says, for one row: "This item and everything derived from it is removed for good. This cannot be undone." Choose Delete for good, or Cancel.

Empty trash, in the bar at the top, does the same for everything in the trash, and its dialog's button reads Empty trash. "Everything derived from it" is what EiN made from a file: its thumbnails, faces, transcript and the rest.

Something in the trash still takes up its space on disk, so removing it for good frees that space.

How long things stay in the trash

Things stay for the number of days set by Days in the trash, CFG_TRASH_RETENTION_DAYS: 30 unless the administrator changed it, under Settings → System, in the category Trash. An empty Trash page gives the number: The trash is empty, "What you delete waits here for 30 days, in case you want it back."

The days count from when you deleted it; for things deleted together, from the last time something was added to that row. EiN clears out what is older when it starts and every 12 hours after that, so something can stay up to half a day past its time. Once it is cleared, nothing in EiN brings it back: EiN has no backup of its own; see Your data: backup, moving and removing.

The trash in the terminal

The trash command works on the same trash as the page:

# in EiN's terminal
trash put "/archive/albums/Sailing in the archipelago/IMG_0412.jpg"
trash list
trash restore 01J9Z8Q4R7XKD3V5B2N6M0PA1C
trash empty --older-than 7

trash list prints one line per row: its id, when it was deleted and where it came from.

01J9Z8Q4R7XKD3V5B2N6M0PA1C  2026-10-22T09:14:03.120Z  /archive/albums/Sailing in the archipelago/IMG_0412.jpg

trash restore takes that id and answers Restored and the path. trash empty --older-than 7 removes for good what was deleted more than 7 days ago; trash empty with no id and no --older-than empties your whole trash, for good.

From the terminal

Besides config and trash above, these commands do in the terminal what this page does in the browser:

Command What it does
user-info <username> Shows an account's display name, numbers, e-mail address and description.
user-update <username> --name=… --description=… Changes your display name and description, as Save changes does.
user-passwd <username> --old-password=… Changes your password; it asks for the new one with Password:. A password typed on the command line can end up in your shell history, so the Security tab is the better way.
star <path>, star -d <path>, star list Hearts a picture, takes the heart away, or lists your favourites. 
# in EiN's terminal
user-info helen
user-update helen --name='Helen Okafor' --description="Peggy's daughter, in Wellington"
star "/archive/albums/Sailing in the archipelago/IMG_0412.jpg"
User: Helen Okafor
UID: 1003
GID: 1003
E-mail: helen@example.com
Description: Peggy's daughter, in Wellington
User 'helen' updated
Starred /archive/albums/Sailing in the archipelago/IMG_0412.jpg

When something goes differently

What you see Why What to do
There is no Me in the account menu, and your picture is letters Your account is not linked to a person in the archive. Ask an administrator to link it, as under Your picture and the person you are.
Update password stays greyed out A field is empty, or the two new passwords differ. Fill in all three, and type the new password the same way twice.
A preference you saved does not show in another tab That tab loaded your preferences before you saved. Reload the tab.
echo $CFG_USER_… prints an empty line You never changed that setting, or the terminal was open before you did. Open a new terminal tab; config list --scope user shows every value, defaults included.
There is no heart in the viewer and no Favourite button You are not signed in. Sign in.
Something you deleted is not in your trash Someone else deleted it, and it is in their trash; or it was deleted for good; or its days ran out. Ask the person who deleted it. Nothing brings back what was deleted for good.
Restoring fails with a message that ends in is gone The folder or album it came from is no longer there. Restore that album or folder first, if it is in the trash, then restore the picture.
