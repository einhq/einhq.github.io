Dates and places of pictures

Every picture in EiN gets a date when anything can tell it one, and a place when it was taken with GPS or someone gives it one. This page is for family members who fix when and where pictures were taken: where EiN finds both, how to correct them, and what happens to scans and to pictures nobody has dated.

Where you see a picture's date and place

Open a picture in the viewer. On a computer the date and the place sit in the bar under the picture; on a phone the date is centred over the top of the picture and the bar at the foot keeps the place.

  • The date is written as precisely as it is known: "Saturday, 16 June 1956, 14:03:11" for a full time, "1975" for a picture known only to a year, "August 1968" for one known to a month. A picture with no date shows none.
  • Point at the date for a tooltip. It names where the date came from (the time source), any note someone left with it, and the time in your own time zone when the picture was taken in another ("… your time").
  • The place is the name of the location the picture is linked to, else its street address, else its city and country.

The details panel (Details (i), or i; on a phone, swipe up) shows more. Its place section has the location's name as a link, the street address and a small map with Enlarge map. The Details fold at its foot holds Taken at (the date exactly as stored, with its time zone), Taken at (UTC) and Time source. Viewing photos, videos and recordings covers the rest of the panel.

In an album the pictures are laid out in sections by day. One known only to a year sits under a heading with only the year; one with no date comes after the dated ones, under Undated. A person's, location's or device's Media tab lists undated pictures after the rest too. The Media feed lists pictures by date, so it leaves out a picture that has none.

Which date a picture gets

EiN looks at up to five sources for a picture's date and takes the first one that has it. The Time source says in words which one it was.

Order Source Time source reads What it is
1 Set by hand entered by hand A date someone gave with Set time or file-set-time. It always wins, and it may be as vague as a year.
2 A sidecar or Google Photos from Google Photos or a sidecar A date a photo editor or scanning program wrote into an XMP sidecar, or a date someone changed in Google Photos before exporting.
3 GPS from GPS The GPS receiver's clock, the one source that knows the exact moment.
4 The camera's clock from the camera clock The date and time the camera or phone wrote into the file, or a video's recording time.
5 The file name from the file name A date the name spells, such as IMG_20190601_123456.jpg, for files from a staging category whose names count as dates.

When none of them has a date, the picture is undated. EiN never dates a picture by when it was uploaded or when the file was last changed.

A sidecar is a small file beside a picture that says things about it: the JSON Google Photos writes for each photo of a Takeout export, or the .xmp file a photo editor writes. EiN pairs sidecars with their pictures as they arrive in staging, in every kind of category but Messaging and Screenshots. Getting your photos in and Supported files say which names it understands.

A few rules decide the close cases:

  • A GPS time far from the camera's clock is ignored. More than 26 hours apart, the GPS time is taken for an old position the receiver kept reporting, and the camera's clock dates the picture.
  • Google Photos' time counts as a person's date only when it differs. More than 26 hours from the camera's clock (or, for a picture without one, from when it was uploaded to Google), it is a date someone set there, and it comes second on the ladder. Closer than that, it is Google's reading of the camera's clock: the picture keeps the camera's time, Google's time tells EiN which time zone the clock was in, and Time source reads "from Google Photos or a sidecar". A GPS time still comes first then, and a camera that recorded its own time zone keeps "from the camera clock".
  • File names count only where a staging category says so, and only the names a phone, a messenger, a screenshot tool or a desktop uploader writes. A name that dates a picture before 1995 is not believed, and a scanner's running number such as IMG_20190601_0001.jpg is no date.
  • A scanner's clock never dates a picture once the scanner is registered as a device with Scanner or digitizer turned on (see Devices and GPS tracks). A GPS time in the file still counts.

Date scans of old prints and slides

A scanner writes the day of the scan into the file, and a scanning app on a phone adds where the scan was made. Neither says when the picture was taken. When files arrive in a staging category of the kind Scans, EiN marks them as scans and sets aside the scanner's clock, the GPS time and position, the file name, and a Google Photos time that agrees with them. A scan is dated only by a date a person gives it: with Set time, with file-set-time, in an XMP sidecar, or by changing it in Google Photos. Until then it is undated, rather than dated to the day it was scanned.

Time source on a scan adds "a scan: its scanner’s clocks are not when it was taken". The mark goes with the file when it is filed into an album, so the scan stays undated there until someone dates it.

To bring in a box of scans:

  1. Open the Create menu (the plus in the top bar) and choose Import media.
  2. Under Into which category?, choose your Scans category, or New category with What it holds set to Scans and a name under Called, such as "Box 3".
  3. Press Choose files or Choose a folder, then Upload, which shows the number of files.

Scans that already arrived in the Inbox can be moved: on the Inbox's Files tab, open the folder's ⋯ menu, choose Move to category…, pick the Scans category and press Move here. EiN dates the moved files again by the scans' rules, in the background. Sorting in staging explains categories and the Files tab.

Note

A scan uploaded straight into an album, or copied into one over SFTP, is not marked: no staging category's rules apply there. For those, register the scanner as a device with Scanner or digitizer turned on. Saving that setting re-dates the files already linked to it and says "Removed scanner dates from N linked files."

Helen Okafor scanned the family's slides of "Cornwall, summer 1968" in March 2012. Uploaded into a Scans category, they wait undated until she gives them a date. Uploaded straight into an album from an unregistered scanner, they would all show March 2012.

In a Scans category the Sort tab keeps the scans in runs, by folder and file name, under To sort, dated or not. Once most of a run has dates set by hand, its name gains their years, and a run splits where two neighbouring scans were given different years.

Time rules of staging categories

Each staging category has two time rules, which come with its kind (What it holds): whether its files are scans, and whether a date in a file name counts.

What it holds Treated as scans File names count as dates
Inbox no yes
Google Photos export no no
Scans yes no
Phone no yes
Camera no no
Messaging no yes
Screenshots no yes
Other no no
  • Files that move into a category with other rules are dated again by that category's rules, in the background. A move within a category, or between two with the same rules, changes nothing.
  • Category settings has no switch for the two rules. Changing What it holds there resets them, together with the category's other settings, to those of the kind you chose.
  • In the terminal, staging category <name> --digitized on|off and staging category <name> --filename-dates on|off change one rule and date the category's files again before they answer.
  • A date set by hand outranks both rules.

Give pictures that need a date a date

Staging shows what has no date. A category's card on the Staging overview, and the strip above the category's tabs, has a bar whose Needs a date part counts the pictures read without a date. On the category's Sort tab, the Needs a date lane holds groups of undated pictures, named like "Undated · Peggy's shoebox", and groups of pictures dated only to a year or a month, named like "1987 · Peggy's shoebox". In a Scans category the scans stay in their runs under To sort instead, as described above.

  1. Open Staging, then the category, then its Sort tab.
  2. Choose Needs a date in the row of lanes above the groups. The row appears when more than one lane has groups, each lane with its number of groups.
  3. Open a group: click it, or move to it with J and K.
  4. Press T, or open the group's ⋯ menu and choose Set the time…. To date only some of its pictures, select them first; T then sets the time of those.
  5. In the Set time dialog, set Start time and the order (see the next section), then press Set time on N files. A picture dated only to a year or a month keeps that date unless you click its row first (see the next section), so a group named like "1987 · Peggy's shoebox" needs a click on each row you want to date. Until you do, the button is off and the dialog says "These pictures are known only to a year or a month and keep that date. Click a row to give it a time."

EiN confirms with "The time is set on N photos of “”". Within a few minutes the organizer puts the pictures into groups by the dates you gave them: an event, a trip, or a year's Everyday lane. On a phone there are no keys; tap the group and use its ⋯ menu.

You can also file a Needs a date group as it is, undated, with New album…, Add to album… or the other decisions of the Sort tab. Its proposal is the category's album when the category has one; otherwise there is none.

Pictures already in the library have no lane. An album lists its undated pictures last, under Undated: select them there and use Set time. In the terminal, search --undated lists everything undated in the library that you may see.

Set the time of several pictures at once

Set time is in the selection bar that appears when you select pictures: on an album page, the Media page, a person's, location's or device's Media tab, and the Files tab in staging. On a narrow screen it is under Actions. It is offered only when you may change every selected picture. In staging's Sort tab, T opens the same dialog.

The dialog is titled Set time, with the number of files under it.

  • Order decides which picture comes first: Current time (the default; earliest first, undated last), Name (by file name, IMG_2 before IMG_10) or Manual. Drag a row by the grip at its left to reorder; dragging switches to Manual.
  • Start time is the time the first row gets. It starts at the earliest date in the batch, or at today's date and time when none of the pictures has one.
  • Each row shows the picture, its name, its current date or "No date", and after the arrow the time it will get.

The times are worked out from the top:

  1. The first row gets Start time.
  2. Each following row gets the time of the row before it plus the gap between their current times. Where either of the two has no date, or your order puts a later picture before an earlier one, the gap is one minute. Pictures taken in the same second stay in the same second.
  3. Changing a row's time pins it: that row keeps your time and the rows after it follow on from it. The round arrow at the end of a pinned row ("Unpin — follow the row above again") lets it follow the row above again.
  4. A picture known only to a year or a month keeps its date. Its row reads " — unchanged", with the tooltip "Keeping this date as it is. Give it a time to replace it.", and the rows after it follow the last row that took a time. Click it to give it a full time instead.

Press Set time on N files, where N counts the pictures that get a time; one that keeps its vague date is left out. Each picture's date becomes one entered by hand, with the time of day included, also for a picture that was known only to the day. Albums holding the pictures update their date range.

Warning

The dialog shows and writes times in your browser's time zone. For a picture taken in another time zone, such as on a trip abroad, the time after the arrow can differ by some hours from its current date before you change anything. Compare the rows before you press Set time on N files.

Some ways to use it:

  • The camera's clock was wrong, an hour behind after a trip or never set at all: select that camera's pictures, keep Current time, and set Start time to the right time of the first picture. The others move by the same amount and keep their spacing.
  • Scans of an album, in page order: select them, choose Name, and set Start time to the day. They spread a minute apart in scan order, so they stay in that order everywhere.
  • Undated pictures where you know the time of some: set Start time, then change the rows whose time you know. The rows between follow on from them.
  • One picture: select only that one.

Give a picture a vague date

A date can be as vague as it really is: a year ("1975"), a month ("August 1968") or a day. EiN keeps it that way and never adds a day or an hour nobody knew. The Set time dialog writes full times only, so a vague date is given in the terminal with file-set-time.

  1. Open the picture in the viewer and open the details panel.

  2. Open the Details fold at its foot and click the path under Path. A terminal tab opens, standing in the picture itself, so . names the picture.

  3. Type file-set-time, the picture and the date:

    # in EiN's terminal, opened from Path
file-set-time . 1968-08

    EiN answers with the date the picture has and where it came from:

    /archive/albums/Cornwall, summer 1968/scan0004.jpg: 1968-08 (manual)

The date can be 1968, 1968-08, 1968-08-14, 1968-08-14T15:30 or 1968-08-14T15:30:00, and the last two may end in a time zone, +01:00 or Z. The year must lie between 1826 and next year; anything else is refused with "file-set-time: not a capture time: ".

More forms, run in the folder that holds the pictures (cd .. from the picture):

# in EiN's terminal, in the album's folder
file-set-time scan0004.jpg 1968-08 scan0005.jpg 1968-08 --note "Peggy remembers the August heatwave"
for f in scan00*.jpg; do file-set-time "$f" 1968; done
file-set-time --clear scan0004.jpg
  • Several pictures go in one call as pairs of picture and date. If one date is wrong or one picture is missing, nothing is written.
  • --note keeps a reason with the date. It shows beside the time source, in the date's tooltip and under Time source.
  • --clear takes away a date set by hand, and its note. The picture falls back to the next source on the ladder, or to no date. The web interface has no button for this.

Time zones

A picture is shown at the time on the clock the photographer was reading. A photograph taken at noon in Wellington reads 12:00, whoever looks at it; when your own time zone differs, the date's tooltip adds the same moment in yours ("… your time").

EiN works out the time zone of a picture from:

  • the time zone the camera recorded in the file, as many phones do;
  • the difference between the GPS time and the camera's clock, rounded to whole hours;
  • the difference between Google Photos' time and the camera's clock, for a picture from a Takeout;
  • the Timezone of the registered device that took it, which knows the summer time of the year the picture was taken in.

A picture none of these places carries no time zone. It is shown at the camera's time without a "your time" line, and EiN reads it in the archive's own time zone, which is the time zone of the machine EiN runs on (TZ, under Environment variables).

When a camera's pictures come out hours off:

  • The camera's clock was set to another zone, for one bought abroad or kept on home time during a trip: give the camera's device the Timezone its clock was set to. For a registered camera, open its page, press Edit and set Timezone; saving re-dates the pictures linked to it and says "Re-dated N linked files." Register an unregistered one from the Devices page with its Timezone, then press Link existing media on its page, which links its pictures and updates their dates. Devices and GPS tracks has the details.
  • A phone's videos are off by exactly its time zone: turn on Video times in UTC for that phone. Phones keep their videos' recording time in UTC.
  • The clock was wrong by some amount: use Set time as described above.

A time from the Set time dialog carries no time zone and is read in the archive's time zone, so a picture from a trip abroad that you re-time loses its "your time" line. In the terminal you can give the zone with the time:

# in EiN's terminal, in the album's folder
file-set-time DSCF0412.JPG 1996-02-03T14:30+13:00

Where a picture's place comes from

A picture gets its place from the first of these that has one:

  1. A place set by hand, with Set location, in Edit file or with file-set-position. It replaces anything the file carries, and EiN reading the file again never puts the GPS position back over it.
  2. The GPS position in the file.
  3. The place in its sidecar: an XMP sidecar's, or a Google Photos Takeout's.

A scan's own GPS position is where it was scanned, so EiN ignores it, and a Takeout's place for a scan too, unless someone set that place in Google Photos.

Street addresses. EiN looks up each picture's position once in OpenStreetMap's Nominatim service, which turns coordinates into a street address. Only the coordinates are sent, never the picture. The address shows in the place section of the details panel, and in the viewer's bar when the picture is linked to no location. It can arrive a while after the picture: Nominatim answers one question a second, and a lookup that fails is asked again later.

Locations. When a picture gets a position, EiN links it to the location whose shape contains the point, the nearest one when several do. A shape is a circle or a polygon drawn for the location, or the circle a location gets from its address or from its pictures. A location that is only a point, because coordinates were typed or clicked when it was created, contains nothing, so no picture joins it by itself. When a location's shape changes, the library's pictures inside it are linked to it and those left outside are let go. The link gives the picture the location's name in the viewer and puts it on the location's Media tab. A link someone made by hand, or one brought in from an older archive, is never replaced. Locations and the map shows how to draw a location's shape.

A device's GPS track is drawn beside the pictures on the map and on an album's Map tab. EiN does not use it to give pictures a position.

Set the place of pictures

  1. Select the pictures in a grid: an album, the Media page, your favourites, a person's, location's or device's Media tab, the pictures behind a pile on the map, or the Files tab in staging.
  2. Press Set location in the selection bar. It is offered only when you may change every selected picture.
  3. The dialog asks "Where was this taken?", or "Where were these taken?" for several. Choose the place in one of two ways:
    • Under A known location ("Pick one, or click the map"), pick a location. The list holds the locations that have a place on the map, and picking one moves the point to it.
    • Under On the map ("Click where it was taken, or type the coordinates."), click the map, or type Latitude and Longitude.
  4. Press Set location. EiN confirms with "Location set on N files".

The hint at the foot of the dialog says what happens: "This replaces whatever position the files carry, for the map and for searching by location." Picking a known location gives the pictures that location's point. They are linked to the location, and show its name, when the point lies inside its circle or polygon.

Change or clear the place of one picture

  1. Open the picture in the viewer and open the details panel.
  2. Click the cog at the top of the panel and choose Edit. The Edit file dialog opens, with Description and the labels on one side and the place on the other, starting at the picture's point.
  3. To move the point, pick under A known location, click the map under On the map, or type Latitude and Longitude. The hint reads "The new location replaces the position the file carries, for the map and for searching by location."
  4. To take the place away, press Clear location. The hint reads "The file will have no location, for the map or for searching by location."
  5. Press Save.

The cog menu has Edit when you may change the picture. Clear location removes the point, whether the GPS or a person put it there, and the picture's link to a location, however it was made. EiN reads a file's GPS position when it takes the file in, so the picture stays without a place. To clear several pictures at once, use file-set-position with --clear in the terminal.

In the terminal

The terminal does the same work with commands, and some of it only there:

Task Command
Give pictures a date, as vague as it is file-set-time <file> <date> [<file> <date> …] [--note <text>]
Take away a date set by hand file-set-time --clear <file>…
List what is undated in the library search --undated
Set the place of pictures file-set-position <file>… --lat=<n> --lng=<n>
Clear the place of pictures file-set-position <file>… --clear
Link a picture to a location by hand ln -s -f /archive/locations/<location> '<file>/location'
Look up a picture's street address again, as an administrator geocode-position -f '<file>/position'
Date a staging category's files again by its rules staging retime [<category>]
Change a category's time rules staging category <name> --digitized on|off or --filename-dates on|off

Write a negative coordinate with =, as in --lng=-5.066: on its own, -5.066 would be read as an option.

# in EiN's terminal, in the album's folder
file-set-position P1000412.JPG P1000413.JPG --lat=50.153 --lng=-5.066
Set location 50.153,-5.066 on /archive/albums/Peggy's Falmouth/P1000412.JPG
Set location 50.153,-5.066 on /archive/albums/Peggy's Falmouth/P1000413.JPG

When something goes differently

  • A scan shows the day it was scanned. It did not arrive in a Scans category, and its scanner is not registered as one. Give it its date with Set time, which outranks the scanner's clock; move its folder into a Scans category while it is still in staging; or register the scanner with Scanner or digitizer.
  • A picture has a date you did not expect. Look at Time source under Details. "from the file name" means its category reads dates from names; "from the camera clock" with a wrong year means the camera's clock was wrong.
  • All pictures from one camera are some hours off. Set the device's Timezone, or for a phone's videos turn on Video times in UTC, as described under Time zones.
  • Set time is not in the selection bar. You may not change every selected picture: select only pictures you may change, or ask whoever owns the others. Your favourites and the pictures behind a pile on the map do not offer Set time at all; select the pictures in their album instead.
  • The times after the arrow in Set time are hours off before you change anything. The pictures were taken in another time zone than your browser's; see the warning under Set the time of several pictures at once.
  • The street address still names the old street after you moved the point. EiN looks up an address once per picture and does not look again when the point moves. An administrator can look it up again in the terminal with geocode-position -f '<file>/position'.
  • A picture is on the map but has no street address. The lookup has not run yet, or the machine EiN runs on cannot reach nominatim.openstreetmap.org. It is asked again later.
  • The picture is not linked to the location you picked. That location is a single point, which contains nothing. Draw a circle or a polygon for it, as Locations and the map describes, and the pictures inside join it.
  • The picture still names its old location after you moved it. Its link was made by hand, and EiN never replaces such a link. In Edit file, press Clear location and Save, then open Edit file again and set the point.
  • file-set-time answers "not a capture time". Write the date as YYYY, YYYY-MM, YYYY-MM-DD, YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM or YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SS, with a year from 1826 to next year.
