Sorting in staging

Everything you upload waits in your own staging area until you decide where it goes. This page is for family members sorting their uploads into albums. It covers the categories that hold your files, the albums EiN suggests, the keys and buttons that file, discard or regroup those suggestions, and how to undo any of it.

What staging is

Pictures, videos and recordings you bring in do not go straight into the family's shared library. They land in your staging area, a private inbox that only you can see. The page says so under its title: "New media to sort before it joins the shared library. Only you can see it." How files get there (Upload, dragging files onto a page, SFTP, a phone's backup app over WebDAV) is on Getting your photos in.

While the files wait, EiN reads each one: its date, its place, the faces in it and its thumbnails. It also checks whether you already have a copy. Then the organizer sorts them into suggested albums, called groups on these pages: a birthday, a trip, a folder you named, a year of everyday pictures. You go through the groups and decide for each one:

  • file it as a new album;
  • file it into an album that is already in the library;
  • discard it to your trash.

Only filing makes the files visible to anyone else. Every decision can be undone.

Until a file is filed, the views of the whole library leave it out: the Media page and its counts, the map, On this day, search, and the number on a person's card on the People page. The faces and voices in it are found all the same, and count once the file is filed.

Note

Staging is only for people who are signed in. A visitor browsing without an account does not see it in the sidebar, and opening its address asks them to sign in.

Open staging

Open Staging in the sidebar, under Media. While you have only the Inbox, staging opens the Inbox's page directly. Once you have more than one category, it opens on the overview of all of them. A category's page leads back to the overview with ← Staging above its title.

The overview

The overview lists your categories as cards, two side by side (one on a phone). Click a card to open the category's page; you upload from there. The header's one button is New category.

Each card shows:

  • its newest pictures, videos and recordings: nine of them, or four or one when it holds fewer, or a crossed-out picture when it holds none;
  • the category's name;
  • a pen, Edit;
  • a bar of its files by how far along they are: Being prepared, To sort, Needs a date and Other files (files that are not pictures, videos or recordings), or "Nothing here yet.";
  • how many files it holds and their size;
  • where the newest files are on their way in (below).

While files are on their way in, the overview's card for their category shows up to three stages. Hold the pointer over one to see what it means.

Stage What it means
N of M arriving Uploading from this browser
N being prepared, about <time> Their dates and places are being read; the groups they join may still change
N being grouped Read, and waiting for the organizer to put them in a group

The organizer groups new files within a few minutes of their arrival.

When a folder that arrived in the Inbox looks like it came from one source, a blue box on the Inbox's Files tab says so, for example "“Takeout” looks like Google Photos", with the two strongest clues.

  • Move to category… opens the move dialog, where you choose the category it goes into.
  • New category… opens the New category dialog with the source's name under Name, here Google Photos; Create and move “Takeout” makes the category and moves the folder into it.
  • The ✕ ("Keep it here, stop asking") leaves the folder where it is and stops asking.

Nothing moves by itself.

On the Inbox's Sort tab, a card heads the groups when GPX files wait for the device they belong to: it lists them, and a button per device gives their routes to that device's track (see GPX files in staging). Another card appears when a family tree (a GEDCOM file) waits; its Review and import opens it (see Look over a family tree before it is imported). A GPX or GEDCOM file uploaded into any category does not stay among its files but waits in the staging area, so the cards are on the Inbox's Sort tab whichever category it came through (and on the only category's while you have just one).

Categories

A category keeps one source together: a Google Photos export in one, a box of scanned prints in another, your phone in a third. Each category is a folder of your staging area. Categories are only buckets to sort in: every category's files are grouped, dated and processed the same way, so which one a file lands in changes nothing but where you find it.

The Inbox is always there and cannot be removed. Uploads land in it unless you choose another category.

Make a category

  1. On the overview, choose New category in the header.
  2. Fill in Name, the name the pages show, for example Box 3. When you came from a folder's hint (New category…), it holds the source's name, such as Google Photos.
  3. Choose Create. The new category's page opens on its Files tab.

Its folder is made from the name, files/box-3 for Box 3, or files/box-3-2 when that is taken. The folder never changes, even when you rename the category.

Change or remove a category

Open Edit with the pen on the category's card, or with Edit in its page's header. Name is the name the pages show; its folder stays as it is. Choose Save to keep a new name.

To remove a category, choose Remove at the left of the dialog's buttons. Only an empty category can be removed: in one that still holds files, Remove is greyed out and the dialog says "Only an empty category can be removed: move or discard its files first." The Inbox cannot be removed, and its dialog has no Remove.

A category's page

The page's title is the category's name. Above it, ← Staging leads back to the overview. On the Files tab inside a folder, the title is the folder's name and the link above it leads one level up: ← 1975 over the folder summer in 1975, or ← Box 3 over a folder at the top of the category.

The header has Upload, which adds files to the category's folder for today, Edit, and under ⋮ Keyboard shortcuts. Inside a folder, the ⋮ menu also has Move to folder…, Move to category… and Discard… for the folder on screen (see Files: folders and moving). You can also drop files anywhere on the page, on any tab, and they go into the category on screen; a folder dropped there keeps its structure.

The page has five tabs, and a category opens on Files:

Tab What it is for Its number
Files The category's folders and files Files held
Sort Deciding the suggested albums, one after another Groups
Duplicates Exact copies and look-alikes, to resolve Exact copies
Done What was decided here, with Undo Files filed and discarded
Info How to get files into it: its folder, SFTP and a phone backup

The Duplicates tab has a page of its own: Duplicates.

Sort: the stream of suggested albums

The Sort tab lists the category's groups one after another, oldest first. Every picture, video and recording that has been read is in exactly one group.

Until the first groups are made, the tab says No groups yet, with "Groups appear as your files are prepared: N still to go." or "Groups appear here once uploaded files have been prepared and organized, within minutes of their arrival." Show the files opens the Files tab.

How the stream looks

At the right of the tabs, the page's header has:

  • "◌ N applying" while decisions you made are still being carried out;
  • the lanes, each with its number of groups, when more than one lane has groups; otherwise "N photos to decide in M groups";
  • Oldest first or Newest first. Groups without a date come last either way.

On a phone the lanes and the order are in one button there.

Each group is a card with its name and all its pictures, in small rows. Click a picture to see it in the viewer; that leaves the open group as it is. One group is open, the one the keys act on, its card's edge drawn in the accent colour. Click a group's name or the space around its pictures, or press J and K, to open the next or previous one. Opening a group changes only its edge, so nothing on the page moves. A group too large to show at once says "Too many pictures to show at once." with Page through them.

To step through a group's pictures and select them, click the chevron in its card's top right corner, or press Space; the same button, or Space again, brings back the one grid. The arrow keys, [, ] and O expand the open group first.

An expanded group shows its pictures in a grid per day. A group that spans more than 40 days, such as a year of everyday pictures, shows a grid per month instead, with the month's days as small buttons above it; click one to select that day's pictures. Pictures without a date come last, under No date. Below an expanded group, "Next:" and a name say which group follows.

A group's header shows its name, then its dates, how many photos and how many days it holds, for example "Christmas Eve" over "24 Dec 1975 · 86 photos": the dates a group's name ends with are left to the second line. Badges may follow:

Badge Meaning
Trip Two or more days away from home
Still indexing "Some of these are still being indexed, so the group may still change"
Yours "You edited this group, so the organizer leaves it as it is"

Point at a group's name and a pen appears beside it: click it, press E, or double-click the open group's name, to rename the group in place. The name you edit is the one shown, without dates; Enter keeps it, Esc gives it up.

In its top right corner every card has:

  • Add to existing, with a dimmed number after it: how many library albums EiN found for the group, at 50% or more. It opens a dialog that lists them (see Add to existing). Before the group is compared with the library it says Add to existing alone.
  • Create album (N), which opens the Create album dialog with the suggested name filled in: a new album from the group. On a phone it is the button under the group's pictures.
  • a ⋮ menu with Open as album page, Not a group, Set the time (T) and Discard (X).
  • the chevron that shows the group's pictures by day, or in one grid again.

On the open group, when you have selected some of its pictures or stepped to a day, Create album, Add to existing and the menu act on those pictures, as the keys do: the menu then says, for example, Discard 12 photos. Before Add to existing, every group has its proposal as a button, what Enter does on it, when the proposal is something other than a new album: for example Add to “Sailing in the archipelago”, File 3 matched days · 120 photos or Keep the library copies · 4 photos go. Rest the pointer on Add to “…” to see how sure it is.

Where you were is kept in the page's address, and per category in your browser, so a reload or a link brings you back to the same group. When a stream holds 30 groups or more, a time rail at its right edge brings the groups from a chosen date up.

The lanes

The stream is divided into lanes. Each lane is a view of some of the category's groups. With groups in two lanes or more, you choose one at the right of the tabs. The stream opens on To sort, or on the first lane with groups when To sort has none, and moves on to the next lane once you have decided the last group of the one you are in.

Lane What it holds
To sort Events, trips and folders a person named
Everyday Each year's ordinary days, as one group per year, or per month in a busy year
Needs a date Pictures with no date, or dated only to a year or a month
Clutter Screenshots and images saved from messages

Events and trips are named by what, where and when, each part only when it is known: "Midsummer Eve · Harbour cottage · 21 Jun 2019", "With Anna and Bo · 3 Aug 2014", "Lisbon · 4–9 May 2015". The what is a folder's name, a holiday, a birthday or the guests at home. The where is a place you named, a point of interest, the town of a trip, or for an outing near home its neighbourhood, unless most of that year's pictures were taken there. A group with neither says when in the week it was ("Saturday evening", "Weekend"), or only its dates.

Home, which trips are away from and outings are near, is decided per year. It is the home on your person's Homes tab that covers most of that year, when your account is linked to your person; else the spot where at least a quarter of the category's located pictures that year were taken; else the year before's home. A home without dates covers every year, so the pictures can overrule it: in a year with 20 or more located pictures, none of them within a kilometre of it, that spot is home instead. A home with dates is always taken as given.

Groups in the Everyday, Needs a date and Clutter lanes are named after what they hold: "Everyday · 2014", "Screenshots and saved images · 2019", "Undated · box-3", or "1987 · box-3" for pictures known only to a year. They are collections, not events, so they are not split, merged or called "not a group". To take some pictures out of one, make them a group of their own with New group on a day, or move them with <, > and G (see Change the groups).

  • Everyday. Each day of an Everyday group is compared with the library's albums. A day that matches one shows it in its header, for example "→ Christmas at the cottage 72%", with File, which files that day's pictures into the album. Select the day with [ or ] and Enter does the same. A closed Everyday group says how many of its days match an album, for example "→ 3 days match an album".
  • Needs a date. Give the pictures a time with T (Set the time…): the Set time dialog takes one start time, and the pictures keep their spacing or follow a minute apart. A picture known only to a year or a month keeps that date unless you click its row to give it a time. Once dated, the organizer puts them with the events and days they belong to. The dialog is explained on Dates and places of pictures.
  • Clutter. Its proposal is Discard.

What Enter proposes

Each group has a proposal: what Enter, or the button on the group, does with it. Each proposal is sure (EiN is confident), likely (probably right; have a look) or unsure (a starting point). Resting the pointer on a group's Add to “…” button says which.

The button says what happens:

Button When it is proposed
Add to “…” A library album you may add to matches the group well (likely or sure)
Create album “…” No album fits: a new album under the group's name. A folder a person named is proposed as a new album under the folder's name, sure.
Keep the library copies · N photos go Most of the group's photos are copies of photos already in the library; the copies go and the rest of the group stays to be sorted
Discard Clutter
File N matched days · M photos An Everyday group some of whose days match a library album you may add to; each matched day goes into its album and the rest stays
"Nothing is proposed for it: …" Nothing to propose, for example an Everyday group with no matched days

A folder a person named goes into an existing album only when the match is sure. Nothing is ever filed by a match on its own: you decide every time.

File a group

  1. Open the group: click it, or press J or K until it is open.
  2. Look at the proposal under the group's name, or on its button.
  3. Press Enter or click the button.
    • For an existing album, the photos are filed at once.
    • For a new album, the Create album dialog opens with the suggested name under Name, without the dates the group's name ends with. Change it if you like, and choose Create album.
  4. The group leaves the stream and the next one opens in its place. A green message says, for example, "86 photos filed in “Christmas at the cottage”", with Undo.

Instead of the proposal you can choose another way, with a button of the card or a key:

Choice Key What it does
Create album N The Create album dialog, with the suggested name filled in: a new album from the group, shared as your new albums are.
Add to existing A The albums EiN found for the group, with some of their pictures, and a search for any other album you may add to; Move here files into one (see Add to existing).
A suggestion 1 2 3 Files the group at once into the first, second or third album of its Add to existing.

Filing moves the files out of your staging into the album. Everything EiN has read about them goes along: dates, places, faces and thumbnails. Text in them, such as a letter or a sign, is read once they are filed. A new album is shared the way your new albums are, as the administrator set it up (see Sharing). Filed into an existing album, the files are shared the way that album is. An album you may only look at is refused before anything moves: "You can't add to “Christmas 1975”."

File part of a group

You do not have to file a group whole.

  1. Press [ or ] to step to a day (or a month) of the open group, or select pictures the way you do in an album (see Albums). With the arrow keys on a picture, Space selects it.
  2. Enter, N, A and X, and the open group's Create album, Add to existing and ⋮ menu, act on those pictures alone. On a day matched to an album, Enter adds it there. Filing the matched days and keeping the library copies are for the whole group, so on a day with no matched album, or on selected pictures, such a group has no proposal: Enter says "Nothing is proposed for it: create an album, add it to one, or discard it."
  3. The rest of the group stays where it is.

With pictures selected, New group of N pictures under the open group makes them a group of their own instead. The bar at the foot of the page then says, for example, "12 photos selected", with Split the group here (in a lane, New group), Move to “…” for the group above and the one below, and Move to a group… (see Change the groups).

Add to existing

Add to existing on a card opens a dialog headed "Add “…” to an existing album" and what it would file, for example "86 photos". It lists the library albums EiN found for the group at 50% or more, best first; one less likely than that is not worth looking at, and is left out. Each shows its name, a percentage, its dates and size, the reasons it was suggested as a list of points, and a row of its own pictures, those taken nearest the group's days. Click a picture to see it in the viewer, or the link beside the album's name to open the album in a new tab.

  • Move here files the group, or the pictures of it you chose, into that album, as 1 to 3 do. An album you may only look at is marked "view only" and cannot be chosen.
  • Not this album says the album is wrong for the group: see Not this album.
  • Find another album searches every album you may add to by name; each one found is shown the same way, with Move here.

When nothing fits, the dialog says "No album in the library fits it well." An Everyday lane's days are matched one by one: expand the lane to see them.

Not this album

When a suggested album is wrong, choose Not this album beside it in the Add to existing dialog. The message "“Christmas 1975” is no longer offered for “Christmas Eve · 24 Dec 1975”" confirms it. The album is not offered again for this group, or for any group made mostly of the same photos, until you undo it on the Done tab.

Discard a group

Discard (X, or Discard in the card's ⋮ menu) moves the group's photos to your trash as one entry on the Trash page: "86 photos moved to the trash". A group of 200 photos or more asks first, in a Discard? dialog. Nothing in staging is deleted outright: Undo in the message, Z, the Done tab, or Restore on the Trash page brings the photos back (see Your profile, favourites and trash).

Decide several groups at once

On a computer you can select several groups and decide them together:

  • tick the box in a group's header, or Ctrl-click (Cmd-click on a Mac) its name or a closed group's pictures;
  • hold Shift to select everything from where the selection started;
  • press Shift+J or Shift+K to select on to the next or previous group;
  • or draw a box around them from an empty part of the page (hold Shift to add, Alt to take away, Esc to stop).

With two or more groups selected, a bar at the foot of the page says how much, for example "3 groups · 412 photos selected", with:

  • Merge N groups, with two or more groups of the To sort lane selected whole: merges them into the first of them in the stream, which keeps its name;
  • Create suggested albums: each group becomes a new album of its own, under its suggested name without the dates.

One group selected has no bar: its card's Create album and the rest act on it.

Its ✕ clears the selection. The keys decide on them too:

  • Enter accepts each group's proposal, after the Accept the proposals? dialog has asked which to take (Every proposal, Likely and sure or Only sure);
  • N makes one album of everything selected;
  • A files it into an album you find by name, in the Add to an album dialog;
  • X discards it after asking.

Esc clears the selection. A message shows the progress, for example "Filing 1,840 of 9,830 photos · 37 of 214 groups", with Cancel; when it is done, Undo all takes the whole run back.

You can also drag selected pictures or groups onto another group's header, which says what the drop would do as you arrive: it merges the groups into it, or moves the pictures there.

Change the groups

The organizer's groups are suggestions. When one is wrong, change it. Every change but a rename says so in a message with Undo, and every change is listed on the Done tab, where Undo takes it back.

To Do this What happens
Split a group Split here on a day or month, or S on a picture, a day or a selection That picture and the ones after it become a group of their own: "“A week in London” split in two". You cannot split before the first picture.
Move pictures to a neighbour Select them, then < or > They join the group above or below.
Move pictures to any group Select them, then G The Move to a group dialog: the five groups on each side, Find a group by name, or New group ("A group of their own, named by their dates").
Make a new group New group of N pictures under the open group; in a lane, New group on a day or S The pictures become a group of their own, named by their dates.

Break up a group Not a group in the card's ⋮ menu The Not a group? dialog: the photos go back to their lanes, Everyday and the like, and the organizer does not suggest them as a group again. No photo is removed.
Rename E, the pen beside the group's name, or double-click the open group's name Type the name and press Enter; Esc keeps the old one.
Keep a group exactly as it is Open as album page in the card's ⋮ menu, then ⋮ → Keep album as is The organizer leaves the group and its files alone. Let the organizer manage album hands it back; Sort in Staging returns to the group in the stream.

You can also drag pictures onto another group's header to move them there.

What the organizer remembers

The organizer looks at a category again a couple of minutes after its files change. Groups can change while files are still arriving and being read, which is what Still indexing warns of. The stream follows along: a group that changes is read again, and one that is removed disappears.

What you decide is kept through every one of those runs:

  • Your changes of the groups (splits, moves, new groups, merges, Not a group, names) are kept on every run until you undo them. On the Done tab all but a rename carry the badge kept: "The organizer keeps this change on every run until it is undone".
  • A group whose pictures you changed by hand shows Yours, and the organizer leaves it as it is.
  • Not this album keeps that album away from those photos until you undo it.

If the organizer changed a group while you were deciding on it, your decision is refused with "it changed while you were deciding; look again". The group comes back with Retry and Dismiss; look at it again before you decide.

Files: folders and moving

The Files tab, the one a category opens on, shows the category one folder at a time. Subfolders are cards with the number of entries in each; click one to go into it. The link above the page's title leads one level up (see A category's page). At the end of the tab row, Media and Nodes choose what the tab shows:

  • Media shows every picture, video and recording in the folder and its subfolders, by day. Select pictures to use Add to album, Set time, and, when you may change them all, Set location and Label.
  • Nodes lists every entry of the folder, whatever its type (a PDF, a sidecar file), as the Nodes page does, filling the page below its header: details, list or icons, sorted by a click on a column's name, a folder opened in place with its arrow or gone into with a double click or Enter, and a panel beside it that previews the entry you are on. It stays within the category: Up stops at it and Home goes back to it. Select entries with a click, Ctrl or Shift and a click for more, or Ctrl+A (on a phone, press and hold one, then tap the others); the top of the panel then offers Move to folder…, Move to category… and Discard… for them, and Delete discards them too.

To move or discard a folder or files, use the ⋯ on a folder's card, the page's ⋮ menu for the folder you are in, or the panel beside a selection in Nodes:

  • Move to folder… moves it to another folder of the same category.
  • Move to category… moves it into another category (offered when you have more than one). It first leaves the groups of its old category.
  • Discard… moves it to your trash.

In the move dialog, choose the Category, step into a folder, and optionally type a name under Into a new folder here. The line under it says where it goes, for example "Into Scans › box-3. Links to them, in albums and suggested groups, follow them." Choose Move here.

The Discard dialog says: "They go to your trash, kept together as one entry on the Trash page. Undo, here or on the Done tab, puts them back where they were." Choose Discard; the message has Undo.

When a folder holds no pictures, videos or recordings, Media says so, adding "Nodes shows every file, whatever its type." when it holds other files or folders. An empty category says "Nothing in Box 3 yet" (with its own name) and offers Upload, which picks files into it.

Info: getting files in

The Info tab says how to get files into the category besides Upload:

  • its folder, files/box-3, and the path to copy into over SFTP, the way for tens of thousands of files; see SSH and SFTP and Staging and importing for administrators;
  • the address and folder a phone's backup app sends into, and in the EiN app Back up this phone here, which has the app's photo backup send into it; see Back up your phone automatically.

Done: what you decided, and Undo

The Done tab lists what was decided in the category, newest first, 50 at a time (Show older for more). At the top: "N filed · M discarded". Everything shows every decision; Regrouping shows only the changes you made to the groups.

Each row shows when it happened and what it was: "86 files → Christmas at the cottage" with the album as a link, "12 files discarded", "Split “A week in London”", "“Christmas Eve · 24 Dec 1975” is not for Christmas 1975", and so on, with the groups it touched. Badges: only you for a private album, kept for a change the organizer keeps, in part for a decision that stopped part-way. Each row has Undo, or "undone" and the time once it is undone.

Undo puts things back:

  • filed files move back into staging, and an album the decision made is removed once it is empty;
  • discarded files come back from the trash, as long as they are still there;
  • the groups get their pictures back, and a change of the groups stops applying;
  • Not this album gives the group its candidates back.

The message says "Undone:" and what was undone. A file that was moved after the decision stays where it is: "Undone in part: some files have moved since, and stay where they are".

In the Sort tab, Z undoes the decisions of this visit, newest first; one still waiting to be carried out is cancelled instead. With nothing left, it says "Nothing to undo from this visit; the Done tab lists every decision."

On a phone

On a phone the Sort tab has no keys and no selecting of several groups. Tap a group to open it. Each closed group has its proposal (or Create album…) as a button under its pictures. Its header has the pen to rename it, the Add to existing button, and the ⋮ menu with Open as album page, Not a group, Set the time and Discard. More on EiN on a phone.

If something goes differently

What you see What it means What to do
No groups yet The files are still being read, or the organizer has not grouped them yet Wait a few minutes
Still indexing on a group Some of its pictures are still being read Decide later, or go ahead; the group may still change
"it changed while you were deciding; look again" The organizer changed the group after you opened it Look at the group, then Retry, or Dismiss
"You can't add to “…”." The album is one you may only look at Choose another album, or ask whoever owns it
"Nothing is proposed for it: …" EiN has nothing to suggest Choose yourself: a new album, any album, or discard
Remove is greyed out in Edit The category still holds files Move or discard its files first
"Undone in part: …" Some files were moved after the decision Look for them in the place they were moved to
