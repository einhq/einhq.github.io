Viewing photos, videos and recordings

The viewer shows one photo, video or recording at a time, large, with what EiN knows about it beside it. This page is for everyone who looks at pictures in EiN, on a computer or on a phone: how to open the viewer and move through files, what the details panel tells you, and how to work with faces, text in pictures, playback, transcripts and slideshows.

Open the viewer

Click any picture in a grid: an album, the Media page, a person's Media tab, your Favourites, a search result. The picture grows out of its tile and fills the screen over the page. With the keyboard, move to a tile with Tab and press Enter or Space. While you are selecting pictures, a click ticks a tile instead of opening it; Albums explains selecting.

The viewer walks the list you opened it from, in that list's order. A search hit on something said in a video or a recording opens it at the moment the words are spoken; see Search and the finder.

Each file in the viewer has its own address: the page's address with the file added in its fullscreen parameter (…?fullscreen=%2Fmedia%2F…), or, opened on its own, /media/ followed by the file's path. You can copy it from the browser's address bar and send it to someone who may see the file. Stepping to another file replaces the address instead of adding to the browser's history, so one press of the browser's Back button closes the viewer and returns you to the page underneath.

What the viewer shows

On a computer:

  • Top left: the close button (×).
  • Top right: the details button (ⓘ, tooltip Details (i)), which shows or hides the details panel, and beside it Chat, where the assistant module is installed, which opens the assistant in a drawer beside the picture. You can ask it about the picture you are looking at; see Chatting with the assistant.
  • Left and right edges: arrows to the previous and the next file.
  • The bar under the picture, from left to right:
    • where you are in the list, such as 24 of 26. The Media page leaves this out, because it loads its feed a part at a time and does not know the whole count;
    • the date and time the picture was taken, such as Saturday, 27 December 2025, 13:16:18;
    • the place: the name of the location the picture is linked to, or else the street address read from its GPS position;
    • the heart, Favourite (s), when you are signed in;
    • Start slideshow;
    • More (⋮).

The date under the picture

The date is shown as precisely as it is known, and no more. A picture known only to the year shows 1975, one known to the month shows March 2004, and neither is given an invented day or hour.

Point at the date to see where it came from: from the camera clock, from GPS, entered by hand, from Google Photos or a sidecar or from the file name. A scan adds a scan: its scanner’s clocks are not when it was taken. A picture taken in another time zone is shown at the time on the photographer's clock, and the tooltip adds the same moment in your own time, such as 27 Dec 2025, 01:16 your time. Dates and places of pictures explains which date a picture gets and how to change it.

The More menu

More (⋮) at the right end of the bar holds what you do to the view rather than to the file:

Row What it does
Pin faces / Unpin faces (f) Shows every face box with its name, or hides them again. It reads No faces, greyed out, when none were found. For photos and videos.
Show text / Hide text (t) Lays the text read from the photo over it. Shown only when text was read.
Edit text / Add text Opens the text for writing by hand. Shown on photos you may change.
Fullscreen / Exit fullscreen Gives the viewer the whole screen.
Download Saves the original file to your device.

The file name at the top of the details panel downloads the original too. To download several files at once, select them in the grid and use Download in the selection bar.

Move between files and close the viewer

  • Next file: →, the arrow at the right edge, or swipe left on a touch screen.
  • Previous file: ←, the arrow at the left edge, or swipe right.

The list goes round: after the last file comes the first again, and before the first comes the last.

To close the viewer, press × at the top left, press Esc, use the browser's Back button, or on a phone swipe down on the picture. The picture shrinks back into its tile, so you see where you were in the grid.

Esc first closes whatever lies over the picture: the enlarged map, the text dialog or Edit faces. During a slideshow it stops the slideshow. Only then does it close the viewer.

Zoom in on a photo

Photos and videos zoom; recordings do not, and nothing zooms during a slideshow.

On a phone or tablet

The viewer takes its phone layout on any touch screen, and in any browser window 768 pixels wide or narrower.

  • The date sits in a dark pill centred over the top of the picture, with the time beneath it.
  • The bar under the picture keeps only the position in the list and the place.
  • ⋮ in the top right corner holds Favourite, Start slideshow, the rows of the More menu and Chat.
  • The details panel is closed when the viewer opens. Held upright, swipe up on the picture to pull up the details sheet over the bottom half, and swipe down on it to put it away. Held sideways, the details open as a panel on the right: press Show details beside ⋮, or swipe up, and swipe the panel to the right to close it.
  • Edit faces is not offered on a phone held upright.
Gesture What it does
Swipe left / right Next / previous file
Swipe up Open the details
Swipe down Close the details; with the details closed, close the viewer
Pinch, or double-tap Zoom a photo
Drag with one finger on a zoomed photo Look around it
Tap a face Open the person's page, or name an unnamed person; pin the faces to see the boxes

A swipe that starts at the very edge of the screen is left to the phone itself, so its own Back gesture closes the viewer.

Turning the phone sideways makes the viewer fullscreen, and turning it upright again leaves fullscreen. An iPhone has no fullscreen for web pages, so there the viewer stays as it is. EiN on a phone covers the rest of the interface on a small screen.

The details panel

On a computer the details panel opens on the right each time you open the viewer. Press the details button (ⓘ) or i to close it while you look; it stays closed until you close the viewer. On a phone you pull it up, as described above.

Each part of the panel is a row with an icon at its left. A row appears only when it has something to show. From the top:

Row What it shows
The file The file name, which downloads the original, and its size. Under it a line of facts: 3200 × 2400 · 7.7 MP · ISO 100 · 1/250 s; a video adds its length and frame rate, and a recording its length, kbps, kHz and mono or stereo. Then the sharing chip, which says who can see the file (Sharing), and the file's labels; a label opens a search for it (Media, Year and labels). The pencil at the right opens the pencil menu.
Description What someone wrote about the file.
Copies in staging Only for a file of yours still in your staging area: its exact copies and look-alikes. See Duplicates.
Transcript For videos and recordings with speech: what is said, line by line. See Read along with a transcript.
Camera The device that took it, linking to its page; Owned by whoever owned it then, with the years; Shot by the photographer, when someone has said who. Without a registered device, the make and model the file names. See Devices and GPS tracks.
Albums Every album the file is in, with its cover. A file can be in several albums without being copied.
People Everyone recognised or tagged in the picture, with their age that day. See People in the picture.
Place The location the file is linked to, as a link, the street address, and a small map. Enlarge map opens a large map of the spot.
Stack For a photo stacked with others in this album: N stacked photos, or Kept separate, with a thumbnail of each and Front on the one the album shows. Click a thumbnail to see it in the viewer. Make front, Keep separate and Allow stacking are there when you may change the album and every photo in the stack. See Albums.
Similar Up to 12 pictures that look most like this one, nearest first, leaving out the picture itself and the rest of its stack. Click one to walk these pictures in the viewer. Show all opens the Media page with every picture like it. Not shown for recordings.
Comments Comments on the file, with who wrote them and when. Everyone who can see the file reads them. If you may change the file, write one in Add a comment… and press Comment. The bin beside a comment of your own (Delete comment) removes it.
Details A folded section for the machine-written facts. See The Details section.

People in the picture

Each person has a badge with their age on the day the picture was taken: 69 years, or 8 months for a child under one. A ~ in front, as in ~44 years, marks an age that is approximate because only the year or month of the person's birth is known. There is no age when the picture has no date or the person has no birth date.

Pointing at a name lights up that person's face box on the picture, and pointing at a box lights up the name. Click a name to open the person's page.

Faces that recognition has grouped as one person but nobody has named yet are listed with a crop of the face and a placeholder name, such as Unknown 8F3C81. Click one to open the review over the viewer, where you name them or say they are someone you already have. People and faces describes the review and how recognition works.

The Details section

Details, at the bottom of the panel, unfolds the facts a reader seldom needs. Whether it is open is remembered in this browser, for the next picture and the next visit.

Row What it shows
Path Where the file is in EiN's tree, such as /archive/albums/cornwall-1968/img20120303_133900582.jpg. Click it to open a terminal tab at the file (The terminal). Visitors who are not signed in see the path as text.
Text in image The text read from the picture.
Text language The language the text was read in, and how sure the reading was. If you may change the file, a menu to choose another; see Read the text in a picture.
Type The kind of file, such as image/heic.
Taken at The capture time exactly as stored, such as 2025-12-27T13:16:18+13:00.
Taken at (UTC) The same moment in UTC.
Time source Where the date came from, in words, and any note left with it.
Created, Modified The file's created and modified times.
Hashes Fingerprints of the file's bytes, such as sha256.
EXIF tags Every tag the file carries, one per row, or No EXIF recorded.

Change a file from the pencil menu

The pencil at the top right of the details panel (Edit media) opens a menu. It is there when you may change the file, and for any signed-in reader for Add to album.

Row Who sees it What it does
Edit Readers who may change the file Opens Edit file: description, labels and location. See below.
Share Readers who may change the file Opens the Share dialog for this file. See Sharing.
Add to album Signed-in readers Opens Add to an album. Type part of an album's name to find it, or a name that is not taken to make an album by that name. The file is linked into the album, not copied; a file from your staging area moves into it instead. A message such as 1 added to Christmas in Wellington offers Undo.
Edit faces Readers who may change the file, on photos and videos, not on a phone held upright Draw, move or remove face boxes. See Draw or correct a face box.
Rotate left, Rotate right Readers who may change the file, on photos and videos Turns the file a quarter turn.
Mirror horizontally Readers who may change the file, on photos and videos Flips the file left to right. A tick shows while it is mirrored.

A turn or a flip is kept on the file, for everyone who sees it. EiN moves the face boxes with the picture and makes the smaller versions it shows again. A video is re-encoded by the media service, which takes a while; until it is done the video plays the old way round. To undo a turn, turn it back. The rotate and mirror rows are not offered for a file that cannot be decoded. There is no cropping and no colour editing in EiN.

Change the description, labels or location

  1. In the pencil menu, choose Edit. The Edit file dialog opens, with the file name under its title.
  2. In Description, write what the picture shows or what you know about it. Formatting such as bold and lists is kept.
  3. In Labels, pick a label in use or type one. A space starts the next label.
  4. To set where it was taken, pick a place in A known location, or click the spot in On the map, or type its coordinates there. The dialog warns: "The new location replaces the position the file carries, for the map and for searching by location."
  5. To take the location away, press Clear location. The dialog warns: "The file will have no location, for the map or for searching by location."
  6. Press Save. It stays greyed out until you have changed something; Cancel closes the dialog without saving.

The date is not changed here. To change when pictures were taken, select them in the grid and use Set time in the selection bar; see Dates and places of pictures.

Faces in the viewer

Face boxes stay hidden until you point at a face; then that face's box and name appear.

  • Pin faces in the More menu, or f, shows every box with its name. They stay pinned as you move from file to file, until you unpin them or reload the page. On a touch screen, where nothing can be pointed at, pinning is the way to see the boxes.
  • Click a named box to open that person's page.
  • Click the box of an unnamed person to open a small field, Who is this?. Type a name and press Enter or the tick. This gives them a name as a person of their own. If they are someone already in the archive, click their placeholder name in the details panel instead and choose the person in the review.
  • A face that belongs to nobody reads Unassigned.
  • A dashed box is a tag: "Tag: a box with a name, not used to recognise faces". It says who is there without helping to recognise them in other pictures. A box drawn by hand is a tag, and so is a found face whose box someone has moved or resized.

Draw or correct a face box

Use Edit faces when recognition missed a face, drew the box in the wrong place, or matched the wrong person. It is offered on photos and videos you may change, except on a phone held upright. On a video it pauses the video, and you draw on the frame on screen; the large play button leaves the picture while you draw, and the one on the bar still plays. A box you draw on a video belongs to that moment and shows from half a second before it to half a second after. A box already there can be chosen and removed or given to someone else, but not moved or resized.

  1. In the pencil menu, choose Edit faces. The bar under the picture reads Draw a new box or select a face, and every box shows.
  2. To add a face, drag a box over it. To change a face, click its box.
  3. Drag the box to move it, or drag one of the round handles on its edges and corners to resize it. The bar reads Adjust the box, change who it is, or remove it.
  4. Under the box, choose:
    • Done to save the box;
    • Cancel to drop your changes to it;
    • the person's name (tooltip Change who this is) to say who the face is, in the dialog described below;
    • Remove, then Confirm remove, to take the face away.

A box you drew is given an unidentified person of its own, and the review opens so you can name them or say they are someone already in the archive. A found face whose box you move or resize keeps its person, but becomes a tag and no longer helps recognition. To leave Edit faces, choose it again in the pencil menu, press × in the bar under the picture, or press Esc.

The dialog for a face's name is Who is this?, with Now marked as Helen Okafor or Not marked as anyone under the title:

  • Someone I know: pick the person in Who it is, then press Move.
  • Someone new: "the face becomes a new unidentified person, for you to name now or later". Press Move.
  • Let recognition decide: the face is matched again against everyone else. Press Match again. The bar then says what happened, such as Recognition matched the face to Ruby Wrenfield., Recognition made the face a new unidentified person. or Recognition left the face unassigned: too small or unclear to start a new person. This choice is not there for a tag, which recognition cannot match.

Changes you make to faces are decisions by a person, and later recognition runs leave them alone; see People and faces.

Read the text in a picture

EiN reads the text in photos: letters, postcards, signs, printed pages, and handwriting in English, Swedish and Finnish. The media service does the reading in the background. A picture still in your staging area is read once it is filed into an album.

  • Show text, in the More menu, or t, darkens the picture and lays the text over it in a typewriter face. Long text scrolls. The row is there only when text was read.
  • The text stays shown as you move from picture to picture, until you close the viewer, so you can page through a scanned letter. It is hidden during a slideshow.
  • Hide text, or t again, takes it away.

The text is also under Text in image in the Details section, and search finds pictures by it; see Search and the finder.

Correct the text, or add it by hand

  1. In the More menu, choose Edit text, or Add text when nothing was read. With the text shown, the pencil at its top right does the same.
  2. The dialog Text in the picture opens: the picture on the left (above, on a narrow screen), which you can zoom to read closely, and a text box on the right.
  3. Type or correct the text.
  4. Press Save, or Ctrl+Enter. Saving empty text removes it.

What you write replaces what was read. It is offered on photos you may change.

Read the text again in another language

When the reading came out wrong because EiN took the text for another language, choose the language yourself:

  1. Open Details at the bottom of the details panel.
  2. In Text language, choose the language. The menu starts at Automatic, which shows what was detected, such as Automatic: English (93%), and offers Swedish, Finnish, English, Danish, Norwegian, German and French.

EiN reads the picture again in that language, and the text follows after a while. Choose Automatic to let EiN tell the language again. Readers who may not change the file see the language without the menu.

Play a video

A video starts playing when it opens. If your browser blocks that, press play.

On a computer, click the picture to pause or play, and double-click it to go fullscreen. A large play button sits in the middle while the video is paused. The controls fade while the video plays and come back when you move the mouse.

Control Key What it does
Play / Pause Space or k Plays or pauses.
Back 10 s j Goes back ten seconds.
Forward 10 s l Goes forward ten seconds.
Previous chapter, Next chapter Shift+←, Shift+→ Jumps between chapters, when the video has them.
Mute / Unmute m Turns the sound off or on. The slider beside it sets the volume, where the browser lets a page do that.
The clock Where you are and how long the video is, such as 0:42 / 2:11, and Chapter 2 of 5 when it has chapters.
Subtitles (CC) c Shows or hides subtitles, when the video has a transcript. The tooltip names the language: Subtitles: English (c).
Picture-in-picture p Plays the video in a small window that stays on top, where the browser offers it.
Cast to a screen Sends the video to a television or other screen. Shown only while your browser finds one.
Fullscreen Gives the player alone the whole screen, with its controls and subtitles.

The volume you set is remembered in this browser for the next video or recording. There is no control for the playback speed.

On a phone, tap the picture to show or hide the controls. Three buttons sit over the middle of the picture: back ten seconds, play or pause, and forward ten seconds, or previous and next chapter when the video has chapters. More (⋮) on the bar holds Picture-in-picture and Cast to a screen…, where the browser offers them. There is no mute button: use the phone's own volume buttons. On an iPhone, Fullscreen opens Safari's own player, which shows the subtitles when they are on. Elsewhere, a wide video put fullscreen with the player's button turns the screen sideways where the browser allows it.

The timeline and chapters

Click or drag on the timeline to jump. Pointing at it shows the time there and, once the media service has made the video's storyboard, a small frame from that moment.

The media service finds the cuts in a video and makes each scene a chapter. A video whose file names the camera or phone that recorded it is taken to be one take, so there only a clear cut makes a chapter, not a lamp switched on or the exposure changing. Chapters show as pieces of the timeline with gaps between them; a video with too many to fit gets a small tick at each start instead. Pointing at the timeline shows the chapter too, such as 1:24 · Chapter 3. Previous chapter more than two seconds into a chapter goes back to its start, and otherwise to the chapter before.

Move to the timeline with Tab to use the keyboard on it: ← and → go back and forward five seconds, Page Down and Page Up go to the previous and next chapter (or a tenth of the video, without chapters), and Home and End go to the start and the end.

Subtitles

A video or recording with a transcript has the CC button. Subtitles start on; once you turn them off, they stay off for the next video or recording in this browser, until you turn them on again. When two or more people speak, each line starts with the speaker's name, in a colour of their own. A voice nobody has named is called Speaker 1, Speaker 2 and so on.

Faces in a video

The face boxes follow the faces as the video plays, and Pin faces works as it does on a photo. In the details panel, a button beside each person shows them: its tooltip reads, for example, Show Ruby Wrenfield at 0:42 · on screen 0:00–0:39, 0:42–1:28. Pressing it pauses the video on the frame where the face is seen most clearly and keeps a box on the face from there. Press it again (Stop following Ruby Wrenfield) to let go.

Listen to a recording

A recording starts playing when it opens. Its picture is the waveform of the sound, and the waveform is the timeline: the part already played is brighter, a line marks where you are, and a click or a drag on it jumps there. A large play button sits on it while the recording is paused. Under it are play or pause (Play (Space), Pause (Space)), Back 10 s and Forward 10 s, mute and the volume slider (not on a phone), and the clock; at the right end Subtitles (CC) when it has a transcript, Picture-in-picture where the browser offers it, and Fullscreen. With subtitles on, the line being said shows between the waveform and the buttons.

Space or k plays and pauses, and j, l, m, c and p work as on a video. Move to the waveform with Tab to use the same keys as on a video's timeline.

Read along with a transcript

The media service writes down what is said in videos and recordings, and works out who speaks when. The transcript appears near the top of the details panel, under the file.

  • Each line starts with its time. The line being said is in bold and is kept in view as it plays.
  • Click a line to jump to that moment.
  • Where the voice changes, the line carries the speaker's name.

Above the lines is a chip for each voice, such as Speaker 1 · 1:38: who it is and how long they speak in the file.

  • The small play button on a chip (Hear Speaker 1) jumps to the start of that voice's longest turn.
  • Click a chip to say who it is. A named voice opens the person's page. An unnamed one opens the review, where you name them or say they are someone already in the archive. A voice that belongs to nobody yet becomes an unidentified person of its own first; this needs permission to change the file.

You cannot correct a transcript's words in EiN; you can name its speakers. A transcript can also read No speech was detected., or Transcript unavailable. when it could not be loaded.

For a recording or video you reach from a search for words that are said in it, the viewer opens at the moment they are said.

Watch a slideshow

  1. Open the picture to start from, in the album or list you want to show.
  2. Press Start slideshow (the play button in the bar). On a phone it is in ⋮. It is greyed out when the list holds only one file.
  3. The browser goes fullscreen, if it allows that; if not, the slideshow runs in the window. The close button, the arrows, the details panel and the face boxes go away, and the bar lies over the bottom of the picture. Pinned face names stay on and follow the picture.
  4. Each photo stays on screen for Seconds per slide, 3 unless you change it, and fades into the next. With Ken Burns effect on, as it is unless you turn it off, each photo slowly pans and zooms. Videos and recordings play to their end before the next file. One that will not play is skipped after one slide's time.

The slideshow follows the list's order and goes round from the last file to the first, until you stop it. There is no shuffle. ← and → still step back and forward. While it runs, the screen is kept from going to sleep.

To stop the slideshow, click the picture, press Esc, or leave fullscreen.

To change the timing or turn the pan and zoom off, open your picture at the top right, choose Profile, then the Preferences tab, and look under Playback:

Setting What it does Default
Seconds per slide How long each photo is shown in a slideshow. 3
Ken Burns effect Slowly pan and zoom each photo during a slideshow. on

They are yours alone and do not change anyone else's slideshows. See Your profile, favourites and trash, and in the settings reference CFG_USER_SLIDESHOW_SECONDS and CFG_USER_KEN_BURNS.

Mark a favourite

Press the heart in the bar, or s; on a phone choose Favourite in ⋮. The heart turns red. Press it again (Unfavourite) to take the picture out of your favourites.

Your favourites are your own: open your picture at the top right and choose Favourites to see them all, by day. To heart many pictures at once, select them in the grid and use Favourite in the selection bar. Visitors who are not signed in have no favourites and see no heart. See Your profile, favourites and trash.

What you see depends on who you are

You are What differs
Signed in, and may change the file Everything on this page.
Signed in, and may only see the file The pencil menu holds only Add to album. You read comments but cannot write them, and see the text language without the menu. No Edit text, Edit faces, rotate or mirror.
A visitor who is not signed in No heart, no pencil menu and no sharing chip. Path is plain text.
Someone who opened a share link The pictures without the details panel; the details button and i are not there.

Sharing explains who can see what.

When something is not as expected

A picture you added a moment ago has no thumbnail, faces, text or transcript yet. The media service works through added files in the background, and the viewer fills in as each step finishes. Until it is done, an administrator sees the Dashboard read Catching up. Text in pictures waits until the file leaves staging. See Getting your photos in.

"This rendition could not be played. It may still be transcoding." The version of the video or recording that the browser plays has not been made yet, or failed. Wait a while and open it again.

"This browser or device cannot play AV1, the format this video is in." EiN makes its playable videos in the AV1 format, and this browser or device cannot decode it. Use a current browser on another device, or Download the original.

"This file cannot be played", with "The original is damaged, so no preview or playable version could be made from it." The uploaded file itself cannot be read; the reason is under the message. Download it to check the file on your own device.

A PDF or a Word file shows nothing. The viewer has no reader for documents. Use Download in the More menu to open the file on your device. Its text can still be found with search; see Supported files.

Show text is not in the More menu. No text was read from this picture, or it is a video or a recording. Add the text yourself with Add text, if you may change the file.

Start slideshow is greyed out. The list holds only one file. Open the viewer from an album or a longer list.

The date or the place is wrong. See Dates and places of pictures.

From the shell

What the viewer shows and changes can also be done in EiN's terminal:

Command What it does
media-details <file> Everything the details panel shows about a file.
file-set-description --text '…' <file> Sets or clears the description.
file-set-text --text '…' <file> Writes the text in a picture by hand.
file-set-text-language <file> <code|auto> Reads the text again in a language.
file-set-orientation --rotate cw <file> Turns a file; ccw and 180 too, and --toggle-mirror flips it.
star <path>, star -d <path> Marks a favourite, or takes it away. 
# in EiN's terminal
media-details /archive/albums/cornwall-1968/img20120303_133900582.jpg
file-set-text-language /archive/albums/cornwall-1968/img20120303_133900582.jpg en
