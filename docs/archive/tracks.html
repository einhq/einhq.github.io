Trips

A device that travels, such as a phone, a GPS tracker on a boat or a logger carried on walks, keeps where it was as a track: positions with their times. The Archive cuts the track into trips, says where they stayed and what the weather was, draws the route on its maps, and makes a trip an album, all on the device's Trips tab. This page is for family members and administrators who bring routes in, correct trips and make albums of them. The devices themselves are EiN's own, on Devices; what took the pictures is on Cameras.

Where tracks come from

A track is a device's endpoint of the type track under /dev: the position that EiN's tracker driver keeps for a phone, a GPS tracker or a logger. Its positions come in two ways:

  • Posted as the device moves. A phone with Traccar Client, or a tracker on a boat or in a car, posts its position to EiN with the device's key (see Report from a phone).
  • From GPX files a logger or a tracking app saved: written to the device, uploaded on its Trips tab, or dropped in staging (see GPX files).

A member of the devices group makes the device first, saying how it travels when it always travels the same way:

# in EiN's terminal, as a member of devices
device-create Sailboat --driver tracker --class boat
device-create 'Ruby phone' --driver tracker --class phone

A track is named by its device's label: the sailboat's is called Sailboat. A phone that takes pictures as well is a camera too, and linking the camera to its device ties the two together (see A phone that records where it was).

The Trips tab

A device with a track has a Trips tab on its page, after Overview, History and Info. Its address is the device's page with ?tab=archive.trips, such as /devices/sailboat?tab=archive.trips. An album's Trip tab and a track's entry on a timeline open the device's page on it; a live track on the Map page, Device on a camera's Info tab and a device in an album's Info tab open the device's page as it opens.

At its top, Trips, Travel and Fixes choose what it shows:

View What it shows
Trips The view it opens on: the trips found in the track, newest first (see Trips)
Travel How the device travels, its home bases and its description (see below)
Fixes The route on a map, with the positions EiN left out (see The route on the map)

Those who may change the track also get Upload GPX and How it travels beside them. That is the device's owner, whoever made it, and the administrator, who owns the devices a driver found; Devices and their keys says more.

On Travel:

  • Travel lists Travels as (its own setting, or the device's class, such as "Boat (the device's class)", or "Not set (read as a car)"), Timezone (UTC when none is set), how many Positions it holds, the days it was Recorded over, such as "12 Jun 2025 – 3 Aug 2025", and its Path in the tree, such as /dev/sailboat/position.
  • Home bases, where arriving ends a trip (see Home bases).
  • About shows the description, when there is one.

How a device travels

A device moves as a boat, a car, a bike or on foot, and that says which units its track is shown in: a boat's distances in nautical miles and its speeds in knots, everyone else's in kilometres and km/h. It also says what lying still looks like when the track is drawn (see The route on the map): a boat lies still within 80 m for 15 minutes, a car or a bike within 50 or 30 m for 10 minutes, someone on foot within 25 m; and a jump faster than 50 knots, 250, 100 or 40 km/h is a GPS error.

How it travels is the track's Travels as setting, or, while that is not set, the device's class when the class is boat, car, bike or foot. A track with neither is read as a car. A phone, carried on a sailing holiday and in the car alike, is best left as it is, and a tracker that stays on the boat made with --class boat.

Its Timezone is the time zone its trips are cut into days in: a day ends at midnight there, and the times of its trips are on that clock. A track without one has its days in UTC.

To change them:

  1. On the device's Trips tab, click How it travels. The How it travels dialog opens.
  2. Choose Travels as: "How it travels, which tells its stops from drift and its trips from rests. A boat’s track is shown in knots and nautical miles, the others’ in km/h and km. Without one, the device’s class says it."
  3. Choose Timezone, "The zone its trips are cut into days in.", from a list of time zones you can type in, such as Europe/Stockholm. Clear it for UTC.
  4. Write a Description if you like, and click Save.

A changed Travels as or Timezone finds the track's trips again from the start, and so does a changed class of its device while Travels as is not set.

Home bases

A track's home bases are the places it belongs: the boat's home berth and winter yard, the house where the car is parked. Arriving at one ends a trip. Home bases on the Travel view of the device's Trips tab lists them, each linking to its location, under the line "Where it belongs: a home berth, a winter yard, the house. Arriving at one ends a trip."

  • Click Add home base, choose a location in the list (Pick a location), and click Add. A place that is not a location yet is added on the Locations page first (see Locations and the map).
  • Click the cross beside a home base to remove it.

While a track has no home bases of its own, the homes of the owners of the cameras linked to its device count instead: each place an owner lived while they owned the camera, for the years both are true (see A phone that records where it was and Homes and residents). It then says "None set, so the homes of the owners of its cameras count:" and lists them with those years, such as "2010 – 2020". With no such home either, it says "None set, and no owner of a camera linked to the device has a home recorded." As soon as the track has one home base of its own, the owners' homes no longer count.

Adding and removing are offered to those who may change the track, and each finds the track's trips again from the start.

The route on the map

A track shows on the Fixes view of its device's Trips tab, on the Map page with its Tracks layer (see Locations and the map), and on the Map tab of an album whose dates it falls in, when its device is on the album's list: a camera that took some of the album's pictures is linked to the device, or the device was added to the album (see Choose which devices the map shows). A GPS tracker takes no pictures, so add it to the albums of the trips it was carried on. The device's own History tab draws its positions too, for a day, a week, a month or a year (Devices).

Fixes draws the route on a map that takes most of the window below the device's header, each day in its own colour, as an album's Map tab does. A panel floats over the foot of the map with what the route adds up to:

Figure What it is
Points How many recorded positions the track holds, without those EiN rejected (see below).
Days On how many days it recorded.
Distance The length of the route, added up day by day, so the gap between one day's end and the next day's start is not counted. In nautical miles for a boat, in kilometres otherwise (see How a device travels).
Avg speed The average speed while it moved, in knots for a boat and km/h otherwise. Time lying still, jumps nothing could make, and gaps of more than an hour between two points are left out.
Duration The time from the first point to the last, in hours and minutes. It includes the time between trips, so a track that holds several trips shows the whole span.

While a tracker lies still, its GPS keeps wandering: around a berth or a parking space it records a tangle of positions, now and then one far out that it never went to. EiN draws neither. Where the track stayed in one place, at walking pace or slower, the line ends on the middle of where it lay and later leaves from there, and a position it could not have reached so fast is left out, unless you accepted it (see Reject or accept a position by hand). What counts as staying and as too fast follows how its device travels (see How a device travels). The Points figure still counts every recorded position, while Distance leaves the wandering out. The Map page and an album's Map tab draw tracks the same way.

A GPS can also be wrong for longer than one position. A receiver that loses most of its satellites can draw a smooth route hundreds of kilometres away for minutes, and a phone that loses its fix falls back to a position a cell or a network gave it, sometimes the same one over and over. EiN keeps every position it is given, and as it adds them to a track it rejects such runs:

  • a stretch the track jumps out to and back from, faster than anything on the ground or the water goes (over 600 km/h for more than a kilometre), within two hours;
  • a position it jumps back onto exactly, to the last decimal, after being away from it longer than it was there.

A rejected position is not drawn, counted in Points or used for trips. A jump that does not come back, as when a logger starts again somewhere else, keeps its positions, and the line is broken there instead of drawn across. A route flown with the logger on is neither: every one of its steps is fast. The same rules apply however the positions arrive: a GPX file uploaded or staged, or a phone tracking live. track-read --rejected reads the rejected positions too, marked, and track-read --only-rejected reads them alone.

A long track is drawn from a thinned copy, and its distance then comes out a little shorter than the route really was.

EiN draws a track on the maps; it does not use it to give a position to pictures that have none. To place those, see Dates and places of pictures.

A track with no positions yet says Nothing recorded yet: "Its route appears here once a GPX file it was recorded in is uploaded, or the device reports where it is.", with Upload GPX under it for those who may change it.

Reject or accept a position by hand

The rules can be wrong both ways: a glitch they miss stays on the map, and a real stretch they reject, such as a flight with the logger off that lands near where it left, is left out. On Fixes the rejected positions are drawn faintly in red, each with a dot, and the panel over the map says how many there are. Click near a position, drawn or rejected, and the panel says when it was recorded and what EiN made of it. If you may change the track, it also offers:

  • Reject, on a drawn position: it is left out, whatever the rules say, and so are the other drawn positions within 40 m of it that day (the day in UTC, as the track colours it). A point on the drawn line often stands for several positions recorded close together, and one taken away would let the next take its place. The panel says how many there are, and the button reads Reject all and the number. Over 25 is a place the track stayed at, not a glitch, and then Reject takes the one position alone.
  • Accept, on a rejected one: it is drawn, whatever the rules say. When it is one of a run of rejected positions, Accept all takes the whole run.
  • Undo, on a position you rejected or accepted: it goes back to what the rules make of it.
  • Undo all N that day, on a position you rejected, when you rejected others that day: every position rejected by hand that day goes back to the rules. Positions you accepted stay accepted.

A position rejected by hand is left out before the rules judge the positions around it, so the line can break somewhere else once it is gone. The track's trips are found again ten minutes later, as when points arrive. To do the same for a time span, use track-mark with --reject, --accept or --clear:

# in EiN's terminal
track-mark sailboat --from 2025-07-01T13:00:00Z --to 2025-07-01T15:30:00Z --accept

--from and --to are both optional: without them the whole track is marked, so track-mark sailboat --clear hands every position back to the rules. --near <lat,lng> marks only the positions within 40 m of a point (--within sets another distance in metres), and --marked reject or --marked accept only those you rejected or accepted. Undo all N that day is this:

# in EiN's terminal
track-mark sailboat --from 2025-07-01T00:00:00Z --to 2025-07-02T00:00:00Z --marked reject --clear

Live positions

A tracker or a phone that posts its position as it moves adds it to its track as it comes (see Report from a phone). While its last point is under four hours old, a Live line tops the panel with its speed in km/h and how long ago that point was taken ("12 km/h · 40 s ago"). The map then draws a pin where it is now and extends the route as points arrive. Click Follow, the crosshair at the end of that line, and the map keeps the newest point in view until you drag it. The Map page follows live tracks the same way; see Follow a track live.

GPX files

A GPX file is the format GPS trackers and tracking apps save routes in. EiN reads the recorded track points of the file, each with its time; points with a time the track already has are not added twice, so bringing the same file in again changes nothing. A point without a time is left out, since EiN cannot tell when the track was there.

A GPX file goes to a device: written to it, cat summer.gpx > /dev/sailboat in the terminal, the device's driver reads its points into the track (see GPX files on the Devices page). The Archive adds two ways, below, and keeps every GPX file it brings in in the track's routes folder, such as /dev/sailboat/position/routes/, linked from the track's own sources folder. The same file brought in twice, byte for byte, is kept once.

Upload a GPX file to a device

If you know which device a file was recorded on, upload it on that device's page:

  1. Open the device's Trips tab. Upload GPX is at its top, beside the views.
  2. Click Upload GPX and choose one or more .gpx files. The file picker offers GPX files only.
  3. Wait for the message: "N new points added to the track", or "The track already had every point in it", followed by "; N without a time left out" when some were. A file that cannot be added gets a message of its own, starting with its name; the others are added all the same.

The button is offered to those who may change the track. The file is kept with the track under its own name, in its routes folder.

GPX files in staging

A GPX file that arrives in your staging area, by Import media, the Media page's Upload or copying over SFTP (see Getting your photos in), is not treated as a picture. EiN reads which device the file names and gives the route to that device; the file then moves out of staging into the device's track's routes folder.

The file names its device in its creator attribute or in one of its <name> entries. It matches a device when one of these holds, as whole words, the device's label, its manufacturer and model together, or its serial number, or the name, the make and model together, or the serial number of a camera linked to the device (see A phone that records where it was). Capital letters and punctuation do not count, and a name shorter than three letters never matches. A file whose creator reads Garmin eTrex 30x joins the device made with --manufacturer Garmin --model 'eTrex 30x', one whose creator reads Apple iPhone 15 joins the phone whose camera has the make Apple and the model iPhone 15, and one named Sailboat joins the sailboat. Either of these places counts:

<gpx version="1.1" creator="Apple iPhone 15">
  <metadata><name>Sailboat</name></metadata>
  <trk><name>Sailboat</name> …</trk>
</gpx>

A <name> inside a track point is a waypoint's label and does not count. Many apps write their own name as the creator (Track Logger 2.1) and no device anywhere; such a file waits for you to choose its device, as below.

When a file names no device, or more than one, it waits for you:

  1. Open Staging in the sidebar. A yellow box titled Needs you says "1 GPX file waits for the device it belongs to." (or how many), whichever category the file was dropped on. The box is on the overview, not on a category's page.
  2. Click Choose device. The page Routes waiting for a device opens, with your staging area's routes folder listed below its header as on the Nodes page, and a panel beside it.
  3. Click a file to see in the panel what it says about itself: its Name and Description, its Tracks, its Creator, when it was Recorded (first and last point, in UTC, and how long between), its Points, its Size, and under Matches the devices it names, or "no device it names". The arrow keys move from file to file.
  4. Select the files of one device: Ctrl and click adds or removes one, Shift and click takes a run of them, Ctrl+A takes them all. On a phone, press and hold a file, then tap the others. At the top of the panel, choose the device in the Device they come from list and click Import (Import 3 files with three selected). Each route joins that device's track and its file moves out of the list into the track's routes folder. A file that cannot be read stays, and the panel names it and why. Back to staging returns to the staging page.

On a narrow screen the panel is below the list.

In the terminal, gpx-info says the same of any GPX file without importing it: gpx-info /home/<you>/staging/routes/*.gpx.

If the device does not exist yet, have a member of devices make it first (see Where tracks come from) and open the page again; a waiting file is not tried again by itself. Waiting files are kept in your staging area's routes folder, /home/<you>/staging/routes/. Sorting in staging describes the staging area.

Trips

EiN cuts a track into trips by itself, and the Trips view of the device's Trips tab lists them, newest first. A trip runs from where the track lay still to where it lay still again:

  • Arriving at one of the track's home bases ends a trip, once it has been there half an hour or the track ends there. Passing by does not.
  • Staying anywhere else longer than three days ends a trip too (two days for a bike or on foot), as does a gap of that long with no points at all.
  • A trip that never gets 500 m from where it started is not a trip, and is left out.

How near a place has to be to count as the same place depends on how the device travels: within 1 km for a boat, 500 m for a car, 300 m for a bike and 200 m on foot.

Each trip's card shows its dates, an Ongoing badge while it has not ended and its last point is less than two days old, how many days it took, its length, how far from its start it got, and where it started and ended: the location of the stay, such as a harbour, an island or your home, or "a point on the way". The farthest place it stayed at is named too. Click a name to open its location. How stays get their locations and names is on Places a track stayed at. A stay of two hours or more across midnight is an overnight stay; it does not end the trip. A trip that is an album names it under Album; see Make a trip an album.

Click N legs to see the trip day by day: each day's date, its first and last time, its length, and where the day ended. A day's length runs from where the day before ended, so a drive across midnight counts, and the days add up to the trip's length. Days follow the track's Timezone, so a day ends at midnight there, and the times are on that clock; a track without one has its days and times in UTC.

The trips are kept up to date as the track grows: ten minutes after points arrive, EiN finds them again from where the last finished trip ended, puts the new stays at locations, and, unless weather lookups are turned off, gives each trip and day the weather along the track, which an album made of the trip shows (The Trip tab). Changing how the device travels (its Travels as or its class), its time zone or its home bases finds them again from the start. Find trips again does it at once.

When the track has no trips, the view says No trips: "Nothing in the track went 500 m or more from where it started.", or, before they were ever looked for, "Trips are found in the track once it has fixes, and kept up to date as it grows.", with Find trips for those who may change the track.

Join and part trips

EiN may end a trip where you do not: a weekend at home in the middle of a holiday, or a long wait for weather in a harbour. Those who may change the track can correct it:

  • Join to the trip before, on a trip's card, makes it and the trip before it one trip, whatever ended the first. The card then says Joined on and the day, with Part again to undo it.
  • Start a trip here, beside a day in a trip's legs, makes that day the first day of a trip of its own. Join to the trip before on the new trip undoes it.

The trips are found again at once. The changes are kept with the track, in its tripEdits setting, and hold when the trips are found again later.

Move a trip to another device

A trip recorded on the wrong device, such as a phone posting the sailboat's trip while it was the car's tracker that should have, can be moved to the right one. Those who may change the track click Move to another device… on the trip's card, pick the device under Device in Move the trip to another device ("Its positions, first to last, leave this device's track for the other's, and the trips of both are found again."), and click Move. The list holds every other device with a track. Positions you rejected or accepted by hand keep that.

The positions recorded where the trip began and ended stay, as does any GPX file the trip was read from, in this track's routes folder. To move another stretch, use track-move with the time span, naming the devices:

# in EiN's terminal
track-move ruby-phone sailboat --from 2025-07-01T06:00:00Z --to 2025-07-03T18:00:00Z

A position at the very same moment as one the other track has already is dropped, and the other track keeps its own.

Make a trip an album

A trip EiN finds is only a proposal until you make it an album. Those who may change the track see, on each trip's card:

  • New album…, which opens Create Album to ask what the album is called. The name starts as the farthest place the trip stayed at when that place has a name; the trip's dates are the album's span, not part of its name. Create makes the album in /archive/albums and opens it.
  • Put in an album…, which opens Put the trip in an album. Before you type, it offers the albums whose dates overlap the trip and whose map draws this device's track (see Choose which devices the map shows); their number keys pick them. Type part of a name to find any album you may change, or type a new name and press New album “…” to make an album of the trip under that name. Choosing an album makes it the trip's. An album that already holds another trip is refused with a message saying so.

Either way the device is added to the album's devices, so its Map tab draws the route, and the album spans the trip even before it has any pictures. The card then names the album under Album, with Take off the album to undo it; the album and its pictures stay, and so does the device on its list. A trip is one album's, and an album holds one trip. Each time the trips are found again, or their stays put at locations, the album follows: it grows while the trip is ongoing, and its stays get their locations. The album holds on to its trip when the device or its track is moved or renamed.

The album gets a Trip tab: the days, the distance in the track's units, where it stayed, and each day's notes. See The Trip tab. Those who may only look at the track see the albums the trip may belong to under Maybe in.

If something goes differently

What you see Why What to do
A device has no Trips tab. It has no track: it is a sensor or a plug, or a tracker made without --driver tracker that has had no position yet. Make a phone or a logger with device-create <name> --driver tracker; see Where tracks come from.
The Trips view says No trips though the track has positions. Its trips have not been found yet, or no part of the track went 500 m or more from where it started. Click Find trips, or Find trips again.
A trip ends at home in the middle of a holiday, or two trips should be one. Arriving at a home base, or staying somewhere longer than three days, ends a trip. Click Join to the trip before on the second trip's card.
A trip's days end at the wrong hour. The track's Timezone is not set, so its days are cut in UTC, or it is set to another zone. Click How it travels and choose the Timezone its days belong in.
A trip is listed under the wrong device. The phone or tracker that recorded it posted to another device, or its GPX file was given to the wrong device. Click Move to another device… on the trip's card; see Move a trip to another device.
How it travels, Upload GPX or Add home base is missing. You may not change this track: it is the device's owner's. Ask whoever made the device, or an administrator.
Upload GPX gives a message ending in "no track points found". The file holds no recorded track points, only a planned route or waypoints. Use the file that was recorded while it moved.
Upload GPX gives a message ending in "none of its N track points has a time". The file's points carry no times: a route drawn on a map, or a file the program saved without them. Use the file that was recorded while it moved, or export it again with times.
A GPX file dropped in staging waits, though its device exists. The file names neither the device nor a camera linked to it, or it names more than one device. Click Choose device under Needs you on the Staging page; see GPX files in staging.
The phone reports nothing to the track. It cannot reach EiN over HTTPS from where it is, its key was renewed or taken away, or tracking is off in Traccar Client. See Report from a phone and Ports, HTTPS and remote access.
A GPX file sits in an album as an ordinary file. It was uploaded straight into the album, where GPX files are not read as routes. Upload it on the device's Trips tab, then delete the file from the album.

From the terminal

Every track command takes a track by its path, or by its device or a camera linked to the device, by its path or its name: sailboat is /dev/sailboat/position. A track's routes folder holds the GPX files it was read from, its homes folder links its home bases, and its .trips keeps its trips. The commands below are in the archive command reference, and every one takes --help; the devices' own commands are on Devices.

Task Command
List the devices' tracks, with their name, kind, positions and span track-list, or track-list <folder> for those under one folder
Find tracks by name or description search-track <words>
Change how a device travels track-update <track> --kind boat (car, bike, foot), --timezone <zone> or --description <text>, or --clear <field>
List a track's home bases track-home <track>; with none of its own, the homes of its cameras' owners and the years they count
Add or remove a home base track-home <track> --add <location>… or --remove <location>…; a location is its path, or its folder name
Read a track as the maps draw it track-read <track> --still; without --still every recorded position, --max-speed <m/s> to change what is too fast
Give a GPX file to a device cat <gpx> > /dev/<device>; track-stage <gpx> --track <device>, which moves the file to the track's routes, or track-import <device> <gpx>, which keeps a copy there
Add a position by hand track-append <track> --lat=<lat> --lng=<lng> --ts <time>
See what GPX files hold, without importing gpx-info <gpx>…: name, tracks, creator, points and their time span, size, and the devices each names
List the GPX files a track was read from source-list /dev/sailboat/position
Find a track's trips track-trips <track>, --full from the start, --dry-run to see them without keeping them
List a track's trips trip-list <track>
Put a track's stays at locations, and name the new ones track-places <track>; see Places a track stayed at
Give a track's trips and days their weather track-weather <track>, --full to look every trip up again, --dry-run to see what it would look up
Join a trip to the one before, or part one trip-edit <track> <trip> --merge, or <track> <day> --split
Make a trip an album, or take it off trip-album <track> <trip> --new, --album=<album>, or --detach
Reject or accept positions by hand, or hand them back to the rules track-mark <track> --reject, --accept or --clear, with --from, --to, --near <lat,lng> and --marked reject
Move a stretch of a track to another device track-move <track> <to-track> --from <time> --to <time>
Link a camera to the device it is part of camera-device <camera> <device>, or <camera> --off
Put a device on an album's map, or take it off album-device-add and album-device-rm <album> <device>…; see Albums
Make a phone's or a tracker's key, or renew or take it away device-key <device>, with --new or --off; see Report from a phone

Making Jon's GPS logger, which he walks with, and adding a route copied into your home folder:

# in EiN's terminal, as a member of devices
device-create "Jon's eTrex" --driver tracker --class foot --manufacturer Garmin --model 'eTrex 30x'
track-update jon-s-etrex --timezone Pacific/Auckland
track-stage "$HOME/tararua-walk.gpx" --track jon-s-etrex

device-create prints the device's path, /dev/jon-s-etrex, and gives it its track, /dev/jon-s-etrex/position; --class foot says how it travels. track-stage moves the GPX file to the track's routes folder once its points are in the track. See Commands by task for the rest of the archive's commands.
