Tracks and trips

A track is a route something travelled, kept as GPS positions with their times: a boat's summers, a car's drives, a walker's hikes. EiN draws tracks on its maps, cuts each one into trips, says where the trips stayed and what the weather was, and makes a trip an album. This page is for family members and administrators who bring tracks in, correct them and make albums of their trips. What took the pictures is on Cameras.

Where tracks come from

A track is a node of the type track, anywhere under /archive. It gets its positions from a GPS tracker or a phone that posts where it is, or from the GPX files a GPS logger or a tracking app saved:

  • A tracker, or a phone posting its position. With the Automation module, a member of the automation group makes a device by hand with a position, and its position's history is the track: device-create Sailboat --position makes /archive/devices/sailboat/position/history (see Devices made by hand). A phone with Traccar Client then adds its position as it moves; that is turned on on the device's page (see Live tracking from a phone).
  • A GPS logger whose files you import. It is a device made the same way. Its GPX files go into its track by Upload GPX on the track's page, by staging or with track-import.

Without the Automation module, a track can still be made in the terminal with core's mknode -t track, under any folder of /archive you may write in.

A track is named by its own label, or else by the device it belongs to: the sailboat's track is called Sailboat. A phone that takes pictures as well is a camera too, and linking the camera to its track ties the two together (see A phone that records where it was).

The Tracks page

Open Tracks in the sidebar, under Browse.

  • The header counts the tracks (N tracks). The filter box (Filter tracks…) matches the name and description as you type.
  • Sort offers Name (A-Z) (the default) and Last recorded, the track that recorded most lately first. Your choice is remembered in this browser.
  • Each card shows the track's name, its description, the days it was recorded over, and badges for how it travels (Boat, Car, Bike or On foot) and how many positions it holds. Pointing at a card shows the track's path. Click a card to open the track's page.

When there is no track, the page says No tracks yet: "Where a GPS tracker, a phone or a GPX file says something travelled: a boat, a car, a hike. A tracker's history is one, kept with its device."

A track's page

Click a card on the Tracks page. A track's page also opens from Track and trips on an automation device's Overview, from Track on a camera's Info tab, from a live track on the Map page, and from an album's Info and Trip tabs. The page's address is /tracks/ and the track's path, encoded, such as /tracks/%2Farchive%2Fdevices%2Fsailboat%2Fposition%2Fhistory, and a tab other than Track adds ?tab=trips or ?tab=info.

For those who may change the track, the header's corner has Upload GPX, Share and Edit; on a phone they are under ⋮. Who may change it is what its Share dialog shows and sets (see Sharing).

Tab What it shows
Track The page opens here: the route on a map (see The route on the map).
Trips The trips found in the route, newest first (see Trips).
Info How the track travels, its home bases and its description (see below).

On the Info tab:

  • Track lists Travels as (or "Not set (read as a car)"), Timezone (UTC when none is set), how many Positions it holds, the days it was Recorded over, such as "12 Jun 2025 – 3 Aug 2025", and its Path in the tree.
  • Home bases, where arriving ends a trip (see Home bases).
  • About shows the description, when there is one.

How a track travels

A track moves as a boat, a car, a bike or on foot. Its Travels as setting, Boat, Car, Bike or On foot, says which, and so which units it is shown in: a boat's distances in nautical miles and its speeds in knots, everyone else's in kilometres and km/h. It also says what lying still looks like when the track is drawn (see The route on the map): a boat lies still within 80 m for 15 minutes, a car or a bike within 50 or 30 m for 10 minutes, someone on foot within 25 m; and a jump faster than 50 knots, 250, 100 or 40 km/h is a GPS error. A track without the setting is read as a car. A phone's track, carried on a sailing holiday and in the car alike, is best left as it is, and a tracker that stays on the boat set to Boat.

Its Timezone is the time zone its trips are cut into days in: a day ends at midnight there, and the times of its trips are on that clock. A track without one has its days in UTC.

To change them:

  1. On the track's page, click Edit. The Edit track dialog opens.
  2. Choose Travels as: "How it travels, which tells its stops from drift and its trips from rests. A boat’s track is shown in knots and nautical miles, the others’ in km/h and km."
  3. Choose Timezone, "The zone its trips are cut into days in.", from a list of time zones you can type in, such as Europe/Stockholm. Clear it for UTC.
  4. Write a Description if you like, and click Save.

A changed Travels as or Timezone finds the track's trips again from the start.

Home bases

A track's home bases are the places it belongs: the boat's home berth and winter yard, the house where the car is parked. Arriving at one ends a trip. Home bases on the track's Info tab lists them, each linking to its location, under the line "Where it belongs: a home berth, a winter yard, the house. Arriving at one ends a trip."

  • Click Add home base, choose a location in the list (Pick a location), and click Add. A place that is not a location yet is added on the Locations page first (see Locations and the map).
  • Click the cross beside a home base to remove it.

While a track has no home bases of its own, the homes of the owners of the cameras that record into it count instead: each place an owner lived while they owned the camera, for the years both are true (see A phone that records where it was and Homes and residents). It then says "None set, so the homes of the owners of its cameras count:" and lists them with those years, such as "2010 – 2020". With no such home either, it says "None set, and no owner of a camera that records into it has a home recorded." As soon as the track has one home base of its own, the owners' homes no longer count.

Adding and removing are offered to those who may change the track, and each finds the track's trips again from the start.

The route on the map

A track shows on its page's Track tab, on the Map page with its Tracks layer (see Locations and the map), and on the Map tab of an album whose dates it falls in, when it is on the album's list: a camera that took some of the album's pictures records into it, or it was added to the album (see Choose which tracks the map shows). A GPS tracker takes no pictures, so add its track to the albums of the trips it was carried on.

The Track tab draws the route on a map that fills the window below the tabs, each day in its own colour, as an album's Map tab does: opening it shrinks the header down to its row of tabs, so the mouse wheel over the map zooms it. A panel floats over the foot of the map with what the route adds up to:

Figure What it is
Points How many recorded positions the track holds, without those EiN rejected (see below).
Days On how many days it recorded.
Distance The length of the route, added up day by day, so the gap between one day's end and the next day's start is not counted. In nautical miles for a boat, in kilometres otherwise (see How a track travels).
Avg speed The average speed while it moved, in knots for a boat and km/h otherwise. Time lying still, jumps nothing could make, and gaps of more than an hour between two points are left out.
Duration The time from the first point to the last, in hours and minutes. It includes the time between trips, so a track that holds several trips shows the whole span.

While a tracker lies still, its GPS keeps wandering: around a berth or a parking space it records a tangle of positions, now and then one far out that it never went to. EiN draws neither. Where the track stayed in one place, at walking pace or slower, the line ends on the middle of where it lay and later leaves from there, and a position it could not have reached so fast is left out, unless you accepted it (see Reject or accept a position by hand). What counts as staying and as too fast follows its Travels as setting (see How a track travels). The Points figure still counts every recorded position, while Distance leaves the wandering out. The Map page and an album's Map tab draw tracks the same way.

A GPS can also be wrong for longer than one position. A receiver that loses most of its satellites can draw a smooth route hundreds of kilometres away for minutes, and a phone that loses its fix falls back to a position a cell or a network gave it, sometimes the same one over and over. EiN keeps every position it is given, and as it adds them to a track it rejects such runs:

  • a stretch the track jumps out to and back from, faster than anything on the ground or the water goes (over 600 km/h for more than a kilometre), within two hours;
  • a position it jumps back onto exactly, to the last decimal, after being away from it longer than it was there.

A rejected position is not drawn, counted in Points or used for trips. A jump that does not come back, as when a logger starts again somewhere else, keeps its positions, and the line is broken there instead of drawn across. A route flown with the logger on is neither: every one of its steps is fast. The same rules apply however the positions arrive: a GPX file uploaded or staged, or a phone tracking live. track-read --rejected reads the rejected positions too, marked, and track-read --only-rejected reads them alone.

A long track is drawn from a thinned copy, and its distance then comes out a little shorter than the route really was.

EiN draws a track on the maps; it does not use it to give a position to pictures that have none. To place those, see Dates and places of pictures.

A track with no positions yet says Nothing recorded yet: "Its route appears here once a GPX file it was recorded in is uploaded, or its tracker reports where it is.", with Upload GPX under it for those who may change it.

Reject or accept a position by hand

The rules can be wrong both ways: a glitch they miss stays on the map, and a real stretch they reject, such as a flight with the logger off that lands near where it left, is left out. On the Track tab the rejected positions are drawn faintly in red, each with a dot, and the panel over the map says how many there are. Click near a position, drawn or rejected, and the panel says when it was recorded and what EiN made of it. If you may change the track, it also offers:

  • Reject, on a drawn position: it is left out, whatever the rules say, and so are the other drawn positions within 40 m of it that day (the day in UTC, as the track colours it). A point on the drawn line often stands for several positions recorded close together, and one taken away would let the next take its place. The panel says how many there are, and the button reads Reject all and the number. Over 25 is a place the track stayed at, not a glitch, and then Reject takes the one position alone.
  • Accept, on a rejected one: it is drawn, whatever the rules say. When it is one of a run of rejected positions, Accept all takes the whole run.
  • Undo, on a position you rejected or accepted: it goes back to what the rules make of it.
  • Undo all N that day, on a position you rejected, when you rejected others that day: every position rejected by hand that day goes back to the rules. Positions you accepted stay accepted.

A position rejected by hand is left out before the rules judge the positions around it, so the line can break somewhere else once it is gone. The track's trips are found again ten minutes later, as when points arrive. To do the same for a time span, use track-mark with --reject, --accept or --clear:

# in EiN's terminal
track-mark sailboat --from 2025-07-01T13:00:00Z --to 2025-07-01T15:30:00Z --accept

--from and --to are both optional: without them the whole track is marked, so track-mark sailboat --clear hands every position back to the rules. --near <lat,lng> marks only the positions within 40 m of a point (--within sets another distance in metres), and --marked reject or --marked accept only those you rejected or accepted. Undo all N that day is this:

# in EiN's terminal
track-mark sailboat --from 2025-07-01T00:00:00Z --to 2025-07-02T00:00:00Z --marked reject --clear

Live positions

A tracker or a phone that posts its position as it moves adds it to its track as it comes (see Live tracking from a phone). While its last point is under four hours old, a Live line tops the panel with its speed in km/h and how long ago that point was taken ("12 km/h · 40 s ago"). The map then draws a pin where it is now and extends the route as points arrive. Click Follow, the crosshair at the end of that line, and the map keeps the newest point in view until you drag it. The Map page follows live tracks the same way; see Follow a track live.

GPX files

A GPX file is the format GPS trackers and tracking apps save routes in. EiN reads the recorded track points of the file, each with its time; points with a time the track already has are not added twice, so bringing the same file in again changes nothing. A point without a time is left out, since EiN cannot tell when the track was there.

EiN keeps every GPX file it reads into a track in the track's routes folder, such as /archive/devices/sailboat/position/history/routes/, and the track links each one from its own sources folder. The same file brought in twice, byte for byte, is kept once.

Upload a GPX file to a track

If you know which track a file belongs to, upload it on that track's page:

  1. Open the track's page. Upload GPX is in the header's corner; on a phone it is under ⋮.
  2. Click Upload GPX and choose one or more .gpx files. The file picker offers GPX files only.
  3. Wait for the message: "N new points added to the track", or "The track already had every point in it", followed by "; N without a time left out" when some were. A file that cannot be added gets a message of its own, starting with its name; the others are added all the same.

The button is offered to those who may change the track. The file is kept with the track under its own name, in its routes folder.

GPX files in staging

A GPX file that arrives in your staging area, by Import media, the Media page's Upload or copying over SFTP (see Getting your photos in), is not treated as a picture. EiN reads which track the file names and adds the route to that track; the file then moves out of staging into the track's routes folder.

The file names its track in its creator attribute or in one of its <name> entries. It matches a track when one of these holds, as whole words, the track's name, or the name, the make and model together, or the serial number of a camera that records into the track (see A phone that records where it was). Capital letters and punctuation do not count, and a name shorter than three letters never matches. A file whose creator reads Apple iPhone 15 joins the track of the phone whose camera has the make Apple and the model iPhone 15, and one named Sailboat joins the sailboat's track. Either of these places counts:

<gpx version="1.1" creator="Apple iPhone 15">
  <metadata><name>Sailboat</name></metadata>
  <trk><name>Sailboat</name> …</trk>
</gpx>

A <name> inside a track point is a waypoint's label and does not count. Many apps write their own name as the creator (Track Logger 2.1) and no track anywhere; such a file waits for you to choose its track, as below.

When a file names no track, or more than one, it waits for you:

  1. Open Staging in the sidebar. A yellow box titled Needs you says "1 GPX file waits for the track it belongs to." (or how many), whichever category the file was dropped on. The box is on the overview, not on a category's page.
  2. Click Choose track. The page Routes waiting for a track opens, with your staging area's routes folder listed below its header as on the Nodes page, and a panel beside it.
  3. Click a file to see in the panel what it says about itself: its Name and Description, its Tracks, its Creator, when it was Recorded (first and last point, in UTC, and how long between), its Points, its Size, and under Matches the tracks it names, or "no track it names". The arrow keys move from file to file.
  4. Select the files of one track: Ctrl and click adds or removes one, Shift and click takes a run of them, Ctrl+A takes them all. On a phone, press and hold a file, then tap the others. At the top of the panel, choose the track in the Track they belong to list and click Import (Import 3 files with three selected). Each route joins that track and its file moves out of the list into the track's routes folder. A file that cannot be read stays, and the panel names it and why. Back to staging returns to the staging page.

On a narrow screen the panel is below the list.

In the terminal, gpx-info says the same of any GPX file without importing it: gpx-info /home/<you>/staging/routes/*.gpx.

If the track does not exist yet, make it first (see Where tracks come from) and open the page again; a waiting file is not tried again by itself. Waiting files are kept in your staging area's routes folder, /home/<you>/staging/routes/. Sorting in staging describes the staging area.

Trips

EiN cuts a track into trips by itself, and the track's Trips tab lists them, newest first. A trip runs from where the track lay still to where it lay still again:

  • Arriving at one of the track's home bases ends a trip, once it has been there half an hour or the track ends there. Passing by does not.
  • Staying anywhere else longer than three days ends a trip too (two days for a bike or on foot), as does a gap of that long with no points at all.
  • A trip that never gets 500 m from where it started is not a trip, and is left out.

How near a place has to be to count as the same place depends on how the track travels: within 1 km for a boat, 500 m for a car, 300 m for a bike and 200 m on foot.

Each trip's card shows its dates, an Ongoing badge while it has not ended and its last point is less than two days old, how many days it took, its length, how far from its start it got, and where it started and ended: the location of the stay, such as a harbour, an island or your home, or "a point on the way". The farthest place it stayed at is named too. Click a name to open its location. How stays get their locations and names is on Places a track stayed at. A stay of two hours or more across midnight is an overnight stay; it does not end the trip. A trip that is an album names it under Album; see Make a trip an album.

Click N legs to see the trip day by day: each day's date, its first and last time, its length, and where the day ended. A day's length runs from where the day before ended, so a drive across midnight counts, and the days add up to the trip's length. Days follow the track's Timezone, so a day ends at midnight there, and the times are on that clock; a track without one has its days and times in UTC.

The trips are kept up to date as the track grows: ten minutes after points arrive, EiN finds them again from where the last finished trip ended, puts the new stays at locations, and, unless weather lookups are turned off, gives each trip and day the weather along the track, which an album made of the trip shows (The Trip tab). Changing how the track travels, its time zone or its home bases finds them again from the start. Find trips again does it at once.

When the track has no trips, the tab says No trips: "Nothing in the track went 500 m or more from where it started.", or, before they were ever looked for, "Trips are found in the track once it has fixes, and kept up to date as it grows.", with Find trips for those who may change the track.

Join and part trips

EiN may end a trip where you do not: a weekend at home in the middle of a holiday, or a long wait for weather in a harbour. Those who may change the track can correct it:

  • Join to the trip before, on a trip's card, makes it and the trip before it one trip, whatever ended the first. The card then says Joined on and the day, with Part again to undo it.
  • Start a trip here, beside a day in a trip's legs, makes that day the first day of a trip of its own. Join to the trip before on the new trip undoes it.

The trips are found again at once. The changes are kept with the track, in its tripEdits setting, and hold when the trips are found again later.

Move a trip to another track

A trip recorded into the wrong track, such as a phone posting the sailboat's trip into the car's track, can be moved to the right one. Those who may change the track click Move to track… on the trip's card, pick the track under Track in Move the trip to another track ("Its positions, first to last, leave this track for the other, and the trips of both are found again."), and click Move. The list holds every other track. Positions you rejected or accepted by hand keep that.

The positions recorded where the trip began and ended stay, as does any GPX file the trip was read from, in this track's routes folder. To move another stretch, use track-move with the time span:

# in EiN's terminal
track-move ruby-iphone sailboat --from 2025-07-01T06:00:00Z --to 2025-07-03T18:00:00Z

A position at the very same moment as one the other track has already is dropped, and the other track keeps its own.

Make a trip an album

A trip EiN finds is only a proposal until you make it an album. Those who may change the track see, on each trip's card:

  • New album…, which opens Create Album to ask what the album is called. The name starts as the farthest place the trip stayed at when that place has a name; the trip's dates are the album's span, not part of its name. Create makes the album in /archive/albums and opens it.
  • Put in an album…, which opens Put the trip in an album. Before you type, it offers the albums whose dates overlap the trip and whose map draws this track (see Choose which tracks the map shows); their number keys pick them. Type part of a name to find any album you may change, or type a new name and press New album “…” to make an album of the trip under that name. Choosing an album makes it the trip's. An album that already holds another trip is refused with a message saying so.

Either way the track is added to the album's tracks, so its Map tab draws the route, and the album spans the trip even before it has any pictures. The card then names the album under Album, with Take off the album to undo it; the album and its pictures stay, and so does the track on its list. A trip is one album's, and an album holds one trip. Each time the trips are found again, or their stays put at locations, the album follows: it grows while the trip is ongoing, and its stays get their locations.

The album gets a Trip tab: the days, the distance in the track's units, where it stayed, and each day's notes. See The Trip tab. Those who may only look at the track see the albums the trip may belong to under Maybe in.

If something goes differently

What you see Why What to do
The Trips tab says No trips though the track has positions. Its trips have not been found yet, or no part of the track went 500 m or more from where it started. Click Find trips, or Find trips again.
A trip ends at home in the middle of a holiday, or two trips should be one. Arriving at a home base, or staying somewhere longer than three days, ends a trip. Click Join to the trip before on the second trip's card.
A trip's days end at the wrong hour. The track's Timezone is not set, so its days are cut in UTC, or it is set to another zone. Edit the track and choose the Timezone its days belong in.
A trip is listed under the wrong track. The phone or tracker that recorded it posted into another track, or its GPX file was uploaded to the wrong track. Click Move to track… on the trip's card; see Move a trip to another track.
Edit, Upload GPX or Add home base is missing. You may not change this track. Ask whoever made it, or an administrator, to share it with you.
Upload GPX gives a message ending in "no track points found". The file holds no recorded track points, only a planned route or waypoints. Use the file that was recorded while it moved.
Upload GPX gives a message ending in "none of its N track points has a time". The file's points carry no times: a route drawn on a map, or a file the program saved without them. Use the file that was recorded while it moved, or export it again with times.
A GPX file dropped in staging waits, though its track exists. The file names neither the track nor a camera that records into it, or it names more than one track. Click Choose track under Needs you on the Staging page; see GPX files in staging.
The phone reports nothing to the track. It cannot reach EiN over HTTPS from where it is, its link was replaced or turned off, or tracking is off in Traccar Client. See Live tracking from a phone and Ports, HTTPS and remote access.
A GPX file sits in an album as an ordinary file. It was uploaded straight into the album, where GPX files are not read as routes. Upload it on the track's page, then delete the file from the album.

From the terminal

Every track command takes a track by its path, or by a device or camera that has one, by its path or its name: sailboat is /archive/devices/sailboat/position/history. A track's routes folder holds the GPX files it was read from, its homes folder links its home bases, and its .trips keeps its trips. The commands below are in the archive command reference, and every one takes --help.

Task Command
List the tracks, with their name, kind, positions and span track-list, or track-list <folder> for those in one folder
Find tracks by name or description search-track <words>
Change how a track travels track-update <track> --kind boat (car, bike, foot), --timezone <zone> or --description <text>, or --clear <field>
List a track's home bases track-home <track>; with none of its own, the homes of its cameras' owners and the years they count
Add or remove a home base track-home <track> --add <location>… or --remove <location>…; a location is its path, or its folder name
Read a track as the maps draw it track-read <track> --still; without --still every recorded position, --max-speed <m/s> to change what is too fast
Add a GPX file to a track track-stage <gpx> --track <track>, which moves the file to the track's routes, or track-import <track> <gpx>, which keeps a copy there
Add a position by hand track-append <track> --lat=<lat> --lng=<lng> --ts <time>
See what GPX files hold, without importing gpx-info <gpx>…: name, tracks, creator, points and their time span, size, and the tracks each names
List the GPX files a track was read from source-list /archive/devices/sailboat/position/history
Find a track's trips track-trips <track>, --full from the start, --dry-run to see them without keeping them
List a track's trips trip-list <track>
Put a track's stays at locations, and name the new ones track-places <track>; see Places a track stayed at
Give a track's trips and days their weather track-weather <track>, --full to look every trip up again, --dry-run to see what it would look up
Join a trip to the one before, or part one trip-edit <track> <trip> --merge, or <track> <day> --split
Make a trip an album, or take it off trip-album <track> <trip> --new, --album=<album>, or --detach
Reject or accept positions by hand, or hand them back to the rules track-mark <track> --reject, --accept or --clear, with --from, --to, --near <lat,lng> and --marked reject
Move a stretch of a track to another track track-move <track> <to-track> --from <time> --to <time>
Link a camera to the track it records into camera-track <camera> <track>
Put a track on an album's map, or take it off album-track-add and album-track-rm <album> <track>…; see Albums
Turn live tracking from a phone on, or replace or turn off its link device-tracker <device>, with --new or --off, from the Automation module

Making Jon's GPS logger, saying he walks with it, and adding a route copied into your home folder:

# in EiN's terminal, as a member of automation
device-create "Jon's eTrex" --model "eTrex 30x" --position
track-update jon-s-etrex --kind foot --timezone Pacific/Auckland
track-stage "$HOME/tararua-walk.gpx" --track jon-s-etrex

device-create prints the track's path, /archive/devices/jon-s-etrex/position/history, and track-stage moves the GPX file to its routes folder once its points are in the track. See Commands by task for the rest of the archive's commands.
