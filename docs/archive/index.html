Archive

The Archive

The Archive is the module that makes EiN a family archive: albums of photos, videos, recordings and documents, the people in them and the family tree, the places they were taken, and the devices that took them. This page is for anyone deciding whether to install it and for anyone starting to use it: what it needs, how to install it, what it adds to EiN, and where to begin. The pages in this section apply once the Archive is installed.

What it is for

Part What it gives you Covered on
Albums and media Albums that hold photos, videos, audio recordings and documents, the Media feed and the Year page, labels, and the viewer Albums, Media, Year and labels, Viewing photos, videos and recordings
Staging A private place for each person, where uploads wait, get their dates and places read, and are grouped into suggested albums to file Getting your photos in, Sorting in staging
People and the family People, the faces found in pictures, the family tree, events in their lives and the homes they lived in People and faces, Family tree, events and homes
Locations and the map Named places with outlines on the map, and pictures placed by their GPS position Locations and the map
Devices and GPS tracks The cameras, phones and trackers that took pictures or recorded routes, their owners and clocks Devices and GPS tracks
Search Finding pictures by what they show, say and contain, and the albums, people, locations and devices they belong to Searching your pictures
Home On this day, News and Events on the Home page, and the life-event charts on the Statistics page Memories, news and events on Home

What it needs

  • The ems module. The Archive reaches the media service through it. EiN installs ems along with the Archive.
  • The media service, a separate container beside EiN that reads dates and places, makes the previews, and finds faces, text and speech. Installing the module does not install it: the installer's --ems option does. Without it, the Archive installs and uploads arrive, but nothing gets thumbnails, dates, faces or searchable text, and uploads wait in staging as being prepared until the service is there. The media service covers it. Some of its models are for non-commercial use only; see Models and their licences.

A language model is optional. With the assistant module's model, the search box also reads people and places out of a sentence; see Searching your pictures.

Install it

Installing takes an administrator. Any of these installs the Archive with the ems module:

  • At the install, with the installer's --module archive, together with --ems for the media service:

    # on the machine that will run EiN
curl -fsSL https://einhq.eu/install.sh | bash -s -- --ems --module archive

    On an EiN that is already installed, ~/ein/setup.sh --ems --module archive does the same. Without --ems, the installer's archive line says low and reminds you that the Archive needs the media service.

  • In the web interface: open Settings → Modules, choose the Published modules button beside the filter, and choose Install beside archive. When it brings ems along, EiN says "Installed, with ems".

  • In the terminal, as the administrator:

    # in EiN's terminal
mod install archive

Its pages appear in the sidebar without a reload. Modules covers installing, updating and removing modules, and Installing in detail the installer's options.

What it adds to the interface

In the sidebar:

Section Entries
(top) Media, and Staging when you are signed in
Explore Year and Map, beside EiN's own Search
Browse Albums, People, Locations and Devices

In the top bar:

  • The Create menu gets Import media (when you are signed in), Album, Person, Location and Device.
  • The account menu gets Favourites.
  • Random album, the button with two crossing arrows beside the search box, opens an album at random; Alt+R does the same.

In EiN's own pages:

  • Home: the Archive fills On this day, News and Events, and offers Import media under an empty News when you are signed in; see Memories, news and events on Home.
  • Statistics: the Events section, with births, engagements, marriages and deaths by month.
  • Search: the People, Locations, Years and Labels rows before you search, and, when you are signed in, the pictures that look like a label in a search for that one label; see Searching your pictures.
  • Nodes: a Duplicates tab among the page's tools, for the folder that is open.
  • The finder in the top bar finds albums, people, locations and devices by name, and offers Pictures of "…" to search the Media page.

Finding your way around shows where each of these sits.

Where its content lives

The Archive keeps its content in the tree: in folders under /archive that it makes when it is set up, and in a staging folder in each person's home:

Path What is there
/archive/albums Albums, and the photos, videos, recordings and documents in them
/archive/people People, with their family, homes, events and devices
/archive/locations Locations, each with its outline on the map
/archive/devices Cameras, phones and GPS trackers, each with the route it recorded
~/staging In each person's home, such as /home/helen/staging: uploads waiting to be sorted, seen by nobody else

Removing the module never removes the folders under /archive, since they hold the content. The tree describes them, and the labels in /archive/tags, which are EiN's own.

Start using it

  1. Upload. In the top bar, open Create (the plus) and choose Import media. Choose Inbox under Into which category?, click Choose files or Choose a folder, and click Upload. Keep the tab open until the Uploads button in the top bar says it is done. For more than about 10,000 files, copy them over SFTP instead. See Getting your photos in.
  2. Sort in staging. A few minutes after the files are prepared, open Staging in the sidebar, then the category, then Sort. Each group shows its proposal, such as New album “…”, which Enter accepts on a computer; or choose New album… in the group's ⋯ menu, check the Name and choose Create album. The pictures leave staging for the album, which is now under Albums. Every decision can be undone. See Sorting in staging.
  3. Share an album. Open the album and choose Share in its header. Under Who can see it, Default lets everyone with an account see it, and Private only its owner; for someone without an account, choose Create link under Share with a link. See Sharing.

Then name the people in your pictures (People and faces), and find what you have (Searching your pictures). An administrator setting EiN up for a family finds the whole order of steps on First steps.
